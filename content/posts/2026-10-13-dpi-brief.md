# DPI Brief — October 13, 2026

**Coverage window:** 12 October 2026, 08:00–13 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

The clearest signals today are practical access, payment scale and security. UIDAI announced a new Aadhaar Seva Kendra in Paderu; NPCI published its 11 October transaction snapshot; and CERT-In circulated a critical warning about abuse of payment and disbursement APIs. MeitY promoted a DPDP explainer, while DARPG highlighted a postal-delivery grievance outcome. These are not all policy changes: the distinction matters when a social post reports activity but does not establish a new rule or system-wide result.

## 1. UIDAI announces a new Aadhaar Seva Kendra in Paderu

UIDAI’s Hyderabad regional office [announced the opening of an Aadhaar Seva Kendra at Paderu, Andhra Pradesh](https://x.com/UIDAIHyderabad/status/2109571534251757629), inaugurated by the district Joint Collector with UIDAI officials and service partners. A nearby enrolment or service point can reduce the cost of access for residents who would otherwise travel farther. The post does not specify opening hours, appointment requirements, accessibility, capacity, or which enrolment and update services are available there. It is a service-access update—not a change to Aadhaar rules or proof that every service can be completed at the new centre. Residents should verify the facility’s current details before making a trip.

## 2. NPCI’s 11 October snapshot records 84.6 crore UPI transactions

NPCI’s [daily payment-system cards for 11 October](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2109663996647461093) show 846.18 million UPI transactions worth ₹87,579.05 crore. The separate [BharatConnect, NACH and NFS card](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2109663837389738296) reports 7.93 million BharatConnect transactions, 5.18 million NACH transactions and 8.78 million NFS transactions. UPI’s month-to-date total through 11 October is 9,293.27 million transactions worth ₹12,10,174.01 crore; the graphic explicitly excludes Credit Card on UPI and Credit Line on UPI.

These are useful scale measures, not a report card. Volume and value do not show how many attempts failed, how long reversals took, the distribution of fraud losses, or whether service quality is equitable across banks and regions. NPCI’s headline is a one-day count; its month-to-date line is cumulative and should not be compared as if it were another daily observation.

## 3. CERT-In flags critical abuse of payout APIs

CERT-In’s [critical advisory on payment and disbursement API abuse](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES02&VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0046) describes campaigns against NBFCs, digital lenders, fintech companies, payment service providers and wallet operators. The agency says attackers have compromised exposed applications or credentials, then invoked payout APIs from whitelisted infrastructure while bypassing checks such as KYC, beneficiary validation and transaction limits. It reports fraudulent transfers routed through IMPS or NEFT to mule accounts. The advisory’s original issue date is **9 October**; CERT-In’s official account publicised it on **12 October**, so this is a newly surfaced warning in the coverage window, not an incident first dated yesterday.

This is not a disclosed UPI protocol flaw, nor evidence that a named provider was breached. The control lesson is more direct: financial firms should remove unused APIs and long-lived static keys, require strong authentication, validate every payout against an approved record, add independent approval and velocity limits, and reconcile transfers promptly. CERT-In rates the advisory critical and asks affected entities to report suspicious activity.

## 4. MeitY promotes a DPDP rights explainer

MeitY [shared Episode 1 of “DPDP Decoded”](https://x.com/GoI_MeitY/status/2109622611798007822), framing it around understanding personal data and knowing one’s rights; the [video is available here](https://youtu.be/c8CxtU7bKOs). This is public-awareness material, not a new notification, rule amendment or enforcement decision. The announcement’s short description does not explain how an individual can exercise a particular right, challenge a data-processing decision, or seek redress. The useful follow-up will be plain, actionable guidance tied to the final legal framework—not merely a slogan that rights exist.

## 5. DARPG spotlights a CPGRAMS postal-delivery case

DARPG’s [CPGRAMS post says a citizen’s India Post consignment was delivered after grievance intervention](https://x.com/DARPG_GoI/status/2109609847415341138). It is a concrete reported outcome and a reminder that the public-grievance channel can escalate service failures. But the post supplies no resolution time, case details or denominator; one success story cannot establish how quickly or consistently similar complaints are resolved. It should be read as an example, not a performance statistic.
