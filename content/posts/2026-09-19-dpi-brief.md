# DPI Brief — September 19, 2026

## 1. Fuel retailers become the first organised defectors from paid UPI (Payments)

The merchant-side reaction to the new UPI merchant discount rate has found its first organised, sector-specific refusal. Petrol pump dealers across Madhya Pradesh — about 4,700 fuel stations — have decided to stop accepting UPI payments above ₹2,000 for petrol and diesel purchases from October 16 unless the MDR is withdrawn, the Confederation of All India Traders announced Friday. [^1] Madhya Pradesh Petrol Pumps Dealers Association president Ajay Singh put the math on record: at the reported 0.4% rate on eligible transactions (₹40 on a ₹10,000 fill), a pump would "bear a loss of Rs 17,700 monthly" on margins that are already thin — and the fuel sector is also reported to attract a flat ₹5 MDR per transaction above ₹2,000 under NPCI's circular. [^2] CAIT has asked the Union government to keep fuel out of the fee entirely, warning that charging merchants who were promised a cashless transition undermines adoption itself. The threat is conditional and stays contingent on a rollback, but it converts the MDR debate from commentary into an operational date — three days after the fee takes effect October 15. In Delhi, the government and payment gateways have begun discussing ways to shield consumers from any MDR pass-through, a sign the implementation mechanics are still being negotiated.

## 2. AgriStack gets a political deadline and a scorecard — and both reveal gaps (Sectoral)

The southern states' agriculture ministers' conference in Hyderabad on Friday delivered the sharpest central push on AgriStack implementation in weeks. Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan told states they must deliver on commitments made at the conference, including deadlines for Farmer ID generation and other AgriStack components, warned against "eating, meeting and sitting" instead of implementation, and said states should review and amend outdated laws and procedures. [^3] [^4] The data presented at the same conference showed why the pressure exists: under the Digital Crop Survey for 2025-26, Telangana has 100% district coverage but only 78% of its villages surveyed, Kerala sits at 36%, and Puducherry and Lakshadweep at zero, while Tamil Nadu (99%) and Karnataka (97%) lead. [^5] Village map georeferencing — the other AgriStack pillar — tells a similar story: Tamil Nadu has completed all 17,164 maps, but Telangana has done 9,786 of 10,887 (90%), Kerala 54%. The farmer ID targets and the survey numbers now form a public scorecard; states that miss them have been named, in effect, by their own data.

## 3. Maharashtra bets ₹4,250 crore on an ABDM-aligned public health stack (Sectoral)

The Maharashtra cabinet has cleared "Pragati," a five-year (2026-31), ₹4,250-crore health sector reform programme funded by a ₹2,975-crore Asian Development Bank loan and ₹1,275 crore from the state, with a digital spine aligned to the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission. [^6] The project commits to online patient registration, OPD/IPD management, digital laboratory reports, e-prescriptions, referral and counter-referral workflows, and electronic and personal health records — the ABDM building blocks — while strengthening the Maharashtra Health Technology Mission and scaling early screening for non-communicable diseases across primary health centres and Ayushman Arogya Mandirs. Physical expansion rides along: a 200-bed tertiary care hospital in Pune, 500-bed facilities at Kolhapur and Nanded, and expansions at Nagpur and Latur. For the sectoral layer, this is one of the larger state-level ABDM integration commitments to date, and its execution — whether e-prescriptions and EHRs reach the PHC level — is the thing to track.

## 4. TRAI hard-codes AI spam detection — and clips the call-blockers (Trust)

The telecom regulator on Friday tightened its anti-spam rulebook, amending the Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018 to bring AI-based detection of suspected spam numbers into the regulatory framework, extend oversight to automated and robocalls, and introduce deterrents including a termination charge on bulk commercial Application-to-Person calls. [^7] The amendments incorporate TRAI's February 2026 direction on AI/ML-based spam intelligence: telcos must flag numbers with a "high probability" of carrying unsolicited commercial communication and share flags across operators; three or more unique complaints in ten days combined with an AI flag triggers early action, and five or more flagged numbers in ten days can bring KYC re-verification, physical verification, or disconnection. The rules also discipline the blockers: call management apps must share spam data with telcos to offer in-app reporting, and blanket blocking or tagging of the 140/1600/1601 series — the flashpoint in TRAI's run-in with Truecaller — is prohibited. A seven-day cap on follow-up commercial calls after a verifiable customer inquiry, and a new consumer appeal route for spam complaint resolutions, round out what is the most consequential rewrite of India's UCC regime since 2018.

## 5. Quick commerce joins the artisan market-linkage stack (Commerce)

The MSME ministry has partnered with Blinkit to sell PM Vishwakarma artisans' products on the quick-commerce platform, piloted first in Bengaluru — extending a market-linkage effort that has already run through ONDC, FabIndia and Meesho, with beneficiaries onboarded on the Government e-Marketplace. [^8] The commerce layer's interesting question is curation: whether 10-minute delivery logistics can carry handcrafted goods without flattening them into commodity listings, and whether quick-commerce discovery gives artisans measurable demand signal rather than one-off festive placement.

## 6. The child Aadhaar free-update window shuts September 30 (Identity)

A consumer deadline worth flagging: UIDAI's one-year waiver of fees for mandatory biometric updates for children — covering an estimated 6 crore children aged 5-17 — expires September 30, 2026, after which the ₹125 charge returns. [^9] The waiver window drove a mission-mode school campaign that had completed updates for 1.2 crore students across more than 1.03 lakh schools by March, integrated with the UDISE+ database. [^10] Parents whose children missed the school camps need an Aadhaar Seva Kendra visit this fortnight to keep school admissions, exam registrations and DBT-linked services uninterrupted.

**The throughline:** The stack's second decade is being stress-tested by its own terms of service. Merchants who were recruited to digitise are now organising refusals over fees; states are being graded publicly on farmer IDs and crop surveys they haven't finished; and the trust layer is simultaneously empowering AI spam detection and restraining the private apps that consumers actually use to filter calls.
