# DPI Brief — September 16, 2026

The MDR question that has hung over UPI since January 2020 finally has an answer. Yesterday brought a gazette notification fixing the fee-free threshold, NPCI's detailed merchant-charges framework, and a devolution formula splitting revenue between banks, acquirers and apps — the most consequential day for India's payments economics in six years. Alongside it: Aadhaar's founding architect waded into the voter-roll revision debate, India's pesticide supply chain went digital-only by law, ONDC's logistics network gained an EV fleet, and the UK and India signed an AI anti-fraud telecom pact.

## 1. UPI's zero-fee era ends for large merchant payments — from October 15 [L2 — Payments]

NPCI on Tuesday [issued the revised UPI Merchant Discount Rate framework](https://www.thehindu.com/business/npci-introduces-04-percent-mdr-charge-on-upi-payments-above-rs-2000-exempts-small-merchants-person-to-person-transfers/article71468913.ece), effective **October 15, 2026**: a 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300; a flat ₹5 for railways, telecom, insurance and fuel; and complete exemption for all P2P transfers, everything up to ₹2,000, and small-merchant (P2PM) transactions. [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-payments-authority-sets-04-fee-upi-merchant-payments-above-2000-rupees-2026-09-15) merchants cannot pass the charge on to customers, and revenue is earmarked for resilience, cybersecurity and innovation spending. [Moneycontrol has the devolution formula](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/issuer-banks-to-get-40-merchant-acquirers-30-upi-apps-to-get-20-of-the-upi-mdr-revenue-article-14030521.html/amp): issuer banks get 40%, acquiring banks 30%, UPI apps 20%, with 5% of collections funding a new small-merchant acceptance-expansion fund. The legal footing landed a day earlier: a [gazette notification dated September 14](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-mdr-rs-2000-threshold) under the newly amended Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act bars any direct or indirect charge on UPI and RuPay debit payments up to ₹2,000. On a rail that moved 24.51 billion transactions worth ₹29.82 lakh crore in August, [the Finance Ministry says only about 4% of merchant transactions will be affected](https://www.rediff.com/news/report/govt-imposes-04-fee-on-upi-payments-above-rs-2000/20260915.htm). The consumer promise — free below ₹2,000, always-free P2P — holds for now. The watch item: the framework gets finalised in consultation with RBI within three months, and the ₹300 cap plus sector carve-outs are exactly where merchant-side lobbying will concentrate.

## 2. Aadhaar's architect pitches identity infrastructure as the fix for the Election Commission's costliest exercise [L1 — Identity]

R.S. Sharma, UIDAI's founding director general, [called the Election Commission's special intensive revision (SIR) of voter rolls "ruinously expensive"](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/ruinously-expensive-uidai-founding-dg-says-ecs-voter-roll-exercise-needs-an-aadhaar-based-alternative-555536-2026-09-15) and argued India should replace it with Aadhaar-based verification — [telling The Wire that SIR is "a 19th-century process"](https://m.thewire.in/article/rights/sir-is-a-19th-century-process-says-aadhaar-architect-r-s-sharma/amp) running paper forms through door-to-door enumeration when a 144-crore-strong digital identity layer already exists. It is a pointed intervention from within the system's own founding team: the man who built Aadhaar now argues electoral roll maintenance should ride on it. The counterweights — Aadhaar proves identity, not citizenship, and binding it to the franchise raises exclusion and surveillance risks — are precisely the debates DPI Watch has tracked as SIR exercises rolled across states. Either way, the episode marks identity infrastructure graduating from welfare delivery into the electoral core.

## 3. India's pesticide supply chain goes digital-only today [L5 — Sectoral]

The [Insecticides (Amendment) Rules, 2026](https://www.global-agriculture.com/crop-protection/indias-digital-first-insecticide-licensing-rules-take-effect-september-15) (G.S.R. 493(E), notified June 17) take effect **September 15**, forcing India's roughly 300,000 licensed pesticide manufacturers, importers, distributors and dealers onto fully digital licensing, electronic stock registers and digital fee payment — paper applications are no longer accepted, and records must be updated within 24 hours of an inspection and filed to the licensing officer. This is the quiet AgriStack-adjacent digitisation that rarely makes headlines: an entire regulatory domain moving from paper to portal in a single day, ninety days after notification. The compliance crunch risk for small rural dealers is real, but the upside — a traceable, queryable record of every insecticide moving through the supply chain — is a consumer-safety win in a sector where spurious pesticides remain a persistent hazard.

## 4. Bounce plugs its EV fleet into ONDC's Open Delivery Protocol [L4 — Commerce]

Electric mobility company Bounce [joined ONDC's Open Delivery Protocol](https://www.passionateinmarketing.com/bounce-joins-ondcs-open-delivery-protocol-to-accelerate-last-mile-logistics) on September 14, bringing 12,000+ riders across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, NCR and Mumbai into a logistics network that now counts 50+ logistics service providers, 150,000+ delivery partners and 12,000+ daily deliveries over interoperable infrastructure. ODP lets any merchant reach any logistics provider through a single integration — the same unbundling logic ONDC applied to retail, now applied to delivery. With tens of thousands of merchants already on ONDC logistics, the open question is whether open delivery can match the unit economics of platform-owned fleets; EV-led supply is one credible path to getting there.

## 5. UK and India sign an AI anti-fraud telecom pact [L7 — Trust]

The UK government and India's Cellular Operators Association of India [signed an MoU on September 14 to cooperate on AI-enabled approaches to online fraud, scams and harms](https://www.unite.ai/uk-and-india-sign-telecoms-mou-to-harness-ai-against-digital-fraud), announced September 15, with TRAI and DoT representatives present at the signing. Digital fraud is the tax on trust that every DPI layer pays: UPI fraud, smishing and digital-arrest scams are the dark side of 24-billion-transactions-a-month adoption. Cross-border cooperation on AI fraud detection at the operator level is welcome; the consumer test is whether it measurably cuts the mule-account and phishing pipelines draining Indians today.
