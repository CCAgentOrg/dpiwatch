# DPI Brief — September 28, 2026

**The UPI merchant fee reaches the Supreme Court today (L2 — Payments).** A bench led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana, [hears a PIL challenging the Centre's 0.4% MDR on UPI person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000](https://www.thehindu.com/business/mdr-on-upi-sc-to-hear-plea-challenging-imposition-on-september-28/article71515498.ece), seventeen days before the levy takes effect on October 15. The petition by advocate Anjan Datta [attacks the September 14 notification and the September 15 framework on both process and design](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/amp/news/digital-payments/supreme-court-to-hear-challenge-against-upi-mdr-on-transactions-above-2000/134517041): no public consultation, no statutory safeguards, and an unexplained asymmetry — RuPay debit cards keep their "no charge" protection without any monetary ceiling while UPI above ₹2,000 gets levied. It asks the Court to require any future MDR classification to rest on evidence: merchant turnover, MSME status, actual margins, geography and ability to pay — not cliff-edge thresholds. The courtroom challenge lands amid widening street resistance: the All India Mobile Retailers Association has [called a "No UPI Day" for October 2](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/upi-must-remain-zero-mdr-mobile-retailers-announce-october-2-protest/articleshow/134517944.cms), petrol stations across Punjab plan to refuse UPI above ₹2,000 from October 16, and [the Centre has ruled out a rollback even as it meets trader bodies](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Sep/27/sc-to-hear-on-sept-28-plea-challenging-imposition-of-mdr-on-upi-payments-above-rs-2000). What no official has yet answered is the consumer question: with merchants free to pass the fee on, who actually ends up paying for "free" payments?

**The rails get stress-tested while the fee is argued (L2 — Payments).** As this brief publishes, bank unions have [begun a three-day nationwide strike (September 28–30) pressing for a five-day week](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Industry/unions-to-go-on-3-day-nationwide-bank-strike-from-september-28-services-to-be-impacted/article71516034.ece), hitting public-sector banks and regional rural banks; several banks kept counters open through Sunday so customers could clear work before September 30 half-yearly closing. The telling part for DPI watchers: [UPI, ATMs and mobile banking are expected to keep running through the strike](https://www.moneycontrol.com/banking/bank-strike-september-28-30-what-services-will-be-affected-and-what-will-continue-article-14039255.html) — branch banking is now the fragile layer, the digital rails the resilient one. That resilience has numbers behind it: an IMF assessment [names UPI the world's largest retail fast-payment system by transaction volume](https://telanganatoday.com/upi-emerges-as-worlds-largest-retail-fast-payment-system-by-volume), and NPCI data put August at about 24.5 billion transactions worth ₹29.82 lakh crore — roughly 790 million payments a day — up from 17.8 million transactions in all of FY2016-17.

**Subsidised LPG goes biometric from Thursday (L1 — Identity).** From October 1, domestic LPG consumers must complete [Biometric Aadhaar Authentication to keep receiving subsidy on refills](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/others/aadhaar-biometric-authentication-required-for-subsidised-lpg-refills-from-october-1); the unverified can still buy cylinders, but at full market price. The Petroleum Ministry reports [27.43 crore consumers — 89.9% of the active base — already authenticated](https://www.babushahi.com/full-news.php?id=227075&headline=Biometric-Aadhaar-Authentication-Mandatory-for-Subsidised-Domestic-LPG-Refills-from-1-October-2026), leaving roughly 3 crore to convert within days. [The Hindu's explainer](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/lpg-ekyc-deadline-how-to-complete-and-what-happens-if-you-dont-finish-it-by-october-1/article71499493.ece) walks through the options — fingerprint, iris or face — and what breaks without them. The worry is the record: as [The Wire chronicles](https://m.thewire.in/article/rights/govt-repeatedly-said-aadhaar-wasnt-compulsory-for-lpg-now-it-is-pushing-it-aggressively), successive governments insisted Aadhaar was not compulsory for LPG, and the requirement keeps returning by another door. De-duplication is a legitimate goal; the test is whether the ~10% who miss the deadline face friction rather than forfeiture of a cooking-fuel subsidy. Same identity window: free biometric updates for children's Baal Aadhaar [close on September 30](https://www.timesbull.com/aadhaar-card-update-how-to-free-aadhaar-update-for-children-ends-september-30), after which the fee applies.

**AgriStack reaches scale in Gujarat as the Rabi dialogue opens (L5 — Sectoral).** Gujarat's Farmer Registry has crossed [63.35 lakh farmers, each record linking Aadhaar-based identity to verified land holdings](https://www.gktoday.in/gujarat-adds-63-35-lakh-farmers-to-digital-registry-linked-with-aadhaar-and-land-records) — exactly the verified farmer-state dataset that AgriStack's credit, insurance and procurement use-cases presuppose. Today and tomorrow, the [National Conference on Agriculture for Rabi Campaign 2026 meets in Delhi](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/cities/new-delhi/rabi-season-govt-to-focus-on-strengthening-centrestate-coordination-1126131) with digital agriculture on the agenda; worth watching is whether other states publish registry coverage at Gujarat's granularity. The consumer stake: a registry that verifies entitlements is a benefit; one that silently gates them is a single point of failure.

**TRAI rewires prepaid recharge rules toward the buyer (L7 — Trust).** The [Telecom Consumers Protection (13th Amendment) Regulations, 2026 — notified September 22 — require operators to offer voice-and-SMS-only special tariff vouchers](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/new-mobile-recharge-rules-30-day-plans-call-only-options-and-more-trai-rules-explained/articleshow/134519014.cms) for every bundled validity of 30 days or less, at appropriately reduced tariffs, alongside a same-date monthly renewal voucher and true 30-day plans — twelve recharges a year instead of thirteen on the 28-day cycle. The rules followed a consultation that drew 1,132 responses, and the push gained force after parliamentarians flagged how the 28-day cycle quietly extracts an extra recharge each year. Plan design sounds minor; for prepaid-majority India it is the price of the network itself, and the trust layer just tilted it back toward the consumer.

**CPGRAMS publishes its August scorecard (L6 — Governance).** DARPG's [52nd monthly CPGRAMS report](https://observervoice.com/government-report-reveals-high-grievance-redressal-rates-for-august-2026-230276) counts 2,51,147 grievances received in August, average disposal time down to 13 days in 2026, and 1,38,801 new registrations — West Bengal (42,054), Uttar Pradesh (17,202) and Maharashtra (10,616) lead — with Common Service Centres now feeding the portal. The fine print deserves skepticism: a backlog of 93,864 cases persists, and "disposal" is not the same as "resolution" — 44% of grievances target the heavy-lift ministries (Rural Development, Labour and Employment, Financial Services) where closure quality is hardest to verify. Speed is improving; whether complaints actually get fixed remains a matter of trust.
