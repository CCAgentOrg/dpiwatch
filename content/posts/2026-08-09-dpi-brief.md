# DPI Brief — August 09, 2026

India's Digital Public Infrastructure ecosystem saw significant activity across multiple layers this week — from a landmark Supreme Court hearing on the tension between data protection and transparency laws, to GeM crossing a ₹20 lakh crore procurement milestone on its tenth anniversary.

## GeM Completes a Decade, Crosses ₹20 Lakh Crore in Cumulative Procurement

**Layer: L4 — Commerce (GeM)**

The Government e-Marketplace (GeM) completes ten years of operations on August 9, marking a significant milestone in India's digital governance journey. As of August 7, the platform has facilitated cumulative procurement worth over ₹20 lakh crore through more than 3.78 crore orders, according to the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

GeM's annual GMV has grown from ₹422 crore in FY 2016–17 to over ₹5 lakh crore in each of FY 2024–25 and FY 2025–26. While it took over eight years to achieve the first ₹10 lakh crore in cumulative GMV, the next ₹10 lakh crore came in under two years — reflecting accelerating adoption and deeper integration into public procurement workflows. The platform now serves as the default procurement channel for central and state government buyers, PSU subsidiaries, and local bodies.

## Supreme Court to Examine Whether DPDP Act Can Cripple RTI

**Layer: L7 — Trust (DPDP Act)**

The Supreme Court on Friday agreed to examine whether the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023, can be used to restrict or undermine the Right to Information Act. The court will hear arguments on whether classifying all government data as "personal data" under the DPDP framework effectively creates a blanket exemption from RTI disclosures.

This is a constitutionally significant question. The RTI Act, enacted in 2005, covers all forms of data regardless of format. The DPDP Act, by contrast, governs only digital personal data. The court's examination could determine whether the newer privacy framework inadvertently overrides India's flagship transparency law — a tension that transparency advocates and journalists have flagged since the DPDP rules were finalised in late 2025.

## AAERI Verify Launches: DigiLocker-Powered Document Verification for Australian Universities

**Layer: L3 — Documents (DigiLocker, API Setu)**

The Association of Australian Education Representatives in India (AAERI) has launched **AAERI Verify**, a document verification tool powered by DigiLocker that enables Australian universities to securely authenticate Indian students' academic, identity, and financial documents. The platform connects directly to DigiLocker APIs, providing tamper-proof, time-stamped verification reports sourced from government databases.

The tool addresses a longstanding concern around document fraud in international student admissions. AAERI has partnered with DigiVerifier, an authorised DigiLocker verifier, to build and operate the platform. Verification requires student consent, and each report includes direct source links — creating an auditable chain from issuer to verifier.

This is a concrete example of India's document DPI layer enabling cross-border trust: Indian digital credentials (degree certificates, mark sheets, Aadhaar, income documents) can now be verified by foreign institutions without physical attestation or third-party notarisation.

## Gujarat's Divyang Bus Pass Integration with DigiLocker Earns National Recognition

**Layer: L3 — Documents (DigiLocker) | L6 — Governance**

Gujarat's integration of the Divyang (persons with disabilities) Bus Pass with DigiLocker has received national recognition for citizen-centric digital governance. Over 4.42 lakh passes have been issued digitally, enabling beneficiaries to access free travel on Gujarat State Road Transport Corporation (GSRTC) buses without carrying a physical pass.

The integration allows pass holders to store and present their digitally signed bus passes through the DigiLocker app, with offline verification capability. The recognition highlights how state-level implementations of central DPI infrastructure can deliver tangible accessibility improvements — particularly for vulnerable populations who benefit most from reducing physical document dependencies.

## UPI Records 23.66 Billion Transactions in July; Government Reaffirms Consumer Access Remains Free

**Layer: L2 — Payments (UPI, NPCI)**

UPI processed 23.66 billion transactions worth ₹29.88 lakh crore in July 2026 — the highest monthly volume recorded, surpassing the previous record of 23.2 billion set in May. The platform now averages 763 million transactions daily, with transaction value growing 19% year-on-year.

Amid ongoing debate around merchant discount rates (MDR), the Payments Council of India clarified that UPI transactions will remain free for consumers and small merchants (including kirana stores). The government has proposed changes to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act via the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026, which creates a legal pathway for threshold-based MDR on select high-value transactions to large merchants — but no MDR has been imposed yet, and person-to-person transactions will not be affected.

NPCI is also developing EMI-on-UPI functionality, which would allow consumers to convert purchases into monthly installments directly through UPI — potentially expanding credit access to a broader user base.

## Farmer ID Saturation Review: Centre Pushes States for Dynamic Registry Updates

**Layer: L5 — Sectoral (AgriStack, Farmer Registry)**

Secretary, Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DA&FW), chaired a meeting on August 7 with all States and UTs to review the progress of Farmer ID saturation under the AgriStack initiative. The Secretary urged all states to ensure dynamic updation of the Farmer Registry, covering landholding data, crop patterns, and demographic information to enable data-driven agricultural service delivery.

The meeting was part of the broader review of the Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses, where Farmer ID data is expected to play a role in targeted procurement and subsidy distribution. Dynamic updation — rather than one-time enrollment — is being emphasised as critical for the registry's utility in credit disbursement, insurance underwriting, and input subsidy management.
