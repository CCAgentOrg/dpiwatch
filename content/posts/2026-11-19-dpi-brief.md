# DPI Brief — November 19, 2026

**Coverage period:** November 18–19, 2026 · **Layers touched:** L1, L2, L3, L4, L5 (plus L6/L7 in quick takes)

## Executive summary

The UPI merchant fee found its shape at the edges: NPCI's steering committee has reportedly settled on a flat ₹5 charge for merchant-to-merchant payments — a fraction of the ₹300 ceiling traders feared — even as the overall rollout date remains unset. The transparency story of the day sits in public procurement: the Central Information Commission ordered GeM to disclose internal findings on cartelisation and market concentration, rejecting a boilerplate confidentiality claim. ONDC laid out its credit roadmap of BNPL, business loans and loans against mutual funds, while exam season pulled Aadhaar biometrics and DigiLocker to the centre of national testing logistics. Chhattisgarh showed what AgriStack looks like when it reaches the mandi floor. Five items below; quick takes from the remaining layers at the end.

## Key developments

### 1. NPCI panel settles on a flat ₹5 fee for B2B UPI payments under the MDR framework (L2 — Payments)

Merchant-to-merchant (M2M) transactions on UPI are set to attract a flat fee of ₹5 under the new merchant discount rate (MDR) framework, against the up-to-₹300 charge traders had feared, according to two people aware of the decision taken by NPCI's UPI and Services Steering Committee.[^1] The flat rate covers wholesaler-to-retailer payments, B2B marketplaces and B2B collections; with average M2M ticket sizes of ₹20,000–40,000, the standard 0.4% rate would have meant ₹80–160 per transaction, and ₹300 at ₹75,000 and above.[^1] Two caveats temper the relief. First, the framework's implementation date is still not finalised — it was deferred from October 15 "until after Diwali" and no revised date has been announced.[^1] Second, the settlement is a negotiated carve-out, not a resolved policy: the All India Consumer Products Distributors Federation has written to the finance minister seeking full M2M exemption, noting that about 65% of consumer payments received by retailers and 40–45% of retailer-to-distributor credit-bill payments already run on UPI, and asking that GST treatment of MDR be examined — a question that rests with the GST Council, not NPCI or RBI.[^1] The fee architecture is being built segment by segment, in public, under pressure.

### 2. CIC orders GeM to open its cartelisation files (L4 — Commerce)

The Central Information Commission has directed the Government e-Marketplace to disclose the general findings of its internal assessments on cartelisation, market concentration and entry barriers, after GeM acknowledged it analyses repeated allocation of orders to the same sellers but refused to release the related reports as "commercially confidential and market-sensitive" under Section 8(1)(d) of the RTI Act.[^2] Information Commissioner Khushwant Singh Sethi observed that the exemption was claimed in a "general and mechanical manner" — the mere description of information as commercially confidential is not sufficient to deny access — and ordered a specific, point-wise reply.[^2] The order also surfaced useful numbers: 3,36,904 unique sellers have received orders on GeM to date, and platform procurement touched ₹5.42 lakh crore in 2024–25.[^2] GeM denied the existence of a purported "link facility" that allegedly routed bid links to selected sellers, and the Commission separately sought a revised, categorical reply on withholding year-wise procurement figures for the Army, BSF, SSB and CRPF, after GeM shifted between exemption clauses.[^2] A public-procurement platform claiming market sensitivity to withhold governance information about its own market is exactly the kind of claim RTI adjudication exists to test.

### 3. ONDC's credit network lines up BNPL, business loans and loans against mutual funds (L4 — Commerce)

ONDC's financial-services network currently has 11–12 active lenders with another 10–12 in the pipeline, and is preparing to add business loans and purchase finance (BNPL) to a stack that already includes credit cards — live with Bajaj Markets and HDFC Bank — and a loan-against-mutual-funds offering, according to ONDC chief business officer Hrushikesh Mehta in ET BFSI's Fintech Diary.[^3] The strategy is distribution-first: bring lenders and consumer platforms onto a common network so credit is discovered and disbursed where commerce already happens, with MSME borrowers the headline segment.[^3] With commerce and finance converging on the same open rails, the watch-items are underwriting standards across a growing lender roster, consent flows for financial data, and how a network built for onions and sarees carries credit risk without becoming an unregulated lending bazaar.

### 4. Exam season turns Aadhaar biometrics and DigiLocker into test-day infrastructure (L1 — Identity, L3 — Documents)

The National Testing Agency's advisory for JEE Main 2027 (Session 1 registration closes November 22; over 6.7 lakh applications in, 4.9 lakh fee-paid) makes MeriPehchaan login mandatory through DigiLocker, e-Pramaan or JanParichay, requires DigiLocker re-KYC to fetch the candidate's latest photograph, and urges candidates to use only their own credentials — while separately advising anyone whose Aadhaar was enrolled before age 15 to complete the mandatory biometric update (due once at 5 and again at 15) before exam day; NRI/OCI applicants may use a passport instead.[^4][^5][^6] The Staff Selection Commission issued a parallel advisory for CGL, CHSL, MTS and GD Constable recruitment exams, asking candidates to update demographics and biometrics and keep biometrics permanently unlocked to avoid verification failures at centres.[^7] Identity and document layers are now operational dependencies at national-exam scale, and every integration quirk — a stale photograph, a locked fingerprint, a mismatched name — becomes a candidate-facing failure the evening before a deadline.

### 5. Chhattisgarh's MSP paddy procurement runs on AgriStack rails (L5 — Sectoral)

Kharif marketing season 2026–27 paddy procurement at minimum support price in Chhattisgarh has begun under a digital surveillance framework that makes registration on the AgriStack portal compulsory: farmers must hold an AgriStack Farmer ID and link their land parcel's (khasra) Farm ID to it, with biometric authentication, digital crop surveys, electronic weighing scales and QR codes tracking every stage from registration through weighing, payment and storage to the supply of milled rice.[^8] The state frames it as keeping non-genuine sellers out and manipulation margins down; the consumer-side flip side is that a compulsory digital ID is now the gate to a livelihood entitlement, and registration friction — deadlines, documentation, camp availability — converts directly into exclusion risk. The pattern is national: Andaman & Nicobar has set a November 30 deadline, warning that benefits including subsidised fertiliser booking and PM-KISAN will stop for farmers without a valid Farmer ID.[^9]

## Quick takes

- **L2 — Payments:** SBI's UPI services go down for scheduled maintenance from 1:15 to 2:00 AM IST on November 19; UPI Lite (₹1,000 per-transaction cap) stays available through the window.[^10]
- **L6 — Governance:** The Controller General of Accounts issued an order dated November 18 reiterating compliance with DARPG's office memoranda and daily monitoring of the CPGRAMS grievance portal across its pay-and-accounts offices.[^11]
- **L7 — Trust:** TRAI's draft Responsible AI guidelines for telecom remain open for stakeholder comments until November 27,[^12] while MeitY's consent-manager registration portal (dpbi.gov.in) continues accepting applications following last week's launch under the DPDP Act.
