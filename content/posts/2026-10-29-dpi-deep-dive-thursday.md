# DPI Deep Dive — Thursday | October 29, 2026

**Focus layer:** L5 — Sectoral Infrastructure (ABHA/ABDM, AgriStack, eCourts)  
**Coverage period:** 22–29 October 2026; checked through 08:30 IST on 29 October

## Executive summary

This week the sectoral-infrastructure layer crossed a threshold: from counting registrations to gating entitlements. AgriStack announced that India has created 11 crore Farmer IDs, five months ahead of its March 2027 deadline — and within days of that milestone, reporting emerged that the Prime Minister will push states to adopt a fertiliser-entitlement framework that turns the same registry, land records and Aadhaar biometrics into the gatekeepers of subsidised fertiliser purchase. The legal counterpoint landed midweek: a LiveLaw analysis argues AgriStack's mass enrolment sits on a "consent deficit" that the DPDP Act's own architecture does not forgive. In health, the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission is consolidating its citizen surface — the standalone ABHA app will be discontinued from 31 October, with citizens steered to Aarogya Setu 2.0 — while simultaneously pitching outward, from NITI Aayog's Global-South framing to NHA-level talks with Abu Dhabi's health data establishment. In justice, Adalat AI's speech-recognition tools have reached more than 6,000 courtrooms across 11 states with a randomised evaluation underway, days before a parliamentary committee formally appraises the e-Courts project and AI in justice delivery. The common thread: sectoral DPI is graduating from build-out to reliance, and the accountability machinery — consent law, evaluation studies, legislative scrutiny — is arriving in the same week.

## Key developments

### 1. AgriStack crosses 11 crore Farmer IDs — five months ahead of the March 2027 deadline

The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare announced on 24 October that India achieved its target of creating 11 crore Farmer IDs by the night of 23 October, around five months before the original deadline of 31 March 2027. [ETGovernment, citing PTI](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital-india/centre-beats-march-2027-target-issues-11-crore-unique-farmer-ids-under-agristack/135169709), reports Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan announcing the milestone in a video message; the Farmer ID — an 11-digit *Kisan Pehchaan Patra* — is described as a single document to cut repeated visits to government offices and banks across schemes, credit, crop insurance and disaster relief. The government also stated that more than six crore farmers have already received benefits through Farmer ID-based service delivery.

The registry is one of three core AgriStack registries — alongside geo-referenced village maps and the crop sown registry — under the Digital Agriculture Mission approved by the Union Cabinet on 2 September 2024, with Farmer IDs linked dynamically to state land records, livestock, crops sown, demographic and family details. [The Indian Express's UPSC Key](https://indianexpress.com/article/upsc-current-affairs/upsc-key-farmer-id-obc-creamy-layer-row-uae-oil-spill-10936036) and [current-affairs roundups](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/farmer-id-scheme-explained) compiling the registry's reported profile note the skew: around 81% of registrants are small and marginal farmers, roughly 26% are women, and about 15% are aged 70 or above.

Two reading cautions. First, the milestone measures enrolment, not accuracy — "created" IDs are a counter of issuance, and the utility of every downstream use case (the fertiliser framework below, credit, insurance) depends on land-record correctness at the village level, which no headline number certifies. Second, the demographics matter for design: a registry where a sixth of farmers are over 70 and four-fifths hold small or marginal plots is a registry whose verification burden falls on the least digitally-served.

### 2. The consent deficit arrives at scale: AgriStack meets the DPDP Act

Days before the milestone, [LiveLaw carried a detailed legal analysis](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/farmer-data-point-agristack-consent-deficit-dpdp-act-555490) (22 October, by Sarvesh JP and Deepanjana Saha) arguing that AgriStack's architecture cannot lawfully operate without the consent mechanics, purpose limitation and accountability that the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and *K.S. Puttaswamy* demand. Its core moves: state agriculture departments are Data Fiduciaries under Sections 2(i) and 2(s) — "person" expressly includes the State — and joint fiduciaries under Section 8(1) given Centre-state convergence; Section 7(b)'s consent-free ground extends only to delivering the specified subsidy or benefit, not to building a persistent, multi-purpose farmer profile or onward sharing with agtech firms; and any consent extracted by conditioning PM-KISAN or MSP access on enrolment is conditional by definition and therefore invalid under Section 6(2).

The authors also note the DPDP Act has no sensitive-data tier: land title, plot coordinates, sowing patterns and credit history are unremarkable in isolation, but aggregated and geo-tagged they form a granular economic biography — which should trigger Significant Data Fiduciary duties under Section 10. A Section 17(2)(a) sovereignty-and-security exemption is described as implausible for a farmer registry under the proportionality test.

The timing is the story. This critique lands the week the registry crossed 11 crore, as the fertiliser framework (below) prepares to make the registry an entitlement gate, and amid the news industry's Supreme Court challenge to the DPDP Act — the Act's consent architecture is now being stress-tested from three directions at once. Whatever the merits of each challenge, AgriStack is the largest pending test of whether the DPDP Act's definitions bind the State as they bind firms.

### 3. From ID to quota: the Prime Minister takes AgriStack-based fertiliser entitlements to the states

[The Financial Express reports](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-pm-to-push-states-on-fertiliser-subsidy-reforms-targets-agri-stack-rollout-4371585) that the Prime Minister will advise states to adopt a "Framework for Fertilizer Sale" (FFS) built on AgriStack: farmers book fertiliser on a mobile app using their landholding and cropping data, receive a QR-coded quota token, and collect it at a retailer's point-of-sale with Aadhaar biometric authentication, with each quota tied to a designated distributor. FFS pilots ran across 222 districts in 29 states and UTs in Kharif 2026, with fertiliser sales down 28% during the pilot period; Madhya Pradesh, which fully implemented the framework, cut consumption from 3.6 to 3.2 million tonnes, saving ₹764 crore. The iFMS backbone already covers over 14 crore Aadhaar-linked buyers and 2.5 lakh retailers. The item reportedly tops the agenda at the chief secretaries' conference in the last week of December.

This is the layer's graduation moment: AgriStack's first mass entitlement use case. The consumer test is exclusion. A quota system is only as inclusive as the land records beneath it — farmers whose holdings are wrong, fragmented, or unrecorded (tenant cultivators are the classic case) get no token, and land-record correction channels suddenly become critical infrastructure rather than paperwork. The biometric step adds a second failure mode familiar from PDS rollouts: failed authentication should mean an offline fallback, not a lost quota. The pilot's 28% consumption decline is presented as leakage reduction, but it bundles everything — genuine reform, crop-mix shifts, and farmers who simply could not get through. Publishing pilot-period exclusion audits alongside subsidy savings would make that bundle separable. This story also binds L5 to L1: Aadhaar biometric authentication is now the transactional hinge for farm inputs.

### 4. ABDM consolidates the citizen surface: ABHA app sunsets 31 October, and the Global-South pitch widens

The Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission has announced that [the standalone ABHA app will be discontinued in the production environment from 31 October 2026](https://ehealth.eletsonline.com/2026/10/nha-uae-digital-health-collaboration-sahi-bodh-secure-data-environments-malaffi), with citizens urged to transition to Aarogya Setu 2.0 — the National Health Authority's unified health app, [relaunched on 29 June 2026](https://www.ndtv.com/health/news/aarogya-setu-2-0-indias-pandemic-app-evolves-into-a-unified-digital-health-platform-11709530) as the national digital health platform. The advisory, which state administrations such as Andaman & Nicobar have been relaying since around 23 October, positions Aarogya Setu 2.0 as the single surface for creating and managing ABHA, consent-based sharing of health records, digitising physical medical documents (with DigiLocker-backed storage), appointment booking, teleconsultation, and discovery of doctors, labs and blood availability. The ABHA number itself — the ID asset — continues; what ends is a citizen-facing app.

The consolidation logic is sound — fewer surfaces to secure and explain, one app where the pandemic-era Aarogya Setu already has name recognition — but transition quality is the open question: what in-app migration support exists for users whose records live only in the ABHA app, and how the sunset is communicated beyond Union Territory e-papers and trade press. Meanwhile the mission pitched outward at FICCI HEAL 2026: NITI Aayog member Dr M. Srinivas, former AIIMS Delhi director, [called ABDM data](https://www.ndtv.com/health/ayushman-bharat-data-could-transform-indias-healthcare-says-niti-aayog-member-12246968) "a gamechanger" for India and the Global South, while flagging that AI on health data requires quality data and privacy safeguards — and separately, Elets reported on 28 October that the NHA explored collaboration with Abu Dhabi's Department of Health spanning secure data environments, AI validation and benchmarking through India's SAHI and BODH initiatives, and Abu Dhabi's Malaffi health information exchange. Health data governance is becoming DPI diplomacy; whether ABDM's consent-based federated design or Malaffi's licensing-mandated PPP model becomes the reference export is worth watching.

### 5. Justice plumbing: Adalat AI reaches 6,000 courtrooms, and Parliament is about to look at e-Courts

[Semafor reported on 26–28 October](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/26/2026/ai-takes-on-the-paperwork-clogging-indias-court-system) that Adalat AI — a nonprofit whose speech-recognition software is trained on India's legal system — has reached more than 6,000 courtrooms across 11 states, and that some jurisdictions now mandate its use for recording witness testimony. The workflow is deliberately manual-in-the-loop: the judge dictates what should be recorded, both parties check the text on screen before moving on, and the verified transcript becomes the official record. Co-founder and CEO Utkarsh Saxena frames the mission as plumbing: "We aren't automating decision-making, we're automating the administrative processes to give judges back time to do the things that actually matter." The stakes are concrete — in 2024, nearly three-in-four people in Indian prisons were undertrials — and the evidence regime is unusually honest for govtech: internal studies show more witnesses heard and orders issued, and a randomised evaluation with J-PAL is underway.

The timing is notable. Per a [Lok Sabha bulletin of 28 October](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/governance/parliamentary-panel-to-review-rti-act-implementation-cic-functioning-amid-case-pendency/135271055), the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice will appraise the e-Courts Mission Mode Project and examine AI use in justice delivery — putting courtroom AI before legislative scrutiny just as it scales. Around the same window, NICSI floated a tender for hardware for 400 paperless courts, and the Supreme Court's AI Committee had released draft AI Regulations in June 2026. Context from [PIB's February backgrounder](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226283) frames the trajectory: SUPACE research assistance, AI transcription and translation under eCourts Phase III, with SUVAS now covering translation across English and 19 Indian languages. The sectoral question shifts from "can AI work in court?" to "does it measurably shorten cases without shortening rights?" — and for once, an RCT is in place to answer.

## Trends & implications

- **Registration is giving way to rationing (L5×L1).** AgriStack's fertiliser framework makes the farmer registry and Aadhaar biometrics transactional gatekeepers for farm inputs. The layer's value and its risk now concentrate at the same point: the entitlement gate, where land-record errors become exclusion.
- **Consent architecture is the binding constraint (L5×L7).** The LiveLaw analysis, the DNPA challenge to the DPDP Act, and Srinivas's own "privacy safeguards" caveat all converge: the DPDP Act's definitions of fiduciary, purpose limitation and valid consent are about to be tested against the State's largest sectoral registries.
- **Citizen surfaces are consolidating (L5×L3).** One ABHA app into Aarogya Setu 2.0, health documents into DigiLocker, records via Scan & Share — fewer surfaces, higher stakes per surface, and transition communication becomes a consumer-protection issue in itself.
- **Evidence regimes are maturing.** A J-PAL randomised evaluation for courtroom AI, parliamentary appraisal of e-Courts, and draft AI regulations suggest sectoral DPI is entering an era where "it works" must be demonstrated, not asserted.
- **DPI diplomacy now has a sectoral edge.** Secure data environments, AI benchmarking frameworks and health information exchanges are becoming India's export offer — and the design differences between ABDM's consent model and Gulf-style mandated exchanges will shape what is actually exported.
