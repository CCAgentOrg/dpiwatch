# DPI Deep Dive — Thursday | October 29, 2026

**Focus layer:** L4 — Commerce & Logistics (ONDC, GeM)  
**Coverage period:** 22–29 October 2026; checked through 08:30 IST on 29 October

## Executive summary

This week the commerce layer ran on two tracks: fee relief and trust questions. At GeM Samvad 2026, the Government e-Marketplace cut transaction charges from 0.50% to 0.30%, doubled the exemption threshold for micro and small enterprises, and capped charges on very large orders — while pointing to a fully wired TReDS network and collateral-free lending as the real working-capital story. ONDC used the Urban Mobility India conference in Bhubaneswar to report 15 transit operators, 24-plus cities and 16.5 million bookings for September 2026, and GeM pushed a storefront of artisan products in front of government buyers. But the week's sharpest scrutiny came from the layer's own data: an AI-assisted audit of Madhya Pradesh purchase records that has now fixed accountability on 392 municipal officials and staff, and a documented investigation into how white-labelled Chinese machines can reach government tenders through verification exemptions. Commerce DPI is getting cheaper to transact on and much more visible to auditors and investigators — the open question is whether verification matures as fast as visibility.

## Key developments

### 1. GeM Samvad 2026: the fee cut is real; the working-capital rails matter more

The week's headline procurement reform came at GeM Samvad 2026, organised with Laghu Udyog Bharati. Per the ministry's release carried by [DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/10/gem-cuts-transaction-charges-to-0-30-raises-exemption-threshold-to-%E2%82%B910-lakh-for-mses), transaction charges fall from 0.50% to 0.30% of order value, the exemption threshold rises from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh, and charges on orders above ₹10 crore are capped at ₹3 lakh — down from an earlier maximum of ₹72.50 lakh. GeM also disclosed that nearly 97% of orders already attract no transaction charges, that caution-money deposits were discontinued in July 2025, and that original-equipment vendor assessment charges have been reduced since January 2024.

Read as a cost curve, this is more redistribution than abolition. The fee cut applies to exactly the orders most MSEs do not win; the real benefit to a small supplier is that mid-value orders between ₹5 lakh and ₹10 lakh now fall outside the charge, and that a ₹100-crore order pays ₹3 lakh instead of a de facto ₹50 lakh-plus. The more consequential items in the release are financial: GeM is now integrated with all five TReDS exchanges as announced in the Union Budget 2026–27, and GeM SAHAY extends collateral-free lending against purchase orders. For a small supplier, the binding constraint on government business is rarely 0.20% of order value — it is the weeks between delivery and payment, which is precisely what invoice discounting and PO-based credit address. GeM CEO Mihir Kumar and Additional CEO Ajit B. Chavan also pointed to the collaboration machinery with Laghu Udyog Bharati — 108 virtual conferences under the 2024 MoU, with facilitation counters planned. The testable follow-up is disclosure of fees actually collected before and after the change, and TReDS discounting volumes routed through GeM — numbers that would show whether the reform moved money or moved headlines.

### 2. ONDC counts its transit month: 16.5 million bookings, and the caveats inside the number

At the 19th Urban Mobility India conference (Bhubaneswar, 23–24 October, organised by MoHUA through the Institute of Urban Transport), [ONDC reported](https://x.com/ONDC_Official/status/2114358616585302457) that its network now connects 15 transit operators across more than 24 cities and recorded 16.5 million bookings in September 2026. The [conference session recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nIWjfGPBlwc) adds texture: roughly 32 months into the mobility build-out, enabled services span ticketing, passes and recharges, with multimodal journey QRs, counter-to-digital QR conversion, and bulk ticketing for events. The same session floated a longer-term "national digital transit and tourism stack" being explored with Maharashtra and the Ministry of Tourism, and closed with a pointed ask: operators should re-evaluate pricing so that the efficiency of digital distribution is passed back to commuters and to the apps doing the distribution.

The 16.5 million figure is a genuine scale marker — transit is now ONDC's most socially legible domain — but it is a network-side count, not a service outcome. "Bookings" does not equal completed journeys or unique commuters, and the disclosure carries no split by city, operator, transport mode, cancellations or fulfilment. Without those, one cannot tell whether the growth is concentrated in a few large metro pass programmes or broad-based across state bus corporations. The pricing ask is the more interesting governance signal: it frames digital ticketing not as a cost-saving to be pocketed by operators, but as a saving with a claimant chain — commuter, distributor app, operator. Who keeps the margin will decide whether open-network transit stays cheaper than proprietary aggregation. Context from the same conference: [PIB reported](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2328059&reg=48&lang=2) the valedictory by Union Minister Manohar Lal, with electric buses now operational in 28 cities — the physical fleet the booking layer sits on top of.

### 3. AABHAR: a storefront is market access, not a market

On 26 October, [PIB detailed](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetail.aspx?PRID=2328424&reg=6&lang=1) "The AABHAR Collection", GeM's digital storefront of curated gifts and hampers sourced from regional craft hubs. It currently features 294 product listings from 10 state and central handloom and handicraft emporiums, tied to the One District, One Product initiative and GI-tagged products, with seven more organisations at an advanced onboarding stage: the Coffee Board, Manjusha and Biswa Bangla (West Bengal), J&K Handloom & Handicrafts Marketing Corporation, Merakai (Nagaland), Utkalika (Odisha) and Panthoibi (Manipur). CEO Mihir Kumar framed the collection as enhancing visibility for "underserved seller groups."

The design logic is sound. Government demand is large, predictable and creditworthy; artisan supply is fragmented; and emporiums — already public institutions — can act as the onboarding intermediary that individual weavers cannot be. Routeing that supply into government gifting and procurement gives artisans a sales channel that does not depend on festival-season footfall or marketplace advertising economics. The caveat is the one this column has applied to fair-based announcements before: listings are not orders, orders are not deliveries, and deliveries are not net income to the artisan. The PIB release offers participation figures, not transaction data — no order values, no fulfilment performance, no seller-side earnings. The durable test is whether AABHAR shows repeat purchase patterns across the fiscal year and whether the emporium model actually passes through a fair share of value. GeM could publish that from its own transaction logs; until it does, AABHAR is best read as an open market-access rail whose throughput is unmeasured.

### 4. Madhya Pradesh's AI audit of purchase data: procurement DPI as an accountability surface

The most consequential governance story of the week barely travelled outside Bhopal. Per [The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Oct/26/ai-helps-madhya-pradesh-govt-identify-135-chief-municipal-officials-for-graft-in-digital-purchases), an AI-based audit of purchase data from two portals — MP e-Nagar Palika and GeM — has led to action against 392 officials and staff of urban local bodies, including 135 chief municipal officers, with accountability formally fixed, Additional Chief Secretary (Urban Development and Housing) Sanjay Dubey said on 26 October. Measures include recovery proceedings, withheld increments, demotions and dismissals. At the time of an earlier training programme whose video went viral, 146 officials had faced action including seven dismissals, around ₹4 crore had been recovered, and more than 80 contractors and suppliers had faced action; the count has since grown. The department had flagged [large price differences in ULB purchases](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/madhya-pradesh/2026/Oct/22/mp-ulbs-under-scanner-over-huge-price-differences-in-purchases-acs-warns-officials-of-action) days earlier.

Two things are notable. First, the audit surface is the purchase log itself: the same transactional detail that makes e-procurement efficient also makes over-pricing comparable across buyers, and anomaly detection at this scale is simply not possible in a paper procurement regime. This is DPI's accountability dividend, delivered in-state. Second, process discipline matters more here, not less. Algorithmic flagging identifies statistical outliers; establishing graft requires inquiry, and officials will have appeal rights. The story names aggregate figures, and this post will do the same — individual culpability should be established by process, not by dashboard. The replicability question is the real headline: every state's buyers transact on the same GeM. Madhya Pradesh built the audit machinery; states that have not are procuring on the same rails without the instrument that keeps them honest.

### 5. White-labelled Chinese machines reach tenders through verification exemptions

[Swarajya's investigation](https://swarajyamag.com/technology/chinese-machines-are-entering-indian-government-tenders-under-indian-labels) (28 October, Karan Kamble) documents how machines made by Chinese manufacturers may enter government tenders under Indian labels. Since the post-Galwan tightening (Rule 144(xi) of GFR 2017), bidders from land-border countries must be registered by a DPIIT-administered committee, and GeM sellers certify they are not owned or controlled from a land-border country and are not agents or resellers for such firms. A February 2023 Department of Expenditure order extends this to Indian companies in sensitive sectors — including medical devices — that operate under transfer-of-technology arrangements with such firms. The hole, per the report, is in verification: GeM normally validates a self-declared manufacturer through an outside agency, but exempts medical-device manufacturers holding a CDSCO manufacturing licence and holders of BIS licences. The report traces customs records from early 2025 — 40 ventilator "spare parts" consignments from a Hong Kong supplier, with the main housing assembly constituting roughly seven-eighths of set value — and an Indian company that obtained 18 model licences in about two years, including ventilators whose CDSCO-registered dimensions match a Chinese OEM's model to the millimetre and whose BIS certificates list breathing modes that are that OEM's EU trademarks.

Treat the specific allegations as allegation-stage: Swarajya reviewed documents, but no regulator has ruled on these cases, and the report itself notes GeM has previously suspended sellers of Hikvision, Dahua and Lenovo brands when the rule was applied. The structural finding stands independent of any single case: licence-based exemptions mean "CDSCO-licensed" and "verified manufacturer" are different claims, and self-certification is only as strong as the cross-checks around it. The report's remedies are concrete — a value test separating components from finished machines, matching seller PANs against import data (IEC records), brand-level penalties, and time-bound DPIIT decisions — all the more salient given [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/technology/india-relaxes-rules-chinese-investment-2026-01-15/) in January 2026 that the government has been working toward relaxing the registration regime for some sectors. A commerce rail that cuts fees (story 1) while its verification seams are probed (story 5) is growing cost advantage and trust risk at the same time.

## Trends & implications

- **Fee relief and verification are on diverging tracks (L4×L7).** GeM is making transactions cheaper while an investigation documents how its verification exemptions can be routed; cost-side and trust-side reform must move together or the layer optimises for the wrong thing.
- **Procurement data is now an audit asset (L4×L6).** Madhya Pradesh shows the purchase log can drive accountability at scale. The differentiator across states is no longer data access — everyone buys on GeM — but audit machinery.
- **Bookings and listings are inputs, not outcomes.** ONDC's transit count and AABHAR's storefront both disclose participation and nothing downstream. Order, fulfilment and payment splits are the metrics that would make these claims testable.
- **Working capital is the quiet reform (L4×L2).** TReDS across all five exchanges plus SAHAY target the actual cash-conversion pain of small suppliers; discounting volumes routed through GeM would show whether it bites.
- **The physical layer got its own budget line.** The Cabinet cleared a ₹10,000-crore container manufacturing scheme on 28 October — [DD India reported](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/10/cabinet-approves-rs-10000-crore-scheme-to-boost-container-manufacturing/) — a reminder that the commerce stack's throughput still depends on steel boxes and rolling stock.
