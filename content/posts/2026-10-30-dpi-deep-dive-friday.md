# DPI Deep Dive — Friday | October 30, 2026

**Focus Layer:** L5 — Sectoral Infrastructure: agriculture, health and justice services  
**Coverage Period:** October 23–30, 2026

## Executive summary

This week's sectoral-DPI story is about the gap between registration and use. Agriculture crossed 11 crore Farmer IDs five months ahead of the March 2027 deadline, shifting attention to what those IDs unlock, how errors get corrected, and who is left out when identity is tied to land records. A parliamentary standing committee has picked the e-Courts Mission Mode Project, digitisation and AI in justice delivery as appraisal subjects for the year — the first structured legislative scrutiny of the justice layer in this session. In health, the National Health Authority pitched India's digital-health playbook to officials in Abu Dhabi, a NITI Aayog member described ABDM's data as a "goldmine" while pushing one interoperable ABHA-plus-EHR platform, and West Bengal's first 75 days on AB-PMJAY showed the distance between card issuance (4.13 crore cards) and actual treatment (1.8 lakh patients). The common test across all four: issuance numbers are cheap; utilisation, correction and consent are where infrastructure earns its keep.

## Key developments

### 1. AgriStack crosses 11 crore Farmer IDs — five months early, with the hard part next

The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare announced on October 24 that India had created 11 crore unique Farmer IDs (Kisan Pehchaan Patra) under the AgriStack framework, completing by the night of October 23 a target the Prime Minister had set for March 31, 2027. Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan framed the milestone as a "historic day," crediting coordination between the Centre, state governments and field-level officials.[^1]

Two official claims deserve attention beyond the headline. First, the ministry says more than 6 crore farmers have already received direct benefits through Farmer ID-based service delivery — credit, insurance, scheme payments — which is a genuine utilisation figure, not an issuance count, and implies roughly 55% of ID holders have transacted on the identity at least once.[^2] Second, states are building arrangements to include tenant farmers and actual cultivators who do not feature in land records — a necessary patch, because Farmer IDs are generated by synchronising farmer details with digitised land records, and the person tilling the soil is often not the person on the record.[^1]

The 11-crore figure should also be read against what the registries feed. A Hindustan Times analysis published October 25 notes the Digital Crop Survey covered more than 31.3 crore plots across 648 districts in Rabi 2025–26, and asks the sharper question: India has built the data foundation, but has not yet built the infrastructure to reward farmers for measurable climate outcomes — water saved, emissions reduced, soil improved. The same analysis warns that records collected for scheme delivery must not silently become licences for commercial use, and that independent audits and grievance channels matter as much as coverage.[^3]

That warning is the right one for this milestone. Speed of issuance is now proven; accuracy, correction and consent are not. The missing public numbers are state-wise error rates, how many correction requests were filed and resolved, and what happens when a land record and a crop record disagree. An ID that cannot be corrected is a liability, not infrastructure.

### 2. Parliamentary panel puts e-Courts, digitisation and AI in courts under appraisal

On October 28, the department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice finalised its subjects for the year, and the justice layer made the list in force. For the Department of Justice, the committee will appraise the e-Courts Mission Mode Project, assess fast-track special courts, review Centrally-sponsored judicial infrastructure schemes, examine digitisation and the use of artificial intelligence in justice delivery, and evaluate the performance of legal aid institutions.[^4]

The same committee will review implementation of the Right to Information Act and the functioning of the Central Information Commission — which faces more than 10,000 pending cases despite operating at its full sanctioned strength of 11 information commissioners — plus public grievance redressal mechanisms, including awareness of CPGRAMS and CPENGRAMS, the National Litigation Policy, and the availability of Supreme Court and High Court judgments in Eighth Schedule languages.[^4]

For the e-Courts layer, this is the accountability moment Phase III has lacked. The mission's own numbers are substantial — the programme reports over 1.25 crore cases e-filed and more than 753 crore pages of court records digitised since inception — but those are input metrics, and the National Judicial Data Grid shows why throughput matters more: pendency at the Supreme Court alone runs to lakhs of cases, dominated by special leave petitions.[^5] The committee's appraisal can force answers the mission's press releases do not volunteer: what share of courts actually function paperless, what e-filing adoption looks like among litigants without lawyers, how the AI tools being examined are governed, and whether digitisation has moved disposal rates or only moved files.

The cross-layer reading is direct: the same committee oversees CPGRAMS (L6 governance) and RTI/CIC (L7 transparency), so its justice-layer findings will land in a system already strained on grievance and information rights. One appraisal, three layers.

### 3. NHA takes the health-DPI playbook to Abu Dhabi

Dr. Sunil Kumar Barnwal, CEO of the National Health Authority, met officials of the UAE Integrative Medicine Council and the Abu Dhabi Department of Health last week to explore digital-health collaboration, accompanied by an officer of the Indian Embassy in Abu Dhabi. The discussions covered secure data environments for health-data research, India's Strategy for AI in Healthcare (SAHI), the BODH benchmarking platform for health AI, genomic data within Abu Dhabi's Malaffi health information exchange, PM-JAY claims data as a window into treatment patterns and costs, and shared platforms including the National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX) and the Unified Health Interface (UHI). Barnwal also flagged data governance, sustainable financing for DPI and workforce capacity as prerequisites.[^6]

This is DPI diplomacy in action — the same export pitch made at FICCI forums and Global South convenings, now in bilateral form. The instructive part is the vocabulary used abroad: "secure data environments," governance frameworks, benchmarking before deployment. Those are research-governance disciplines India's own health data has not fully adopted at home. If NHA is prepared to recommend SDE-style controls to Abu Dhabi, the test of seriousness is whether secondary use of Indian health data — by researchers, AI developers, insurers — gets the same gated, auditable treatment domestically. The DPDP rules (L7) will set the legal floor; NHA's own operational guidance will decide whether the floor is lived in.

### 4. "Data goldmine": NITI Aayog's framing test for ABDM

Speaking at FICCI Heal 2026 on October 27, NITI Aayog member Dr. M. Srinivas — a former AIIMS Delhi director — called the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission's data a "gamechanger" and a "goldmine" for India and the Global South, saying that "once this data is harnessed, AI can be optimally utilised" for transformative outcomes. He added that through the NHA, the government is building "structured data with interoperability": "We will have one platform of ABHA ID and electronic health records, which will be interoperable. That is something that we are pushing for."[^7]

At the same event, Srinivas pressed a different, complementary point: Indian hospitals need a basic minimum standard of patient safety and quality, so that the world recognises Indian healthcare's floor — not just its digital ceiling.[^8]

The "one platform" ambition is the right destination, but the current mileage argues for patience. As of the Mission Steering Group's last review in July, ABDM had issued over 93.95 crore ABHA numbers and linked over 105 crore health records, but only 2.72 lakh facilities had adopted ABDM-enabled software — identity at population scale, records still concentrated in a minority of facilities.[^9] A "goldmine" whose seams are thinly worked is the honest description. The analysis that matters now: what share of linked records are full EHRs rather than single-visit documents, which states and facility classes are actually generating them, and who audits downstream use of the data once it is "harnessed." Data called a goldmine without a consent architecture attached is a phrase, not a policy.

### 5. West Bengal's first 75 days on PM-JAY rails: cards fast, treatment slower

AB-PMJAY has delivered medical services worth ₹291 crore in West Bengal since its August 16, 2026 rollout in the state, treating more than 1.8 lakh patients, according to figures reported October 29. Around 4.13 crore residents have received Ayushman Bharat health cards, with 1.8 crore more applications pending, and roughly 4,600 patients have used the scheme's portability provision for treatment outside the state. Nearly 2,500 hospitals in the state are empanelled, out of a reported national network of around 40,000. The Centre has allocated ₹426 crore for implementation, with the state contributing ₹284 crore.[^10]

The context makes this a genuine sectoral-infrastructure story rather than a scheme announcement: the rollout followed years of resistance from the previous state administration, which ran its own Swasthya Sathi programme — which itself has received applications from nearly 2.5 crore people. The state's first-month milestone, announced by Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari in September, was 50,000 beneficiaries; the run-rate has clearly accelerated since.[^10]

But read the ratios. 4.13 crore cards in 75 days is one of the fastest identity-issuance exercises in the country's health system; 1.8 lakh treated is roughly 4.4% of card-holders. Cards measure eligibility infrastructure; treatment measures delivery. The questions that will tell us whether the rails work: claim-settlement turnaround inside the state, hospital empanelment density by district (not just the state total), denial and fraud-audit rates, and whether portability cases are settled without the patient chasing paperwork. NHCX's claims standardisation — if and when PM-JAY claims run through it at scale — is the technical fix for exactly this opacity.

## Trends and implications

- **Issuance is no longer the bottleneck; correction and use are.** Eleven crore Farmer IDs, 93.95 crore ABHA numbers, 4.13 crore health cards — every sectoral registry now issues identity at population scale. The binding constraints have moved to error-resolution routes, consent enforcement and actual service completion.
- **Parliamentary scrutiny is becoming the DPI accountability channel.** The e-Courts appraisal, RTI/CIC review and CPGRAMS examination sit in one committee with one calendar — a rare chance for consolidated, public findings across three layers.
- **The export pitch writes a domestic standard.** NHA recommending secure data environments and benchmarked AI in Abu Dhabi creates a benchmark against which its own domestic secondary-use rules will be judged.
- **Utilisation ratios are the metric that matters.** 55% of Farmer ID holders receiving benefits; 4.4% of WB card-holders treated. These denominators, tracked over time, are the honest scorecard of sectoral DPI.
- **Every layer now has a data question attached.** Farm data for climate finance, health data as "goldmine," court data on NJDG — in each sector, the governance of secondary use is catching up with collection, and not yet overtaking it.

## What to watch next

1. AgriStack: state-wise Farmer ID accuracy and correction statistics; progress on tenant-farmer inclusion arrangements; whether PM-KISAN and crop-insurance delivery through Farmer IDs is published as audited outcomes.
2. e-Courts: the standing committee's appraisal report — its evidence sessions, findings on AI in courts, and whether e-filing and paperless-court adoption get measured per-court rather than cumulatively.
3. NHA-UAE: any memorandum or pilot emerging from the Abu Dhabi talks, and whether NHA's domestic guidance for health-data research adopts the SDE model it is exporting.
4. ABDM: the next Mission Steering Group review — whether facility software adoption (2.72 lakh as of July) and full-EHR record depth are reported alongside the ABHA issuance headline.
5. West Bengal: quarterly PM-JAY settlement data — claim turnaround, empanelment by district, portability settlements — as the counterweight to card-issuance announcements.
