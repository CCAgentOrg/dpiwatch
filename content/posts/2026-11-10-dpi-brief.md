# DPI Brief — November 10, 2026

**Top DPI headlines for November 10, 2026** — UPI's record October lands while merchant charges wait for a notification, the health ministry picks AI and cybersecurity as ABDM's next-phase priorities, and the government tells newsrooms — informally — that journalism isn't a data-fiduciary business.

## [L2 · Payments] October was UPI's biggest month ever — and the MDR clock is still not running

UPI processed a record **25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October**, according to NPCI data, and crossed a first: average daily *value* above ₹1 lakh crore ([ETGovernment](http://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/how-government-initiatives-and-policy-push-are-strengthening-indias-infrastructure-for-the-festive-demand-surge/135606258)). That is roughly 4% more volume than August's 24.51 billion, which NPCI itself described as its previous record in a Supreme Court affidavit filed November 3 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/npci-says-mdr-charge-has-been-imposed-to-save-the-upi-ecosystem-from-turning-ouroboric/article71669829.ece)). The affidavit is the sharpest public statement yet of why the network wants merchant charges: "zero MDR" for all UPI is "no longer sustainable" and would burn functional costs into the public exchequer. NPCI's numbers frame who is insulated — person-to-person transfers are 37% of volume and 70% of value and stay free at any amount; person-to-merchant payments up to ₹2,000 remain "entirely free of MDR"; small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh monthly inward are exempt; and merchants cannot pass charges to customers ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/judiciary/mdr-charges-needed-to-sustain-upi-ecosystem-merchants-cant-pass-them-to-customers-npci-tells-sc/3092196)). But the levy's legal basis remains contested — commentators note the amended Section 10A does not itself fix MDR, leaving the September 14 notification and NPCI's circulars to carry the weight ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-merchant-charges-can-npci-legally-impose-fees-on-businesses-126110601476_1.html)). Three weeks after Diwali, the "date to be notified separately" still hasn't been notified. A record month is exactly when consumers are least equipped to notice a charge appearing at the till; the notification, when it comes, should be read against October's baseline.

## [L5 · Sectoral] NHA's national review picks ABDM's next-phase priorities: AI, cybersecurity, and hospital-system integration

The National Health Authority convened a two-day national review in Kolkata on November 5–6 with states and union territories, covering both AB PM-JAY and the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission — and the readout sketches where the sectoral layer goes next ([Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/union-health-ministry-reviews-ab-pmjay-abdm-implementation-sets-priorities-for-digital-health-integration), [Express Healthcare](https://www.expresshealthcare.in/news/ab-pm-jay-and-abdm-implementation-reviewed-by-centre-and-states-at-national-meeting/455791)). Three priorities stand out. First, deeper integration of hospital information systems with ABDM infrastructure, flagged as important for claims processing and adjudication quality — the recognition that a health records network is only as good as the hospitals actually writing into it. Second, AI in healthcare operations, continuing the mission's automation turn. Third — and most notable for a welfare database holding sensitive medical records — cybersecurity received particular attention as a review priority ([Nation Path India](https://nationpathindia.com/health/ayushman-bharat-new-phase-healthcare-ai-digital-integration-2026)). The state-level marker from the same reviews: Patna leads Bihar with over **1.13 crore ABDM-linked digital health records**, followed by Muzaffarpur and Saran ([Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/patna-records-highest-abdm-health-record-linkage-in-bihar-crosses-1-13-cr-digital-health-records)). Linkage counts are the layer's favourite metric; the review's own agenda concedes the harder questions are data quality, claims adjudication, and who protects the records once linked.

## [L7 × L6 · Trust meets Governance] The journalism-exemption fight gets a soft answer, days before obligations bite

The government's response to the DPDP Act's press-freedom problem has arrived — informally. Government sources say journalists and news organisations are **not "data fiduciaries"** under the Act, easing the fear that reporting itself triggers compliance duties, and that the government is "very open-minded" on whatever action is needed ([Rediff](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/journalists-newsrooms-exempt-from-dpdp-act-govt-sources/57707220261107)); a trade publication reported on November 9 that the government is open to revising the Act and its rules if provisions undermine constitutional press protections ([VARINDIA](https://www.varindia.com/news/government-open-to-revising-dpdp-act)). The context is a tightening calendar. The DPDP framework's second phase of obligations begins **November 13** ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-it-act-dpdp-act-ai-compliance-47725.html)); the Digital News Publishers Association's Supreme Court challenge — which targets Sections 7, 17(2), 18, 19, 27, 29, 36, 37 and 44(3) along with Rules 16, 17 and 23 for their absence of a journalistic exemption — is next listed for **November 17** before a bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/articles/data-protection-law-investigative-journalism-557513)). The gap matters: an exemption that lives in sources' remarks and MeitY's goodwill is not an exemption. It can be reinterpreted by the next set of rules, relied on by a data-fiduciary defendant, or narrowed by the Data Protection Board. Newsrooms are asking for the carve-out in the statute, where only Parliament can move it. Watch what — if anything — is notified before the Court sits on the 17th.
