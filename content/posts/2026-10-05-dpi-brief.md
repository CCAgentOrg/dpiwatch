# DPI Brief — October 05, 2026

Window covered: **8:00 a.m. IST on October 4 to 8:00 a.m. IST on October 5.** The date-matched official record was quiet across most of the seven layers. Three useful signals concern sectoral digital services and pension-grievance handling. Two are ministry accounts of systems built or work already under way—not announcements that those systems launched in this 24-hour window.

## 1. Cotton procurement: KAPAS Kisan connects farmers to CCI processes

A [Ministry of Textiles release posted October 4](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseDetail.aspx?PRID=2318919&reg=3&lang=1) says the Cotton Corporation of India will showcase its reforms at a Hyderabad event on October 6. The release describes work undertaken over several years, including farmer registration, digital slot booking, direct payments, quality assessment, traceability, cotton sales and stakeholder engagement. Its KAPAS Kisan platform brings together farmer registration, slot booking, procurement-related communication and Aadhaar-enabled payments.

This is a sectoral digital-service development in **L5**, but not a newly announced national rollout. The release gives no platform user count, transaction total, uptime figure or process for correcting a registration or disputing a slot, weight or payment record. The citizen-facing test is whether farmers can see and correct each step, reconcile the procurement record with the payment received, and get help when the digital route fails. Those are questions for the showcase and subsequent reporting, not outcomes established by the release.

## 2. Silk Mark services and Mera Resham: digitisation paired with field work

The same release reports that Silk Mark Organisation of India has progressively digitised registration, renewal, document processing, pre-inspection, payments, label indenting, sample testing, issuance, records and grievance redressal. The ministry reports **more than 7,000 authorised users**, **500-plus new enrolments annually**, and processing within **one to two working days** when applications meet requirements. These are official programme figures, not an independent audit of usage or service quality. Digitising certification can make the process easier to track; the public account does not explain how applicants can inspect or correct records, or how complaints are closed.

The release also reports the Central Silk Board’s Mera Resham Abhiyaan, a technology-transfer campaign launched in 2025. It says the campaign reached **75,000 farmers** through **1,400-plus programmes** in **129 districts across 26 States and Union Territories**, using digital baseline surveys and district sericulture plans alongside training and field demonstrations. This is relevant to **L5’s sectoral-service layer**, but should not be mistaken for an AgriStack integration or a new app launch. The useful signal is the combination of digital planning with local delivery; whether those plans improve adoption and farmer outcomes needs evidence beyond participation counts.

## 3. Pension Adalat: CPENGRAMS complaints move to a hybrid hearing

A [Department of Pension and Pensioners’ Welfare release posted October 4](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseDetail.aspx?PRID=2318868&reg=3&lang=1) announced the **17th Pension Adalat** for October 5 at the Indian Institute of Public Administration, with hybrid participation. The ministry said **897 long-pending grievances** from super-senior pensioners and family pensioners, concerning **24 Ministries and Departments**, had been filed on the CPENGRAMS portal and would be taken up for redressal.

For context, the department reports that in the preceding 16 Adalats **20,676 of 28,797 grievances (71.79%)** were resolved on the spot; it says the remaining cases were addressed later through inter-ministerial review. These are the department’s cumulative figures. The 897 cases are scheduled for consideration, not confirmed closures. This is a **L6 governance** update: the digital portal supplies the docket, while the hearing provides a route to resolve cases crossing departmental boundaries. The measure to watch is case-level closure time and post-hearing outcomes—not simply how many complaints were listed or heard.

The day’s signal is not a new all-India DPI launch. For both, transparency depends on citizens being able to trace, correct and challenge records—and on agencies publishing outcomes, not only activity counts.
