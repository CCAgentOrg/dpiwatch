# DPI Brief — November 17, 2026

The identity layer failed its accessibility test again in the Supreme Court — with contempt now on the table — while NPCI's own statistics exposed how unevenly credit-on-UPI has actually shipped inside the apps Indians use. At the trust layer, the consent-manager registration portal finally went live; the regulator that must approve every application still has no members. The health stack spent the weekend showcasing ABHA at the India International Trade Fair.

## 1. Supreme Court threatens contempt as Aadhaar face authentication keeps locking out blind users [L1: Identity]

A Bench headed by Justice J.B. Pardiwala warned of [contempt proceedings if the Union government and UIDAI fail to file a detailed response](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/supreme-court-warns-contempt-if-authorities-fail-to-give-detailed-reply-to-plea-on-visually-impaired-unable-to-do-aadhaar-face-authentication/article71707355.ece) to an application by advocate Amar Jain — a blind lawyer who was himself a petitioner in *Pragya Prasun Singh v. Union of India*, the 2025 judgment that ordered accessible digital KYC. His complaint is blunt: the Aadhaar App's face authentication requires eyes to be "attentive" and looking into the frame, which a visually-impaired user simply cannot do — and since e-KYC for bank accounts, SIM cards and financial products increasingly [relies solely on face authentication](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Nov/16/sc-warns-centre-uidai-of-contempt-over-lack-of-accessible-aadhaar-authentication-for-visually-impaired-persons), that is exclusion with no workaround. Jain annexed his own rejection slip: a private bank refused his credit-card e-KYC in October 2026. The application also flags that there is no grievance mechanism for accessibility complaints against KYC apps, no visible accessibility audits, nodal officers or human review of failed attempts across regulated entities — and seeks directions to the Union, RBI, MeitY, TRAI, DoT, SEBI, PFRDA and IRDAI to actually implement the 2025 ruling. A year after the judgment, the gap between the DPI identity promise and its delivery is being policed by contempt jurisdiction.

## 2. NPCI's own data shows the big two UPI apps trailing on credit features [L2: Payments]

PhonePe and Google Pay processed 77.4% of all UPI transactions in September — but per [NPCI data reported by Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/phonepe-google-pay-trail-smaller-apps-in-rupay-credit-card-upi-features-126111601733_1.html), the two largest apps trail smaller rivals on nearly every RuPay credit-card-on-UPI feature. On biometric authentication for credit transactions up to ₹10,000, Cred leads with 54.7% of its credit-on-UPI volume done via biometrics, followed by Kiwi (29.14%) and Scapia (26.28%). On AutoPay for RuPay credit cards, Paytm alone holds 40% — versus its 8.1% overall UPI share — while PhonePe and Google Pay together account for just 22.8%. EMI conversion is the exception: HDFC Bank's app leads with 19.6% by value, with PhonePe second at 17.7%. Since the RBI permitted only RuPay credit cards to link with UPI in 2022, the network's credit push has been NPCI's flagship monetisation story — and industry sources say the biggest apps simply aren't competing on it. If the two default apps don't surface credit-on-UPI, the feature that gives the zero-MDR rail its revenue future stalls at the front door.

## 3. The consent-manager portal is live; the Board that approves it is not staffed [L7: Trust]

MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan on November 14 [launched the online registration portal for DPDP consent managers](https://techobserver.in/news/egov/meity-dpdp-consent-manager-registration-portal-330464), a day after the registration framework took effect under the phased commencement of the DPDP Rules — exactly one year after their November 13, 2025 notification. The bar is real: Indian-incorporated companies, ₹2 crore minimum net worth certified by a chartered accountant, interoperability with every data fiduciary, and independent certification of the platform against Board-published standards. But the follow-through problem from the weekend remains unsolved: the [Data Protection Board of India still has no appointed chairperson or members](https://ppc.land/a-year-after-india-fixed-its-data-board-at-4-members-none-are-appointed) — applications were advertised in May 2026, and a Kerala High Court hearing leaves the court expecting movement by about December 2. A registration window that cannot process registrations is ceremony. The substantive obligations for data fiduciaries — notices, consent, breach reporting, penalties up to ₹250 crore per category — commence May 13, 2027, and every fiduciary's compliance plan now runs through an unstaffed chokepoint.

## 4. NHA takes ABHA to IITF 2026 as the health stack crosses 96 crore IDs [L5: Sectoral]

The National Health Authority is showcasing ABHA and the ABDM stack at the [India International Trade Fair 2026 in Delhi](https://www.facebook.com/AyushmanNHA/posts/at-iitf2026-an-abha-beneficiary-shares-their-experience-highlighting-the-role-of/1489811676575498), which opened November 14 — the outreach face of a layer that has quietly become one of India's largest identity systems: roughly [96 crore ABHA IDs and over 100 crore linked health records](https://clinicsoncloud.com/blog/abdm-integration-preventive-screening-abha), with ABDM's Scan-and-Share token service having issued 23.21 crore ABHA-linked tokens by June 2026, cutting hospital OPD waits from about an hour to minutes in studied facilities. Since Aarogya Setu 2.0 launched in June as ABDM's citizen-facing gateway and personal-health-record app, the identity-plus-records combination is the sectoral layer's most consumer-visible product — and the trade-fair presence signals NHA is pushing it beyond hospitals into general public familiarity.

**Quiet layers:** DigiLocker (L3), ONDC and GeM (L4), AgriStack (L5-agri) and CPGRAMS/eOffice (L6) saw no new national announcements in the window. The UPI MDR start date remains unannounced — which keeps the Vyapaar Saksham acceptance-infrastructure scheme and its ₹300-per-QR-code incentives formally parked, since the three-year scheme clock starts only when MDR does.
