# DPI Deep Dive — Friday | August 14, 2026

**Layer 5: Sectoral Infrastructure — ABHA, AgriStack, eCourts**

India's sectoral Digital Public Infrastructure is having a blockbuster week. Three parallel systems — health IDs, farmer registries, and digital courts — are scaling at a pace that few predicted even 18 months ago. This deep dive examines the most significant developments across ABHA, AgriStack, and eCourts from August 7–14, 2026.

---

## 1. ABHA Crosses 96 Crore: India's Health ID Enters the Real Economy

On August 13, the government released fresh data on the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) that puts ABHA — the 14-digit health identity number — at **96.43 crore registrations** as of August 12, 2026. That is not the headline number, though. What matters more is the **110 crore health records now linked to ABHA**, alongside **5.47 lakh registered health facilities** and **10.50 lakh healthcare professionals** on the platform. [^1]

This is a network effects story. ABHA has moved beyond being a static ID to becoming a digital rail on which actual clinical data flows. The linkage of over a billion health records means hospitals, diagnostics labs, and pharmacies can now retrieve patient histories across providers — something that was functionally impossible in India's fragmented healthcare system even two years ago.

In parallel, the AB-PMJAY insurance scheme — which uses ABHA as its identity backbone — has issued **45.5 crore Ayushman Cards** and covered **12.69 crore hospitalisations** worth ₹1.92 lakh crore in expenditure as of June 30, 2026. [^1] The convergence of identity (ABHA) and insurance (AB-PMJAY) is creating what the Finance Ministry has described as a "demand-side DPI" — the ability to walk into any empanelled hospital, present a digital identity, and have cashless treatment triggered automatically. India now has **1.86 lakh Arogya Mandirs** (upgraded primary health centres under PM Ayushman Bharat) operational across the country, creating a massive physical network that the ABHA digital layer sits on top of. The government described the combination as a comprehensive "digital infrastructure backbone" for the health system.

**Why it matters:** India is building the world's largest interoperable health records system without a centralised government database — by federating records across thousands of providers, all keyed to a voluntary digital identity. The DPDP Act 2023 governs this data, with consent as the legal basis for sharing. Whether that consent framework holds at scale, particularly for vulnerable patients who may not understand what they are signing, is the central governance question for ABHA in 2027.

## 2. AgriStack at 10.31 Crore Farmer IDs: The "Next UPI" Hits the Ground

This week saw AgriStack — India's farmer-centric DPI — cross a symbolic threshold: **10.31 crore Farmer IDs** created as of August 3, 2026, up from 8.48 crore just weeks prior. The government has set an 11 crore target for FY 2026-27, meaning the system is on track to achieve near-complete coverage of India's estimated 12+ crore cultivators. [^2][^3]

The scale is impressive, but the more interesting story this week is the **state-level politics of AgriStack adoption** — and the resistance emerging in procurement-heavy states.

### Odisha: AgriStack Not Mandatory for Paddy Procurement

On August 11, an inter-ministerial committee clarified that **AgriStack registration is not mandatory** for paddy procurement in Odisha — walking back concerns that the digital ID would become a gatekeeping requirement for farmers to sell their kharif produce. Food Supplies Minister Krushna Chandra Patra confirmed that farmers can complete procurement registration through existing channels without an AgriStack ID. [^4][^5]

This matters because Odisha is one of India's largest rice-producing states, and the kharif procurement season is imminent. There had been confusion and anxiety among farmers that AgriStack ID was becoming compulsory — the kind of mission creep that has historically derailed DPI adoption in agriculture (remember Aadhaar-PDS linkages that excluded genuine beneficiaries). The government's explicit clarification is a prudent step.

### Rajasthan Sets Up AgriStack Commissionerate

Meanwhile, Rajasthan's AgriStack Steering Committee this week approved the creation of an **Agristack Commissionerate** — a dedicated state-level institutional body to oversee the rollout and governance of AgriStack services. This is significant: it signals that states are not just implementing a central scheme but building their own administrative capacity to manage a new digital layer. [^6]

### The "Next UPI" Ambition

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has called AgriStack "one of the next UPI" initiatives, and the Union Budget 2026-27 announced **Bharat-VISTAAR** — a multilingual AI platform that will integrate AgriStack data with ICAR agricultural advisories to provide personalised guidance to farmers. The platform, dubbed "Bharati," will offer 24/7 crop planning, pest management, weather, and market price advisories. [^7]

**Why it matters:** AgriStack is the first large-scale application of DPI principles to agriculture anywhere in the world. The Farmer ID is designed to be the foundational layer for credit (KCC), insurance (PMFBY), procurement (MSP), and DBT — essentially the Aadhaar of Indian agriculture. But the Odisha episode shows that digital identity in a welfare context is inherently political. Making it optional for procurement is the right call, even if it slows the network effects that AgriStack's architects want.

## 3. e-Courts Phase III: 753 Crore Pages Digitised, ₹7,210 Crore Allocated

On August 7, the PIB released a comprehensive backgrounder on the **e-Courts Mission Mode Project** that quantified the digital transformation of India's judiciary with striking numbers: over **753 crore pages of court records digitised**, **1.25 crore cases e-filed**, and **4.18 crore remote hearings conducted** since inception. [^8]

The e-Courts project — now entering Phase III — has allocated **₹7,210 crore** to make courts paperless and expand digital infrastructure. As of June 30, 2026, the system operates **49 e-Sewa Kendras at High Courts** and **2,535 at District Courts**, providing assisted digital access for litigants who cannot navigate the system independently. [^8]

### AI and Interoperability

Phase III is focused on moving beyond digitisation to intelligence. The ₹7,210 crore allocation — among the largest for any digital governance project outside UPI itself — signals that the judiciary is now a first-class citizen in India's DPI architecture. The Supreme Court has initiated deployment of several AI tools:

- **SUPACE** (Supreme Court Portal for Court Efficiency): AI-assisted case summarisation and research
- **PVJ** (Public Video Journal): Automated transcription and analysis of proceedings
- **LegRAA**: AI-powered legal research and document analysis
- **ASR-SHRUTI**: Voice-to-text conversion for court proceedings [^8]

Chief Justice Surya Kant, speaking this week, said that "technology enables courts to fulfil constitutional responsibility more effectively," while cautioning that AI should assist, not replace, judicial reasoning. [^9]

### The Digital Divide Problem

However, a critical analysis published by Bar & Bench this week flagged the **uneven rise of virtual courts** — noting that digital hearings are concentrated in high courts and urban districts, while subordinate courts in rural areas remain overwhelmingly paper-based. The risk, the piece argues, is that "virtual courts risk becoming one more axis of inequality layered on top of the existing rural-urban and rich-poor divides." [^10]

**Why it matters:** e-Courts is the least glamorous but perhaps most impactful of India's sectoral DPIs. India has over 4.4 crore pending cases. Digitisation alone cannot solve backlog — that requires more judges and procedural reform — but it can eliminate the absurd delays caused by missing paper files, manual service of summons, and physical presence requirements. Phase III's ₹7,210 crore allocation suggests serious commitment, but the digital divide data demands attention.

## Cross-Layer Connections

This week's developments reveal important cross-layer linkages:

- **AgriStack ↔ ABHA**: As AgriStack scales to 10+ crore farmer IDs, the integration with ABHA health IDs could enable a unified citizen profile across health and agriculture — relevant for crop spraying safety, occupational health monitoring, and nutrition-linked scheme delivery.

- **e-Courts ↔ AgriStack**: Land records digitisation — the foundational data layer for AgriStack's Farmer ID — is also the most litigated category in Indian courts. As e-Courts digitises records and e-Courts Phase III brings AI-powered legal research, land dispute resolution could accelerate, which in turn improves the quality of AgriStack's land data.

- **All three ↔ DPDP**: The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 is the legal scaffolding for all three sectoral DPIs. AgriStack handles sensitive farmer financial and land data; ABHA manages health records; e-Courts processes case data that may include personal information. The DPDP Act's consent-based framework is being tested at scale across all three this year.

---

## Bottom Line

India's sectoral DPI is no longer aspirational. With 96 crore health IDs, 10 crore farmer IDs, and 753 crore digitised court pages, these systems are operational at population scale. The challenges have shifted from "can we build it?" to "can we govern it fairly?" — particularly around mandatory vs. optional adoption, rural digital divides, and data protection at scale.

The next 12 months will determine whether AgriStack avoids the Aadhaar-linkage exclusion traps, whether ABHA moves from identity to actual interoperable health records, and whether e-Courts Phase III reaches district courts rather than remaining a High Court phenomenon.
