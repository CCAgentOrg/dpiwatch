# DPI Brief — November 14, 2026

The trust layer took centre stage in the last 24 hours: the DPDP Act's consent-manager regime formally went live on the Rules' first anniversary — even as the Data Protection Board that must approve every applicant still has no publicly confirmed members — and the Reserve Bank consolidated its cyber-security rulebook for payment apps into a single master direction with new, directly user-visible fraud safeguards. Around them, UIDAI extended its free Aadhaar document-update window another year, and the UPI MDR start date remained stubbornly missing.

## 1. The Consent Manager era begins — with a Board that hasn't publicly staffed up [L7: Trust]

Three provisions of India's data-protection framework took effect yesterday: Section 6(9) of the DPDP Act, which requires every [consent manager to be registered with the Data Protection Board of India](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330613/20261113/india-data-protection-board-has-no-appointed-members-consent-manager-rule-takes-effect-friday.htm); Section 27(1)(d), which lets the Board inquire into breaches of registration conditions and impose penalties; and Rule 4 of the DPDP Rules, 2025, timed to start [one year after the Rules' November 13, 2025 notification](https://the420.in/data-protection-board-takes-centre-stage-as-india-advances-digital-privacy-law/). Consent managers are the registered intermediaries through which individuals can give, review, manage and withdraw data-processing consent — the plumbing the entire notice-and-consent regime rests on. The bar is deliberately high: Indian-incorporated company, ₹2 crore net worth, interoperability across all data fiduciaries, seven years of consent records, and technical designs in which the consent manager itself cannot read the personal data flowing through it.

The awkward part: as the registration requirement kicked in, legal trackers reported the Board had [no appointed chairperson or members](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330613/20261113/india-data-protection-board-has-no-appointed-members-consent-manager-rule-takes-effect-friday.htm) to approve applications. MeitY invited applications for Board posts back in May 2026, and an August parliamentary response confirmed advertisements were issued — but appointments have not been announced, and trackers report no registrations filed yet. The Supreme Court is also still seized of a petition by the Digital News Publishers Association challenging the Board's constitution process. The big compliance cliff is unchanged — [May 13, 2027](https://the420.in/data-protection-board-takes-centre-stage-as-india-advances-digital-privacy-law/), when fiduciaries' substantive obligations and penalties up to ₹250 crore per category commence — but a consent regime without a functioning regulator is a promise, not a system. Watch for Board appointments as the single most consequential trust-layer signal of the coming months.

## 2. RBI consolidates payment-app cyber rules — the 12-hour lock and remote-access block become law of the land [L2: Payments]

On November 5, the RBI issued a [single master direction consolidating cyber-security rules for non-bank payment system operators](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-cyber-security-rules-payment-companies) — the companies that run the payment surfaces you actually touch: NPCI itself, card networks, payment aggregators, and wallet issuers. Firms are tiered Large/Medium/Small with obligations scaled accordingly. The user-facing provisions are the most interesting: payment apps must now impose a **minimum 12-hour cooling period before online transactions work after a user changes their registered mobile number or email** — the classic window SIM-swap fraudsters exploit; block payments while remote-access apps are live — the tool of "digital arrest" screen-share scams; place OTPs at the end of alert messages so numbers can't be prepended; mask account numbers; enforce automatic logout and MFA on every electronic debit; and run a 24×7 staffed fraud desk with an in-app dispute button.

On the back end: incidents must be reported to RBI within six hours (alongside CERT-In obligations), annual 20-area security audits fall due June 1, and disaster-recovery backups must sit in a different seismic zone with drills every six months. The 12-hour lock will mildly inconvenience genuine number-changers; it catastrophically inconveniences fraudsters. That's the right trade.

## 3. UIDAI extends free online Aadhaar document updates to June 14, 2027 [L1: Identity]

UIDAI has pushed the free window for uploading fresh Proof of Identity and Proof of Address documents on the myAadhaar portal [out to June 14, 2027](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/want-to-update-your-aadhaar-photo-or-address-check-list-of-uidai-charges-for-biometric-and-demographic-changes-11793147181911.html) — another year-long extension of the periodic free-update campaign designed to keep Aadhaar records current without an enrolment-centre visit. The paid channel remains: ₹75 for demographic updates and ₹125 for biometric updates at centres, with email updates free through the Aadhaar App [until December 31, 2026](https://www.jagranjosh.com/general-knowledge/aadhaar-cardholders-uidai-alert-free-update-deadline-moved-to-june-2027-aadhaar-to-be-replaced-by-voter-id-biometrics-for-these-payments-key-details-1820013723-1). Combined with this week's milestone of 300 offline-verification entities on the Aadhaar App, the identity layer's story is consistent: pull record-hygiene and verification onto the resident's phone, keep the paid queue as the fallback.

## 4. The MDR date watch continues — "immediately after Deepavali" is now five days old [L2: Payments]

Diwali fell on November 8, and NPCI had promised the revised UPI MDR — 0.4% on eligible person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300, leaving [95–96% of P2M transactions untouched](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/upi-mdr-charge-0-4-fee-on-select-payments-above-%E2%82%B92000-deferred-until-after-diwali) — would begin immediately after. As of November 11, the answer remained ["there is no announcement on this at the moment"](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/two-days-past-deepavali-npci-yet-to-announce-a-date-for-upi-mdr-rollout/article71689098.ece), with unconfirmed press chatter of a January 2027 start. The Supreme Court challenge proceeds without a stay; NPCI has told the court the levy sustains the ecosystem and cannot legally be passed to customers. The newly announced Vyapaar Saksham acceptance-infrastructure scheme (covered yesterday) starts "from the date MDR is implemented" — which makes that missing date the single most consequential number in Indian payments right now.

## 5. CERT-In flags "CRAFTY FALCON" probing payment flows; RBI advisory targets fintech API security [L7: Trust + L2]

Trust and payments layers crossed this week: CERT-In identified a threat actor tracked as **CRAFTY FALCON** using ₹1 test transfers to validate fund-flow plumbing before larger strikes, and the RBI circulated a [confidential advisory urging banks to tighten API security](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-cyber-security-rules-payment-companies) for the NBFC and fintech partners they expose, citing AI-assisted attack tooling. As DPI composites deepen — every lending app, wallet and merchant platform is now stitched to bank rails via APIs — the attack surface is the seam, and regulators are finally auditing the seams.

**Quiet layers:** DigiLocker (L3), ONDC/GeM (L4 — the DigiDukaan Chintan Shivir was October 31; GeM's deputy-CEO applications close December 9), AgriStack/ABHA (L5 — the Odisha Rabi crop-insurance and A&N welfare deadlines flagged yesterday stand), and CPGRAMS/eOffice (L6) saw no new national announcements in the window. UPI volume context: 916.9 million transactions on Diwali, up 32% year-on-year, with October closing at 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore.
