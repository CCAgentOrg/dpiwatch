# DPI Brief — October 01, 2026

## Identity: Aadhaar Day 2026 — face authentication heads to the ration counter

UIDAI marked the 17th Aadhaar Day with its 'Aadhaar Samvaad' stakeholders' conference in Kolkata on September 29 — over 600 policymakers, industry leaders and technocrats, with Minister of State for Electronics & IT Jitin Prasada as chief guest — and launched five initiatives ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2316706)). The most consequential is **Face Authentication for ePDS**, jointly undertaken by UIDAI, the Department of Food and Public Distribution and NIC: a consent-based, contactless verification mode for ration beneficiaries, built for exactly the situations where fingerprint scans fail. States and Union Territories can now integrate it into their ePDS systems — if uptake follows, this quietly re-engineers the last mile of PDS identity verification at national scale.

The rest of the package widens who can build on Aadhaar at all. **SWIK Portal 2.0** ([swik.meity.gov.in](https://swik.meity.gov.in/)) gives eligible non-government entities a structured online route to seek approval for Aadhaar authentication under the Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance framework, routing proposals directly to the concerned Ministry or Department. The new **Sub-AUA/Sub-KUA (LITE) Framework** lets low-volume entities ride on an existing AUA/KUA's authentication infrastructure with real-time onboarding — no hardware or compliance stack of their own — which should lower the entry barrier for smaller service providers. A **Token Management System** formalises queue tokens at UIDAI state and regional offices, and a revamped **uidai.gov.in** goes screen-reader compatible and keyboard navigable, with BHASHINI integration supporting 13 languages now and 23 planned.

## Payments: UPI volume unmoved by MDR storm; 'No UPI Day' called off

The first hard numbers since the merchant discount rate controversy suggest consumers are not walking away from UPI. September averaged **802 million transactions a day**, about 1% more than August's 792 million on a like-for-like first-29-days comparison ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/upi-usage-holds-steady-in-september-despite-backlash-over-new-mdr-norms-126093001390_1.html)). Even in the fortnight after NPCI published the MDR framework on September 15 — a 0.4% fee on person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000, effective October 15 — daily volumes were essentially flat against August (777.6 million vs 777.9 million).

The politics moved faster than the volumes. Leading trader bodies withdrew their planned **'No UPI Day' protest for October 2** after a delegation met Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman ([Latestly](https://www.latestly.com/india/news/traders-call-off-no-upi-day-what-changed-after-meeting-fm-nirmala-sitharaman-7627247.html)). The All India Mobile Retailers Association had been the most aggressive voice — planning to drape UPI QR codes in black cloth on Gandhi Jayanti, and estimating a net monthly loss of ₹2,000–12,000 for a small retailer processing ₹5–30 lakh via UPI ([The Pioneer](https://dailypioneer.com/uploads/2026/epaper/september/delhi-english-edition-2026-09-28.pdf)). The real test of elasticity arrives October 15, when the charge actually switches on; until then, flat volumes measure anticipation, not acceptance.

## Commerce: ONDC gets a National Digital Tourism Stack; West Bengal mandates GeM

The Ministry of Tourism and ONDC have launched the **National Digital Tourism Stack**, unveiled by Union Tourism Minister Gajendra Singh Shekhawat at Bharat Mandapam on World Tourism Day, with support from the International Centre for DPI Innovation and Advancement ([NewsOnAir](https://newsonair.gov.in/government-launches-project-to-build-national-digital-public-infrastructure-for-tourism-sector)). The target: make **23,000 tourism experiences and homestays** — roughly 14,000 experiences and 9,000 homestays — discoverable, trustworthy and bookable on the network by end-2028 ([News18](https://www.news18.com/lifestyle/travel/one-ticket-one-pass-is-indias-new-tourism-stack-the-end-of-juggling-flights-hotels-and-cabs-10361121.html)). Over 1,000 experiences already transact on ONDC, including 170 ASI monuments and 29 museums. Tourism is a genuinely different stress test for ONDC beyond retail: dispersed, quality-uneven supply meeting fragmented demand, with the trust layer as the actual product.

On the procurement side, the West Bengal government has made **GeM mandatory** for purchase of goods and services ([NewsOnAir](https://newsonair.gov.in/west-bengal-govt-makes-gem-mandatory-for-procurement-of-goods-and-services)) — one more large state folding its buying into the central marketplace, extending GeM's footprint beyond central ministries and PSUs.

## Sectoral: ABHA saturation drive hits ground friction in Haryana

Haryana's push to create **ABHA IDs for all residents** is proving "an uphill task" for healthcare professionals on the ground ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/ayushman-bharat-digital-mission-faces-roadblocks-in-statewide-drive/)). It is a useful counterweight to the registry-milestone headlines: saturation-phase ABDM work falls on front-line health workers, and the last mile of ID coverage costs real staff time. As health records and scheme linkages deepen on ABHA, the quality of mass enrollments — and the consent behind them — will matter more than the count.

## Trust: TRAI regulation arms spam detection with disconnection powers

TRAI has notified the **Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026**, mandating that telecom operators act on senders their AI and analytics systems flag as spammers: re-KYC identified spam senders, disconnect violators' connections, and even file complaints with law enforcement agencies ([ET LegalWorld](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulators/trai-mandates-telcos-to-take-action-against-spammers-identified-by-their-ai-based-caller-id/134590849)). This converts TRAI's AI-based spam caller-ID stack from a labeling system into an enforcement pipeline — a detection flag can now cascade into KYC re-verification and disconnection. For consumers, the open question shifts from "can telcos detect spam?" to "will they act on it?" — the regulation now says they must.
