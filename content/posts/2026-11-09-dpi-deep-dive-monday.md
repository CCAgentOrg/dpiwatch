# DPI Deep Dive — Monday | November 09, 2026

**Focus Layer:** L1 — Identity & Authentication: UIDAI, Aadhaar, eKYC  
**Coverage Period:** November 2 – November 9, 2026

## Executive summary

The identity layer had a week of two opposing motions. In one direction, UIDAI kept scaling the consent-first, data-minimal branch of Aadhaar: 300 organisations are now registered as Offline Verification Seeking Entities, the Aadhaar App is near 7.5 crore downloads just ten months after launch, and residents have executed 4.6 crore biometric lock/unlock transactions — users actively gating their own biometrics. In the other direction, the old conditioning-of-welfare reflex resurfaced: a Union Minister proposed replacing Aadhaar with voter ID (EPIC) plus biometrics for Panchayati Raj welfare payments, while a state health society set a hard November 30 deadline for Aadhaar linkage of 1.14 lakh Ayushman cards. Meanwhile UIDAI ran a quiet regulator roadshow — workshops with IRDAI and urban co-operative bankers in Hyderabad on November 2, and a meeting with SEBI and KYC Registration Agencies in Delhi on November 3 — pushing Aadhaar authentication and eKYC deeper into insurance, co-operative banking and securities onboarding. The through-line: the infrastructure is getting better at letting residents share *less*, even as programme designers keep reaching for identity as the screening mechanism of first resort.

## Key developments

### 1. Paperless verification scales up: 300 OVSEs, and the Aadhaar App nears 7.5 crore downloads

On November 5, UIDAI and MeitY confirmed that 300 organisations have been onboarded as **Offline Verification Seeking Entities (OVSEs)** since the framework opened earlier this year, spanning hotels and hospitality, healthcare, education, events, the gig economy and consumer-facing services.[^1][^2] The OVSE route is the quiet redesign of how Aadhaar is consumed at a hotel desk or a hospital admission counter: instead of photocopies and stored Aadhaar numbers, the resident shares digitally signed, QR-verifiable identity data from the Aadhaar App, choosing complete or selected fields, with the 12-digit number not transferred to the verifying organisation and no connection to UIDAI's central database required at verification time.[^2][^3]

The accompanying app telemetry is the more revealing dataset. The Aadhaar App — launched January 28, 2026 as the successor to mAadhaar — has recorded nearly **7.5 crore downloads**, crossing 5 crore only in late August, which implies roughly 2.5 crore additions in about ten weeks.[^4][^5] Residents have used it for 86.37 lakh mobile-number updates, 22.16 lakh address updates and 40.25 lakh email updates — transactions that previously required an enrolment-centre visit — and, critically, **4.6 crore biometric lock/unlock transactions** plus 3.61 lakh full Aadhaar locks.[^1][^6]

The lock/unlock figure deserves more attention than it gets. Biometric locking is the resident-side control that suspends fingerprint, iris and face authentication until deliberately re-enabled; 4.6 crore transactions means crores of users are actively deciding when their biometrics are reachable. UIDAI frames the whole stack as aligned with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act's minimisation logic.[^1] That framing will be tested soon: the first substantive DPDP Rules compliance obligations fall due on November 13, 2026, and KYC-linked personal data flows sit squarely inside scope.[^7] The consumer-grade test for the OVSE expansion is whether verifiers genuinely store only what the consent screen showed — and whether UIDAI audits that, or merely asserts it.

### 2. UIDAI's regulator roadshow: IRDAI, urban co-operative banks, then SEBI and the KRAs — all in 48 hours

Between November 2 and 3, UIDAI ran three separate ecosystem-extension events. On November 2, it conducted a workshop at **IRDAI headquarters in Hyderabad** with chief compliance officers of insurance entities, covering Aadhaar authentication mechanisms, use cases and effective utilisation of UIDAI services.[^8] The same day, a Hyderabad workshop targeted **urban co-operative banks**, positioning Aadhaar authentication as the onboarding rail for a banking segment that has historically lagged in digital KYC.[^9] On November 3, at UIDAI's Delhi headquarters, the authority met **SEBI and its KYC Registration Agencies** to explore adoption of Aadhaar authentication and e-KYC within the securities-market KYC ecosystem.[^10]

None of this is new policy — it is distribution. SEBI has allowed market entities to use Aadhaar e-KYC as sub-KUAs since a March 2024 circular, and its January 16, 2026 consultation paper on simplifying client on-boarding and rationalising KRAs is the live reform that this meeting plugs into.[^11] On the banking side, the cooperative-bank framework developed with the Ministry of Cooperation, NABARD and NPCI already targets all 34 State Cooperative Banks and 352 District Central Cooperative Banks.[^12] UIDAI has also industrialised the plumbing: the September 16, 2026 Circular No. 9 created a simplified "Sub-AUA/Sub-KUA (LITE)" onboarding framework for government and low-volume entities, and the Global Fintech Fest releases — a native Face Authentication SDK for Android/iOS, a developer sandbox, and a BFSI ease-of-onboarding playbook — have put face authentication (over 500 crore transactions, near 200 adopting entities as of September) into ordinary app development.[^13][^14][^15]

The direction of travel is clear: Aadhaar is being positioned as the default identity rail not just for banking and telecom but for insurance distribution and securities onboarding. The consumer-protection question that should accompany every step of this expansion is failure-handling — what happens at the counter when the face match fails, and whether the exception path is as standardised as the onboarding path.

### 3. "Voter ID plus biometrics" for welfare: the Panchayati Raj Minister's proposal, and why it collides with the law

The week's sharpest identity-politics story came from the Ministry of Panchayati Raj. Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh said — first at his ministry's People's Plan Campaign workshop in Delhi, then reiterated in Gangtok on November 3 — that welfare payments under his ministry's ambit should be identified by **EPIC (voter ID) number plus biometric authentication**, with Aadhaar no longer the primary identification basis. His stated rationale: Aadhaar cards can be made "in five minutes" at "every street corner," inviting fraudulent beneficiaries.[^16]

The legal and evidentiary ground here is more complicated than the soundbite. First, the Aadhaar Act itself — Section 7 — provides that no eligible beneficiary shall be denied benefits for non-assignment of Aadhaar or authentication failure, and the government told Rajya Sabha in March 2026 that over 3,100 DBT schemes and 360+ public services run on Aadhaar-based verification, with alternate means mandatory on failure.[^17] Second, the exclusion problem the Minister is trying to solve by switching registers is well documented *within* Aadhaar: the Public Accounts Committee has heard evidence of high biometric failure rates blocking PDS rations and MGNREGA work,[^18] and analysts have argued a ~6.5% authentication failure rate is a design outcome, not a glitch — unmeasured where it is most likely, with exception handling left to local discretion.[^19] A voter-ID-plus-biometrics regime would import the same biometric failure modes while adding a second exclusion vector: the Supreme Court has already held that welfare benefits cannot be linked to voter-list exclusion, and this proposal arrives amid contested Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls.[^16] Third, and most concretely: as of November 5, **no notification, scheme list or timeline exists** — the CPI(M) politburo's November 4 statement opposing the move flagged exactly the biometric-failure exclusion risk for elderly workers and manual labourers.[^20]

What gives the proposal its weight is money at stake: the Sixteenth Finance Commission has recommended ₹4,35,236 crore in grants to 2.55 lakh panchayats for 2026–31, and Panchayati Raj ministries sit on large direct-transfer programmes.[^16] The consumer-position is straightforward to state: whichever register screens welfare, the legal duty to serve beneficiaries on alternate means when authentication fails is the part that actually prevents exclusion — and that duty does not disappear by re-labelling the identifier.

### 4. Deadline-gated linkage returns: Nagaland's November 30 Ayushman cutoff, and eKYC hardening at the LPG counter

While the national debate asked whether welfare should run on Aadhaar, states continued wiring welfare *to* Aadhaar with deadlines. The Nagaland Health Protection Society has declared Aadhaar linkage **mandatory** for Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY cards, with November 30, 2026 as the cutoff and over **1.14 lakh beneficiary cards** still unlinked; after the deadline, unlinked cards cannot be used at empanelled hospitals. Linkage channels are the CMO offices, CSCs/VLEs, card vendor agencies and empanelled hospitals, and UIDAI's Guwahati regional office is running special Aadhaar Vahan enrolment drives to support the push.[^21][^22]

Two design features are worth naming. The deadline is attached to *use* — an unlinked card effectively zeroes out a family's hospitalisation cover at the point of claim, which is a much harder failure than a delayed subsidy. And the population carrying the 1.14 lakh unlinked cards is disproportionately the one that state-level Aadhaar saturation drives exist precisely because it is hard to reach — remote villages, elderly residents with worn fingerprints, households without stable documentation. The Aadhaar Act's alternate-means obligation applies to PM-JAY verification as much as to PDS; the operational test is whether Nagaland's empanelled hospitals are equipped and instructed to admit a genuine emergency on non-Aadhaar verification after November 30, or whether the deadline silently converts Section 7's proviso into a dead letter. The same hardening pattern appeared at the cooking-gas counter, where biometric Aadhaar authentication has been mandatory since October 1 for booking subsidised LPG refills — cylinders still ship, but the subsidy stays withheld until e-KYC completes — a subsidy-identity link tightened by notification rather than debate.[^23]

### 5. The steady-state engine: 145 crore enrolments, a vigilance campaign closing, and face authentication carrying pensions

Underneath the headline events, the layer's routine machinery reported its numbers. UIDAI's public dashboard now shows **145.32 crore Aadhaar enrolments** and over **2,632 crore eKYC transactions** — roughly 26 billion — as cumulative load.[^24] The three-month preventive vigilance campaign that opened August 17 closes on November 16, having run Vigilance Awareness Week (October 26–November 1) under the theme "Probity for Prosperity" — the internal-integrity apparatus that matters more than usual in an enrolment ecosystem now being courted to onboard low-volume government entities at scale.[^25] On November 6, UIDAI was again pushing **Aadhaar face authentication as the Digital Life Certificate route for pensioners** — the Jeevan Pramaan workflow that removes fingerprint-device dependence for elderly users, running inside the Department of Pension's month-long nationwide campaign.[^26] And the authority's leadership context frames all of it: CEO Saurabh Vijay (1998-batch IAS, Maharashtra cadre) took charge on May 18, 2026, succeeding Bhuvnesh Kumar, and has been publicly highlighting face-authentication adoption as the growth curve to watch.[^27]

The steady-state story is the one with the largest compounding effect. Every crore of app-based updates, lock/unlock transactions and face-auth DLC submissions is a counter visit that didn't happen — measurable ease-of-living, and the strongest evidence base for the OVSE-and-app model described in story one. The gap between that evidence base and the unpublished one — authentication failure rates by demographic, OVSE verifier compliance audits, exclusion counters on deadline-gated linkage — remains the layer's standing accountability deficit.

## Cross-layer connections

- **Identity meets payments and markets (L1→L2):** the IRDAI/UCB/SEBI-KRA roadshow is the identity layer recruiting financial-sector distribution, building on the sub-KUA/sub-AUA frameworks and the LITE onboarding circular. Aadhaar eKYC is becoming the assumed onboarding rail across banking, insurance and securities simultaneously — which concentrates onboarding-failure risk on one authentication provider.
- **Identity meets welfare (L1→L5/L6):** the EPIC-plus-biometrics proposal and Nagaland's PM-JAY deadline are two faces of the same question — which database screens entitlements, and what happens to the person who fails the check. Section 7's alternate-means duty is the hinge; both stories test whether it is operational or ornamental.
- **Identity meets privacy (L1→L7):** the OVSE expansion and 4.6 crore biometric locks are the DPDP-alignment story made concrete, with the November 13 compliance milestone arriving days after this week's events. Lock/unlock adoption is also the clearest user-side privacy behaviour the layer has produced.
- **Electoral rolls meet welfare (L1 internal, contested):** folding EPIC into benefit screening ties welfare continuity to electoral-roll health during a politically charged SIR period — the specific combination the Supreme Court has already warned against.

## What to watch next

1. Whether the Panchayati Raj ministry issues any scheme list, notification or pilot order operationalising EPIC-plus-biometric verification — or whether the proposal stays at the press-conference stage.
2. The outcome of UIDAI's SEBI-KRA follow-through: any new circular extending sub-KUA usage across KRAs, timed against SEBI's pending KRA-rationalisation reforms.
3. Post-November 13 DPDP compliance disclosures touching Aadhaar data flows — and whether UIDAI publishes any OVSE verifier-audit results rather than framework assertions.
4. Nagaland's November 30 linkage outcome: how many of the 1.14 lakh cards get linked, and whether empanelled hospitals document non-Aadhaar admissions after the deadline.
5. The Aadhaar App crossing the next download milestone, and any published breakdown of the 4.6 crore lock/unlock transactions by user segment.
