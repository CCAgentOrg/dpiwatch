# DPI Brief — October 24, 2026

**Coverage window:** 23 October 2026, 08:00–24 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

Five developments crossed the DPI watchlist in this window: a more tightly verified rural-work attendance app that also preserves offline and exception routes; an NPCI engineering report on payment-system timeouts; a finance-council agenda tying DPI expansion to cyber resilience; a governance showcase built around surveillance and panchayat data; and a high-severity CERT-In alert for DNS software. The official releases provide the facts below; their performance and outcome claims have not been independently audited here.

## 1. NMMS upgrade combines face checks with offline and exception paths (L1 — identity; L5 — sectoral)

The Rural Development Ministry’s [23 October release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseDetail.aspx?PRID=2327556&reg=6&lang=1) says an upgraded National Mobile Monitoring System app was launched for recording attendance under VB-G RAM G. Developed with IIT Madras support, it adds device-integrity checks, liveness detection, anti-tampering protections and AI-based face verification. Crucially, the release also describes offline attendance recording and an exception mechanism for genuine workers facing authentication difficulties.

The same release says 10.997 crore Farmer IDs have been created—about 30,000 short of the 11-crore target set for December 2027. It reports nearly 46 crore person-days under the rural work scheme, about 40 crore recorded through face authentication, and says nine states have reached full face-authenticated attendance recording. Those are government-reported totals, not independent measures of accuracy. As biometric checking scales, the practical safeguard is whether offline entry and exceptions are easy to use, promptly reviewed and auditable—and whether a failed match can delay or block wages. The announcement outlines fallback processes but publishes no failure, appeal or correction rates.

## 2. NPCI says two server changes sharply reduced timeouts (L2 — payments)

In an [official post on 23 October](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2113599172263969004), NPCI said switching to Confluent Kafka and enabling Gunicorn’s `reuse_port` setting reduced daily timeouts from more than 5,000 to 0–16 while holding throughput near 20,000 transactions per second. The post frames this as supporting more than 700 million transactions a day and links to an NPCI engineering write-up.

That is a useful reliability disclosure in an ecosystem usually discussed through transaction counts alone. It is also a platform-side claim: the post does not specify the measurement period or show independent validation. Timeout reduction is not the same as a verified improvement in end-to-end payment success, failed-transaction reversals, complaints or fraud losses; those consumer-facing measures still matter.

## 3. FSDC puts DPI scale-up and cyber resilience on one agenda (cross-cutting finance DPI; L7 — trust)

At its 30th meeting on 23 October, the Financial Stability and Development Council [discussed](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2327596&lang=1&reg=3) promoting fintech innovation, asset tokenisation, digitising KYC and onboarding, and scaling the Account Aggregator and Unified Lending Interface frameworks, particularly for MSMEs. It also reviewed the financial sector’s cyber resilience strategy, protection of critical information infrastructure and Regional Rural Bank preparedness.

This was an agenda and review, not a new rule or confirmed rollout. The important test is whether expansion comes with clear consent, redress and security obligations—and whether a public implementation timetable follows. The release records no dates or measurable targets for these discussions.

## 4. DARPG showcases both mass surveillance and data-led local planning (L6 — governance)

A [DARPG webinar reported by PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2327457&lang=1&reg=3) on 23 October presented two award-winning initiatives. TRINETRA uses more than 450 AI-enabled cameras, facial recognition and predictive crowd monitoring around Ujjain’s Mahakaleshwar Temple. The project brief cited by the release reports emergency responses falling from 15–20 minutes to under five, plus missing-person cases resolved and thefts intercepted. The Panchayat Advancement Index, by contrast, applies 150 indicators and 230 data points across nine themes to around 2.6 lakh Gram Panchayats for planning and performance review.

These are showcase presentations, not new national mandates. The webinar release supplies no facial-recognition accuracy, retention period, access-control or redress details for TRINETRA. Those omissions matter: a safety case should be assessed alongside safeguards for people who are misidentified or recorded, while the PAI’s value depends on the quality and contestability of its underlying data.

## 5. CERT-In flags high-severity ISC BIND vulnerabilities (L7 — trust)

CERT-In’s [23 October vulnerability note](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES01&VLCODE=CIVN-2026-0519) rates multiple ISC BIND issues **High** severity. It warns of denial-of-service risk and possible remote code execution in affected BIND 9 releases, which could interrupt DNS resolution. Operators should identify affected versions and apply the vendor updates referenced in the advisory; this is a concrete infrastructure-maintenance item, not evidence that Indian DPI services were attacked.
