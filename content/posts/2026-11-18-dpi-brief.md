# DPI Brief — November 18, 2026

**Coverage period:** November 17–18, 2026 · **Layers touched:** L2, L4, L5, L6, L7 (plus L1/L3 in quick takes)

## Executive summary

The last 24 hours belonged to plumbing. The payments industry formally asked the finance ministry and NPCI to name a date for UPI's new merchant fee, while the operator's agentic-payments architecture — a registry of AI agents under a proposed Unified Agentic Protocol — kept moving toward a regulated debut. MeitY opened registration for DPDP Consent Managers, the first operational citizen-facing institution of India's data-protection regime. GeM logged a ₹2.5 lakh crore milestone for state-level public buying, digital health took a public-facing turn at IITF 2026, and TRAI published a quarter's worth of spam-enforcement numbers. Five items below, quick takes across the remaining layers at the end.

## Key developments

### 1. PCI presses the finance ministry and NPCI to name the MDR date (L2 — Payments)

The Payments Council of India has made multiple representations to the finance ministry and NPCI seeking an early, firm announcement of the final implementation date for the merchant discount rate (MDR) on select UPI transactions, after the rollout — originally set for October 15 — was deferred "until after Diwali" with no replacement date notified.[^1] The industry has also flagged that banks and payment companies have not received government incentive payments for processing UPI and RuPay debit-card transactions for more than 18 months, even as technology, merchant-acquisition, servicing and regulatory-compliance costs keep rising.[^1] The ask lands nine days past Diwali (November 8): the fee's design is settled — 0.4% capped at ₹300 on P2M transactions above ₹2,000, pass-through prohibited, small merchants and P2P flows untouched — but its calendar remains politically held. Every unsettled day converts the eventual cutover into more of a surprise for merchants, acquirers and PSPs alike.

### 2. AI agents move a step closer to the payment rail (L2 — Payments)

The same coverage carried the next structural shift: AI agents may soon be able to make UPI payments on a user's behalf.[^1] NPCI is developing a registry to identify, verify and monitor AI agents that transact for users, as part of a proposed Unified Agentic Protocol (UAP) under which customers could authorise agents to pay within predefined limits — beginning with routine, low-value transactions.[^2] NPCI chairman Ajay Kumar Choudhary has framed the architecture as separating intent, authorisation and settlement: agents may determine user intent, but should not independently authorise payments.[^2] A caution flag from reporting to date: Business Standard reported in September that the UAP rollout had been put on hold pending regulatory clearance and further work on user protection, liability and governance.[^2] The registry-plus-limits design is the consumer-protection story to watch — who is liable when an authorised agent pays the wrong merchant, at the wrong time, for the wrong amount, is still unwritten.

### 3. DPDP's consent-manager era opens for business (L7 — Trust)

MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan launched the online portal for registering Consent Managers under the DPDP Act on November 14, with the registration framework effective November 13 — the day the consent-manager provisions came into force, one year after the DPDP Rules were notified.[^3] Eligible Indian-incorporated companies with a minimum net worth of ₹2 crore can now apply to the Data Protection Board via dpbi.gov.in. Registered consent managers will let individuals — data principals — give, review, manage and withdraw consent across participating organisations through interoperable interfaces, while remaining accountable to the individuals they serve and without accessing the underlying personal data.[^3] This is the first operational citizen-facing institution of India's data-protection regime, and the test of it will be convenience: consent that cannot be easily reviewed or withdrawn offers little practical protection. The next milestone is May 13, 2027, when core obligations — notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, children's data, data-principal rights — become operational.[^3]

### 4. GeM: States and UTs cross ₹2.5 lakh crore in cumulative GMV (L4 — Commerce)

GeM's weekly bulletin (November 17) reported that States and Union Territories have collectively surpassed ₹2.5 lakh crore in cumulative gross merchandise value on the Government e-Marketplace — a benchmark for how deeply sub-central public buying has shifted onto the digital procurement platform.[^4] On the open-network side of the layer, ONDC-powered DigiHaat reported 175.6% year-on-year growth in festive-season non-food orders, a signal that network-based commerce is capturing festive demand well beyond the metro e-commerce majors.[^5]

### 5. Digital health takes the public floor at IITF 2026 (L5 — Sectoral)

The National Health Authority is exhibiting at the 45th India International Trade Fair (November 14–27, Bharat Mandapam), where visitors can learn about Aarogya Setu 2 and ABHA and watch live demos of creating and maintaining digital health records.[^6] The Health Ministry's pavilion spans 24 national health programmes across 39 stalls on 989 square metres, with digital health — ABHA-linked records, and AI-enabled TB screening using handheld X-ray devices — among the headline exhibits.[^7] These are deliberate demand-side demos: ABHA adoption has historically been driven from the supply side via facility onboarding, and a consumer trade fair is where the mission is now persuading citizens directly.

## Quick takes

- **L1 — Identity:** a quiet window for announcements; the identity layer's mass-market presence showed up obliquely, with NTA's JEE Main 2027 registration FAQ routing candidates through MeriPehchaan and DigiLocker for verification and document fetch (regular registration closes November 22).[^8]
- **L3 — Documents:** no platform announcements in the window; DigiLocker's verification role in JEE Main registration (above) was the layer's most visible action of the week.
- **L6 — Governance:** DARPG's capacity-building outreach continued with a November 16 interactive session for Mizoram secretariat officers covering CPGRAMS, e-Office and AI-in-governance applications;[^9] the department's daily reporting logged 7,474 grievances and 1,679 appeals disposed on November 10.[^10]
- **L7 — Trust:** TRAI's quarterly UCC enforcement report (July–September) recorded action against more than 1.24 lakh telecom resources for spam violations — 1,10,325 barred from outgoing services for 15 days on first violation and 13,777 disconnected for a year on repeat offences — alongside 374 entities blacklisted for a year;[^11] separately, TRAI's draft Responsible AI guidelines for telecom remain open for stakeholder comments until November 27.[^12]
