# DPI Brief — October 08, 2026

**Coverage window:** 7 October 2026, 08:00–8 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

Three developments cleared the date and evidence checks: a new UPI circular listing, TRAI’s Balasore network-quality assessment, and a CERT-In vulnerability note. The TRAI findings come from field tests conducted in August, though the assessment was posted this week. No new dated primary-source announcement surfaced for the other named layers in this scan; that is a coverage result, not proof that nothing changed anywhere.

## 1. NPCI adds a UPI circular on international mobile numbers

The latest comparison of NPCI’s UPI circular index, captured on 7 October, lists **UPI OC No. 161A**, titled “Addition of New Countries to the List of Permitted International Mobile Numbers in UPI.” The [NPCI UPI circulars page](https://www.npci.org.in/circulars/upi) now shows the entry. This is an L2 payments update: it concerns the eligibility list for international mobile numbers associated with UPI, rather than a change to everyday domestic transfers.

The title alone does not establish which countries were added, when the change takes effect, or which banks and apps have implemented it. NPCI’s linked [circular PDF](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/UPI_OC_161_A_Digital_fd2c628efb.pdf) returned HTTP 403 during verification, so those details could not be checked. A second new index row, OC No. 238, has only the generic title “Please get in touch with your bank” and no downloadable document; it is not possible to summarize its substance. We therefore treat OC 161A as a newly listed notice—not evidence that service is already available in newly added countries.

## 2. TRAI finds uneven signal quality on Balasore test routes

TRAI’s [7 October assessment](https://www.trai.gov.in/notifications/press-release/trai-assesses-mobile-network-quality-and-around-balasore-city-covering) reports drive tests across 345 km in Balasore and nearby Odisha locations on 18–20 August. Its [technical annexure](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_No128of2026.pdf) counts poor-signal samples against TRAI’s thresholds: 1,029 of 43,646 for Airtel, 6,758 of 41,512 for BSNL, 948 of 42,851 for Reliance Jio, and 7,002 of 42,776 for Vodafone Idea. That is roughly 2.4%, 16.3%, 2.2% and 16.4%, respectively, of the samples collected for each operator.

These figures identify sampled signal shortfalls on a defined route; they are not a measure of the share of residents without coverage, nor a nationwide ranking. The same annexure records dropped calls and periods of call silence, but the tests are a snapshot from August, not a live October measurement. The public-interest question is what operators and the Odisha service area do with the identified weak spots. The release presents test results; it does not set out a remediation deadline or a follow-up measurement date. TRAI sits in L7 Trust in this brief’s map because reliable connectivity is part of the environment in which digital services have to work.

## 3. CERT-In flags a denial-of-service flaw in Citrix gateways

CERT-In’s [CIVN-2026-0492](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES01&VLCODE=CIVN-2026-0492), issued on 7 October and rated **High**, describes a memory-overflow vulnerability in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway. CERT-In says a remote attacker could send a specially crafted request and cause denial of service. Affected releases include ADC/Gateway 14.1 before 14.1-73.41 and 13.1 before 13.1-64.28, with separate FIPS/NDcPP thresholds listed in the advisory. Administrators should check their deployed versions and apply the [Citrix security update](https://support.citrix.com/support-home/kbsearch/article?articleNumber=CTX697174). The notice describes potential impact; it does not report confirmed exploitation. This is an L7 operational-security alert, not an Aadhaar or UPI breach.
