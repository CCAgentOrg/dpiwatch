# DPI Brief — October 16, 2026

**Coverage window:** 15 October 2026, 08:00–16 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

The strongest developments this morning are not a new national DPI rule but three kinds of infrastructure evidence: a cluster of Ayurveda digital launches, a regulator’s route-level mobile-quality measurements in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and a private 5G authorisation for Mormugao Port. NPCI’s latest daily card adds a payments-volume snapshot. These announcements differ in maturity: a launched app, a test report, a licence and a transaction count should not be mistaken for proof of nationwide availability or service quality.

## 1. AIIA launches a digital and startup package for Ayurveda (L5 — sectoral)

At KAUMARACON-2026 on 15 October, the Vice-President inaugurated the [AYUSH Start-up Portal, the AIIA–iCAINE website and the Ayurveda Digital Innovation and Venture Acceleration Facility (AyuDIVA)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2323997&reg=1&lang=1). The same event launched the Vamana App and Panchakarma Assessment App. The announcement places entrepreneurship infrastructure alongside digital tools intended for clinical practice in a sector where patient records and treatment decisions can be sensitive.

The PIB release establishes that the launches were announced; it does not explain each app’s functions, what patient or practitioner data they collect, where that data is stored, or whether the tools connect to ABDM/ABHA. Nor does a launch announcement establish clinical validation or uptake. The practical follow-up is to inspect the apps’ privacy notices, intended-user guidance and evidence behind any assessment outputs before treating them as dependable health infrastructure.

## 2. TRAI’s Andaman test makes connectivity quality visible (L7 — trust)

TRAI published its independent drive-test findings for parts of South Andaman and the Port Blair–Havelock coastal route, based on testing from 17–21 August. The [official release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2323754&reg=6&lang=1) says testing covered 259.8 km of city routes, ten hotspots, a 1.1 km walk test and 52.8 km of coastal route; the [TRAI report](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_No136of2026.pdf) contains the route-level results.

On city-route voice tests, the report counted poor-signal samples at 4,734 of 37,333 for Airtel, 9,228 of 37,623 for BSNL, 5,123 of 38,739 for Reliance Jio and 15,927 of 36,434 for Vodafone Idea. Dropped calls were 5/266, 38/268, 9/285 and 8/262 respectively. These are observations on the tested routes and test days—not a ranking for every island or a forecast of each user’s experience. Still, publishing the sample counts gives residents and operators something more concrete than coverage claims alone. TRAI’s title places the tests under West Bengal LSA; the locations tested are in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, so the LSA label should not be confused with geography.

## 3. Mormugao Port gets authorisation for a private 5G network (cross-cutting infrastructure)

The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways [announced on 15 October](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2323822&reg=1&lang=1) that Mormugao Port Authority had secured Captive Non-Public Network authorisation for a private 5G network in September. PIB describes it as the first such authorisation for a government autonomous body and major port, with proposed uses including smart port operations and cargo tracking.

That is a permission milestone, not proof that the network is already operating. The release does not set out a rollout timetable, vendor, cost, security design or how access to operational data will be governed. The port is a useful test of whether private 5G can improve a specific industrial workflow; any future efficiency claim should be measured against a published baseline, not inferred from the licence itself.

## 4. NPCI’s latest UPI card is a scale measure, not a quality score (L2 — payments)

NPCI’s [daily statistics posted on 15 October](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2110675291849056467) report 835.76 million BHIM UPI transactions worth ₹1,05,163.02 crore on 14 October. The month-to-date card, through 14 October, shows 11,802.23 million transactions worth ₹15,31,668.27 crore. NPCI says the UPI figures exclude Credit Card on UPI and Credit Line on UPI accounts.

The count underscores UPI’s scale, but the card does not show failed attempts, reversals, fraud losses, transaction latency or distribution by bank and region. Those are the measures needed to judge whether a high-volume system is also dependable and fair to users. No new UPI MDR implementation date surfaced in this window; [NPCI’s 13 October notice](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2109916852667421003) says select merchant charges will begin after Diwali, with the exact date to follow.

## Seven-layer scan

- **L1 Identity:** No new UIDAI, Aadhaar or eKYC rule or service launch surfaced in the official sources checked.
- **L2 Payments:** NPCI posted 14 October system statistics; no new MDR date surfaced.
- **L3 Documents:** No new DigiLocker, API Setu or eSign change surfaced.
- **L4 Commerce:** ONDC posted a Doordarshan feature about helping small sellers reach wider markets; this was promotion, not an announced network-rule or service change ([official post](https://x.com/ONDC_Official/status/2110713743957279143)).
- **L5 Sectoral:** AIIA’s Ayurveda portal, website, facility and two apps were announced.
- **L6 Governance:** No material CPGRAMS or eOffice change surfaced.
- **L7 Trust:** TRAI published its South Andaman test results; no new CERT-In advisory or DPDP notification surfaced.

**Watch next:** Whether the AIIA tools publish clear data and clinical-governance documentation; whether the port’s private network moves from authorisation to measured operation; and whether TRAI’s route tests lead to operator remediation in poor-signal areas.
