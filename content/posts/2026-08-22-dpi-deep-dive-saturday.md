# DPI Deep Dive — Saturday | August 22, 2026


**Layer L6: Governance & Grievance — DARPG, CPGRAMS, eOffice, and India's Digital Governance Stack**

This week on the L6 Governance & Grievance layer saw India aggressively positioning its digital governance platforms as exportable models, upgrading internal grievance infrastructure, and extending governance capacity-building partnerships abroad. Five developments stand out.

---

## 1. India Exports CPGRAMS and Samadhan Didi to Malaysia

On August 18, the 2nd India–Malaysia Joint Working Group (JWG) on Public Administration and Governance Reforms convened in New Delhi, co-chaired by Puneet Yadav (Additional Secretary, DARPG) and Datuk Dr. Mohd Bakhari bin Ismail (Deputy Director General of Public Service, Malaysia).[^1]

The headline takeaway: India is now actively marketing CPGRAMS — the Centralised Public Grievance Redressal and Monitoring System — as a governance export. The Indian delegation presented CPGRAMS as "the world's largest 24x7 digital citizen interface platform for grievance redressal," and showcased Samadhan Didi, the AI-enabled voice chatbot launched on May 30, 2026, which makes the platform multilingual and more accessible through conversational AI.

Malaysia presented its own approach built around "People, Process and Technology," and both sides exchanged views on digital government, AI, cloud services, cybersecurity, blockchain, and data-driven governance. Crucially, the Malaysian side tabled the **India-Malaysia Governance Partnership: 2027–2029 Strategic Roadmap** — a three-year implementation framework covering areas under their existing MoU.

**Why this matters:** CPGRAMS has been India's internal grievance backbone for years. Exporting it — with Samadhan Didi as the AI layer — signals that DARPG views the platform not just as a domestic tool but as a replicable governance DPI. This mirrors India's approach with UPI (payments) and Aadhaar (identity), where domestic scale becomes diplomatic leverage. The question is whether CPGRAMS can function outside India's unique bureaucratic context, where pendency culture and institutional inertia are the real bottlenecks, not the technology.

---

## 2. DARPG's 20th National e-Governance Webinar: Process Re-engineering Goes Central

On August 21, DARPG organised the 20th National e-Governance Webinar (NeGW) under the theme "Government Process Re-engineering by use of technology for Digital Transformation (Central Level)."[^2] Chaired by Avanish Kumar Mishra, Joint Secretary, DARPG, the webinar attracted over 1,200 officials from central ministries, state governments, districts, and local bodies.

Two National Award-winning initiatives were showcased:

- **AgriStack (Gold Award, Central Level Initiatives, National Awards for e-Governance 2026):** Presented by Dr. Pramod Kumar Meherda, Additional Secretary, Department of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare. The platform now covers 31.36 crore plots across 648 districts, has created over 10 crore unique Farmer IDs, and completed 5.52 lakh geo-referenced village maps. Farmer IDs are being integrated with PMFBY, PM Asha, MahaDBT, and MSP Procurement — a cross-layer connection between L5 (Sectoral Infrastructure) and L6 (Governance), where identity verification (L1) feeds directly into governance service delivery.

- **e-Jagriti (Silver Award):** Presented by G. Mayil Muthu Kumaran, DDG, NIC. This unified national digital ecosystem for consumer dispute resolution connects National, State, and District Consumer Commissions into a single platform. It replaces fragmented legacy systems with end-to-end digital workflows — online filing, digital scrutiny, payment processing, virtual/hybrid hearings, automated cause lists, real-time notifications, and analytics dashboards.

**Why this matters:** The webinar series is DARPG's primary mechanism for disseminating governance innovation across India's federal structure. That e-Jagriti won a national award for process re-engineering is significant — it transforms consumer dispute resolution (a grievance redressal function squarely in L6) from a paper-bound, geography-constrained process into a digital workflow. The 1,200+ official attendance suggests real institutional uptake, not just performative broadcasting.

## 3. CIC Launches AppCoMS 2.0: RTI Grievance Infrastructure Gets an Upgrade

On August 17, the Central Information Commission (CIC) launched AppCoMS 2.0 — the upgraded Appeal and Complaint Management System — under Chief Information Commissioner Raj Kumar Goyal.[^3]

AppCoMS is the digital backbone for filing and tracking appeals and complaints under the Right to Information Act, 2005. The 2.0 upgrade aligns with the government's broader push for "transparent and citizen-centric governance through technology and digital transformation." While specific technical details of the upgrade were sparse in the initial release, the launch itself signals that the CIC is treating its digital infrastructure as a living system requiring periodic modernisation.

**Why this matters:** RTI is India's primary transparency legislation, and the CIC's ability to process appeals efficiently directly impacts citizens' ability to hold government accountable. AppCoMS sits at the intersection of L6 (Governance & Grievance) and L7 (Security, Privacy & Trust) — it's both a grievance mechanism and a transparency tool. The upgrade matters because the first-generation AppCoMS had known limitations around user experience and tracking visibility. Whether 2.0 addresses these or merely reskins the interface remains to be seen.

---

## 4. Sujalam Bharat Gets a Data Governance Policy — Rural Water Data Enters the DPI Framework

On August 20, the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (DDWS), Ministry of Jal Shakti, issued the **Policy on Data Sharing, Interoperability and Stakeholder Access** under the Sujalam Bharat Digital Public Infrastructure initiative.[^4]

The policy establishes a "secure, federated, interoperable and accountable framework" for sharing rural drinking water data across states, union territories, gram panchayats, and authorised sectoral stakeholders. It positions Sujalam Bharat as "long-term public digital infrastructure for rural water utility governance" — explicitly using the DPI framing.

Key design principles: federated data ownership (states own their data), governed and interoperable data exchange between DDWS and state/UT systems, and defined stakeholder access tiers.

**Why this matters:** Sujalam Bharat is a lesser-known but structurally important DPI layer. While it sits nominally under sectoral infrastructure (L5), the data governance policy announced this week has direct implications for L6 — how rural governance institutions (gram panchayats, state water departments) share and manage data. The federated architecture mirrors AgriStack's approach (states own registries, national layer ensures interoperability), suggesting an emerging design pattern across Indian DPIs. The critical test will be whether the data actually flows to the citizens who need it — farmers, panchayat officials, and civil society organisations monitoring water quality and availability.

## 5. Anubhav Awards and Digital Life Certificate Campaign 5: Pension Governance Goes Digital

On August 17, Dr. Jitendra Singh, Minister of State for Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions, conferred the 9th National Anubhav Awards at Vigyan Bhawan, alongside the 60th Pre-Retirement Counselling Workshop.[^5]

The event also saw the release of the **Handbook on Pension Mitra** and the **Guidelines for the Nationwide Digital Life Certificate (DLC) Campaign 5**. The Anubhav platform enables retiring and retired central government employees (across 12 public sector banks and CPSEs) to share institutional knowledge. Fifteen awardees were recognised for contributions to projects including Passport Seva Kendra operations and regulatory reform.

**Why this matters:** Pension governance is a quiet but massive part of L6. The Digital Life Certificate — which uses Aadhaar-based biometric verification (L1 Identity layer) to enable pensioners to submit life certificates without physical presence — is a textbook cross-layer DPI application. Campaign 5 suggests the government is pushing for wider adoption, but the persistent challenge remains: biometric authentication failure among elderly pensioners, particularly in rural areas where connectivity and device access are limited. The Anubhav platform itself is a knowledge-management tool — capturing retiring officials' institutional memory before it walks out the door.

## Cross-Layer Connections

This week's developments reveal several inter-layer dependencies within India's DPI stack:

- **L1 → L6:** Samadhan Didi (CPGRAMS AI chatbot) and Digital Life Certificate both depend on Aadhaar-based identity infrastructure for authentication and personalisation.
- **L3 → L6:** e-Jagriti's digital document workflows for consumer dispute resolution rely on the kind of document exchange infrastructure (DigiLocker, eSign) that L3 provides.
- **L5 → L6:** Both AgriStack and Sujalam Bharat, while sectoral in nature, are fundamentally governance platforms that determine how the state interacts with citizens — farmers and rural water users respectively. Their data governance frameworks (federated ownership, interoperable registries) are governance choices, not just technical ones.
- **L7 → L6:** The CIC's AppCoMS 2.0 and the Sujalam Bharat data policy both touch on transparency and accountability — the trust layer that determines whether citizens actually use these systems.

---

## The Bigger Picture

India's governance layer is maturing from digitisation (putting forms online) to process re-engineering (redesigning how government works around digital capabilities). The DARPG webinar explicitly named this shift. But the real test isn't technology — it's whether these platforms reduce the friction that citizens actually experience. CPGRAMS handles millions of grievances, but pendency rates remain stubborn. e-Jagriti unifies consumer commissions, but district-level infrastructure and judicial capacity vary enormously. Exporting these systems to Malaysia is good diplomatic signalling, but India's own governance challenges are far from solved.

The week's most structurally significant development may be the Sujalam Bharat data policy — not because it's flashy, but because it codifies a governance pattern (federated data, interoperable registries, defined access tiers) that is becoming the default architecture for Indian DPIs. How well this pattern serves citizens — versus serving bureaucratic data-hoarding instincts — will determine whether L6 delivers on its promise.
