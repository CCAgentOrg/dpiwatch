# DPI Brief — November 07, 2026

**Top DPI headlines for November 7, 2026** — NPCI defends the UPI merchant fee at the Supreme Court, CERT-In flags an AI-driven campaign attacking Indian fintech while issuing AI audit rules, TRAI proposes a wholesale satellite-network authorisation with direct-to-device norms, and the Centre's health-stack review leans on agentic AI as a state makes AgriStack registration mandatory for paddy farmers.

## [L2 · Payments] NPCI tells the Supreme Court: MDR stays, and merchants cannot pass it on

NPCI's counter-affidavit in the pending Supreme Court challenges to the amended Payment and Settlement Systems Act, the Finance Ministry notification and the revised UPI MDR framework doubles down on both the levy and its non-pass-through design ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/merchants-cannot-pass-on-upi-charges-to-customers-mdr-necessary-to-sustain-upi-ecosystem-npci-tells-supreme-court-557510), [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/judiciary/mdr-charges-needed-to-sustain-upi-ecosystem-merchants-cant-pass-them-to-customers-npci-tells-sc/3092196/), [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/npci-defends-mdr-charges-in-sc-says-merchants-cant-pass-on-them-to-customers/articleshow/135576316.cms)). The numbers as filed: MDR up to 0.4% (capped at ₹300) applies only to person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000 — roughly 4% of P2M volume — while P2P, transactions under ₹2,000 and small merchants with monthly inward credits up to ₹1 lakh stay free. Pass-through to customers is prohibited as a "mandatory directive", enforced contractually through PSP and TPAP agreements, with violations punishable up to termination of UPI membership. NPCI also proposes a small-merchant fund (for those with turnover up to ₹20 lakh) seeded by a 5% contribution from each participant's MDR share, and argues zero-MDR left a structural gap between operating cost and revenue that now threatens cybersecurity and fraud-prevention investment. The record-setting October — 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore, the first month past both the 25-billion and ₹30-lakh-crore marks, with daily value crossing ₹1 lakh crore ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/festive-spending-drives-record-upi-transactions-in-october/articleshow/135420426.cms)) — is the backdrop the court will weigh. The consumer watch item: a pass-through ban is only as strong as its enforcement, and there is no published complaint channel for merchants or customers to report surcharge demands. The fight is shifting from whether merchants pay to who verifies they don't.

## [L7 · Trust] "Crafty Falcon": AI-speed attacks on Indian fintech, and the audit rules that follow

CERT-In and I4C have warned of an active AI-driven campaign, dubbed Crafty Falcon, targeting NBFCs, digital lenders, payment aggregators, crypto exchanges and virtual-asset service providers ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/certin-i4c-warn-financial-firms-of-ai-driven-cyberattacks-unauthorised-fund-transfers-101793848576741.html), [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/technology/tech-news-technology/cert-in-crafty-falcon-ai-driven-hacking-campaign-fintech-10951672/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/06/threat-actor-targets-indian-fintechs-on-weekends-when-defenders-arent-watching/5308000)). The advisory names four AI toolkits — ARTEX, PentAGI, Cairn and CyberStrikeAI, at least two with prior links to China — used to map targets, generate exploits and move money through victims' own payment and disbursement APIs. Signature tactics: ₹1 test transactions before large unauthorised IMPS/NEFT transfers to mule accounts, attacks timed for weekends and holidays, victim applications rewritten to make malicious API calls look legitimate, and re-targeting of firms whose remediation was incomplete. The response architecture is arriving the same week: CERT-In's AI Security Auditing Guidelines (CISG-2026-04, November 4) set a four-tier risk framework with mandatory independent audits for moderate-to-critical systems, six-hour incident reporting, an AI bill of materials, and agentic-specific checks for prompt injection, tool misuse and unsafe delegation ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/certin-issues-ai-security-audit-guidelines-sets-4-tier-risk-framework-101793856260232.html), [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/new-cert-in-guidelines-mandate-6-hour-reporting-for-all-ai-related-cybersecurity-incidents-article-14070205.html)). RBI's parallel mandate adds API client inventories, maker-checker controls and a December 3, 2026 compliance deadline for existing integrations ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-bankers-ai-cyber-attacks-security-audits/)). The gap worth naming: the audit guidelines do not state their legal basis, applicability timeline or penalties for non-compliance — guidance without teeth is advisory until a regulator makes it a rule.

## [L7 · Trust] TRAI's satellite framework: a wholesale authorisation and direct-to-device norms

TRAI released recommendations on November 5 for a new Satellite Communication Network (SCN) provider authorisation under section 3(1)(b) of the Telecommunications Act, 2023 — a wholesale, network-as-a-service licence (up to 20 years, renewable) for entities that sell satellite capacity to telcos rather than to users directly, at an 8% AGR fee with no minimum ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/trai-proposes-new-satcom-provider-category-issues-d2d-connectivity-norms-126110502012_1.html), [ANI via The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/trai-proposes-separate-authorisation-framework-for-satcom-network-providers-backs-direct-to-device-connectivity)). Spectrum terms: FSS feeder links and FSS/MSS user links at five-year assignments extendable by two, with a ₹3,500-per-MHz annual minimum charge. The headline for consumers is direct-to-device: TRAI recommends allowing D2D satellite services on IMT bands (600–2100 MHz) on a non-interference basis without waiting for WRC-27 outcomes. For the DPI stack this is the connectivity substrate question — satellite-to-phone is the resilience path for identity verification, payments and grievance access in rural, remote and disaster-hit areas. It remains a recommendation: DoT must decide and notify, and Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite are still clearing security requirements for commercial launch ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/telecom/trai-proposes-new-satellite-authorisation-paves-way-for-direct-to-mobile-connectivity-in-india-14072154.html/amp)).

## [L5 · Sectoral] Health-stack review leans agentic; Chhattisgarh makes AgriStack registration compulsory for paddy

The Centre's two-day national review of Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY and ABDM (Kolkata, November 5–6) set a roadmap heavy on technology: demonstrations of Sandbox 2.0 with agentic AI for faster partner integration and real-time ABHA creation, alongside pushes for data analytics and automation in health delivery ([DD News](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/11/centre-reviews-ayushman-bharat-schemes-sets-roadmap-to-improve-healthcare-outcomes-and-digital-integration/)). Tripura, showcased at the same meeting, moves registration, prescriptions and diagnostics paperless from November 10 under its IHMIS ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/guwahati/tripuras-digital-healthcare-model-earns-natl-recognition/articleshow/135612644.cms)). Meanwhile Chhattisgarh has made AgriStack registration mandatory for farmers under the Krishak Unnati Yojana Kharif-2026, directing integration of Farmer IDs, Farm IDs, the Digital Crop Survey and land records, and verification of farmers who have not sold paddy ([Navbharat Times](https://navbharattimes.indiatimes.com/state/chhattisgarh/raipur/registration-in-agristack-is-mandatory-for-farmers-instructions-issued-to-verify-farmers-not-sold-paddy/articleshow/135604419.cms)). The rights question is structural: a registry built on consent becomes a condition of subsidy access the moment procurement money rides on it. That is how DPI registries harden into gatekeeping — watch which states copy this.
