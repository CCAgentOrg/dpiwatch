# DPI Deep Dive — Wednesday | November 18, 2026

**Focus Layer:** L3 — Documents & Data Exchange: DigiLocker, API Setu, eSign
**Coverage Period:** November 11 – November 18, 2026

## Executive summary

The document layer spent the week demonstrating, from four different angles, a single proposition: a document is only as trustworthy as the machinery that produced, signed, and verified it. At the signing toolchain's edge, CERT-In flagged a fresh vulnerability in eMudhra's emBridge utility — the client-side middleware that carries digital signature certificates into MCA V3, DGFT and the National Single Window System — weeks after the same company's eight weakly generated intermediate CA keys forced it out of the global TLS business.[^1] That exit crossed its own milestone this week: November 16 was the date eMudhra stopped issuing certificates from its last publicly trusted emSign root, with Microsoft beginning distrust and Chrome and Firefox refusing anything logged later.[^5][^6] At the layer's founding purpose — proving a document is real — the state itself failed spectacularly: a forged Allahabad High Court order, complete with logo, seal and a QR code that led nowhere verifiable, became the basis for blocking 95 websites, and a purported Rajasthan High Court order blocked 34 more, before a Reuters reporter's email to the court registry exposed both forgeries.[^8] In Karnataka, the digital-first marksheet policy stranded graduates for nearly two years because universities' upload software stalled while KEA and KPSC verification ran exclusively through DigiLocker — prompting the Higher Education Council to quietly decide to resume physical marksheets.[^9] And at national scale, NTA's JEE Main 2027 registration funnelled 7.86 lakh candidates through MeriPehchaan — DigiLocker, e-Pramaan or JanParichay — by midweek, with FAQs papering over the Aadhaar-mismatch and credential-confusion failure modes such mandates create.[^10][^12] The layer's scale makes all of this load-bearing: 73.53 crore registered users and over 936 crore issued documents as of September's official figures.[^13]

## Key developments

### 1. emBridge: the signing middleware gets its own security note

On November 13, CERT-In published a vulnerability note on emBridge, eMudhra's browser-side utility that lets government portals talk to the digital signature certificates and hardware tokens plugged into citizens' computers. The flaw — missing authorisation checks in emBridge's FileBrowser API endpoints — let an unauthenticated attacker on the same local network enumerate file and directory names by luring the application's local HTTPS Web API with a malicious webpage. CERT-In was careful to bound the harm: no file contents, no modification or deletion, no code execution, no access to cryptographic keys or token PINs.[^1] eMudhra had already fixed it on November 11, and its remediation is the analytically interesting part: it did not patch the authorisation logic, it removed the FileBrowser endpoints entirely — "with these endpoints removed, the file enumeration capability is no longer available through emBridge."[^1] Patched builds shipped as 5.9.4.x and later on Windows and 3.2.7.x and later on macOS, with users urged to upgrade immediately.[^2]

The significance sits in what emBridge is: the last-mile middleware of the DSC regime. Every company secretary filing on MCA V3, every exporter signing on DGFT, every applicant on the National Single Window System, and every taxpayer managing a DSC for the Income Tax Department depends on this piece of client-side software standing between a browser and a hardware token.[^1] A flaw here never makes headlines the way a root-key compromise does, but it lives closer to the user and to the government workflow than almost anything else in the trust chain. Two eMudhra security events in five weeks — eight weak intermediate CA keys disclosed in October, then a middleware authorisation gap flagged in November — have turned the company's software supply chain into a standing question for the layer's largest vendor, now under a KPMG independent review appointed October 30.[^1]

### 2. November 16: the day emSign G1 stopped being a going concern

The week's quiet milestone was a deadline set three weeks earlier finally arriving. On November 4, Chrome announced that TLS certificates chaining to `emSign Root CA - G1` whose earliest Certificate Transparency timestamp falls after November 16, 2026, 23:59:59 UTC would no longer be trusted in Chrome 153 and later; certificates logged before the cutoff keep working for their lifetime.[^4] eMudhra confirmed the next day that Mozilla had set the same date, that Apple would remove the G1 root entirely on November 30, and by its own account planned to stop all issuance under G1 by the November 16 deadline itself.[^5][^7] The InCommon academic certificate service's November 10 update added the last piece: Microsoft begins distrusting G1 certificates the same day, leaving Microsoft the only major root program without a full retirement timeline.[^6] This lands on top of the earlier cascade — the G3 root removed from Mozilla NSS on October 20 and from the Chrome Root Store by October 27, six RSA TLS issuing CAs revoked on November 7, and the board's October 22 decision to exit the global public TLS business altogether.[^3][^5][^7]

The analytical point is not that an Indian CA failed — that was October's story. It is who did the governing. Every consequential decision in this incident was made and enforced by Chrome, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft trust stores acting through CT logs, SCT cutoffs and root removals; CERT-In's advisories informed, but no Indian authority constrained. The globally traded product of a licensed Indian CA was disciplined by foreign root programs in weeks, while the domestically critical product — the DSC and eSign infrastructure running India's compliance rails — continues to operate on the assurance that it is "completely separate."[^1] That separation is asserted, documented, and plausibly true. It is also the one fact in this entire episode that no external trust store has ever tested, because no equivalent of the Chrome Root Store audits who may sign an Indian company's MCA filing. The G1 sunset date passing should prompt the domestic question the global process already answered: what is the emBridge-to-eSign equivalent of a CT-log cutoff, and who runs it?

### 3. The forged High Court order: the layer's founding failure, inside the state

Reuters' November 11 investigation exposed the week's most consequential document-integrity failure, and it ran entirely on government desktops. A purported Allahabad High Court order — dated August 14, 2026, attributed to Justice Garima Prashad, in a case titled *Sahil Singh v. Director General (DoT Cyber Law)* bearing Writ-A No. 30480 of 2026 — sat on the Department of Telecommunications' own website, where DoT relied on it to direct internet service providers on September 2 to block 95 websites for betting and fake IPL streaming. The document carried the court's logo and seal and, most remarkably, a QR code that appeared to offer verification but led to an unrelated webpage displaying the case number — decoration masquerading as authentication. The forgery surfaced only when Reuters reporter Munsif Vengattil emailed the High Court administration on October 30; the Registrar (Judicial) found no such case existed in the court's records. A purported Rajasthan High Court order, used on July 21 to block 34 more betting sites, drew the same verdict — "appears to be fraudulent," absent from the court's server. Total: 129 websites blocked on paper that does not exist. The Allahabad High Court, acting on the Chief Justice's directions, had a Joint Registrar file a complaint and UP Police registered an FIR last week under six BNS sections against unknown persons.[^8]

This is the document-exchange layer's founding premise inverted. DigiLocker, eSign and the NAD exist to make documents verifiable — provenance anchored to an issuing authority, tamper-evidence, a check that works. India's courts, the highest-authority document issuers in the system, have no equivalent: a judicial order is a PDF, and the executive branch that blocks websites on judicial authority had no machinery to check whether the judicial authority was real. The asymmetry is stark when set against the same layer's binding mandates — SEBI wrote DigiLocker into onboarding law for non-residents last week, IRDAI herds insurers onto the platform, while DoT accepted an unverifiable PDF from an unknown "advocate Kartikey Pandey" and blocked 95 domains with it.[^8] The parallel X Corp disclosure to the Delhi High Court — takedown notices to IFF and SFLC sent by an impostor posing as Delhi Police — shows the same gap operating in the other direction.[^8] Until court orders carry cryptographic provenance and the departments acting on them are required to verify it, the layer's reach into exam halls and bank onboards coexists with a command channel for speech restrictions that any forger with a logo and a QR code can operate.

### 4. Karnataka's marksheet limbo: when the issuer, not the citizen, breaks the rail

The Bangalore Mirror's November 16 report put a face on issuer-side failure. Karnataka's universities discontinued physical marksheets and shifted entirely to DigiLocker, on the logic that digital records curb malpractice and simplify verification. But several universities have failed to upload degree marksheets for nearly two years — the state's own department sources attribute the delay to software used for pushing marksheets to DigiLocker not being updated. The burden fell entirely on graduates: job offers that could not be joined, government recruitment applications that could not be filed, postgraduate admissions that could not be evidenced — because KEA and KPSC had adopted DigiLocker-based verification to automatically check candidates' qualifications, so a missing upload made a degree invisible to the state's own recruiters.[^9]

The institutional response is the telling part. The Karnataka State Higher Education Council, meeting under Higher Education Minister Basavaraj Rayareddy, reportedly decided to resume physical marksheet distribution — a partial retreat from digital-first — with the minister saying "it would be better to issue physical marksheets along with digital marksheets." University responses followed the familiar pattern: Bengaluru North University's vice-chancellor cited "technical issues" with promises of resolution; RGUHS's registrar said digital marksheets "cannot be issued due to certain technical problems."[^9] Students' demands cut to the design flaw: a fixed timeline for uploads, and retraining for colleges. The structural lesson generalises beyond Karnataka — in a verification rail where the consumer's credential exists only if an issuer's backend works, issuer negligence becomes citizen invisibility, and the verifying institutions (KEA, KPSC, private employers) never see the failure at all. DigiLocker's 5,437 document types are only as good as the 2,822 issuers behind them;[^13][^14] a platform that cannot publish per-issuer upload latency or failure rates is flying its most consequential metric blind. Dual-track physical-plus-digital, as the minister now concedes, is not nostalgia — it is the failure-mode design the platform skipped.

### 5. JEE Main 2027: MeriPehchaan becomes a national-scale funnel, with a FAQ for its failures

Registration for JEE Main 2027 Session 1 — opened October 24, closing November 22 — made MeriPehchaan login mandatory: every candidate must enter through DigiLocker, e-Pramaan or JanParichay, authenticate, and let the application pull identity and documents. By the week's live updates, 7,86,687 candidates had registered and 5,94,259 had paid fees.[^10] The funnel's friction showed up in NTA's own communications through the window: a November 13 advisory walking applicants through nationality and Aadhaar-status categories, confirming that NRIs without Aadhaar may proceed on a valid Indian passport, and warning candidates to use *their own* DigiLocker credentials and match names and dates of birth to Class 10 certificates.[^11] A FAQ released around November 15–16 then addressed the predictable wreckage — Aadhaar detail mismatches, DigiLocker authentication trouble, APAAR ID questions — even as NTA relaxed rules to allow fresh applications after form errors.[^12]

Two observations worth keeping. First, this is the document layer operating as the state's default onboarding gate for one of India's largest annual civic flows — every 17-year-old in the engineering pipeline now meets DigiLocker before they meet a college, and identity verification itself has diversified: NTA's parallel UGC NET flow accepts Aadhaar, PAN or driving licence, fetched through DigiLocker.[^10] Second, the mandate manufactures its own failure modes, and the public evidence of them is already visible — one UGC NET applicant's comment under NTA's own video describes completing a JEE form authenticated against a father's DigiLocker by mistake, then finding the credentials locked to the wrong identity with the fee paid.[^10] An SSO layer built on document fetches is only as forgiving as its disambiguation of families sharing phone numbers and Aadhaar-linked lockers. The layer's institutions treat the FAQ as the remedy; a mature layer would treat per-candidate document-match telemetry — mismatch rates, wrong-account authentications, abandonment at the auth step — as the platform's responsibility to measure and publish, not the applicant's to discover in November of an exam cycle.

## Cross-layer watch

- **L7 — Security & Trust:** the emBridge note and the G1 sunset are both, functionally, security-layer events acting on document-layer infrastructure; CERT-In's advisories now form a two-incident public record on India's dominant CA vendor.[^1][^4] MeitY's DPDP Consent Manager registration opening, covered in today's brief, will put consent artifacts — themselves documents — under a new regulator.
- **L1 — Identity:** NTA's Aadhaar-optional drift is real: passport-for-NRI and PAN/DL-via-DigiLocker alternatives mean the exam funnel no longer hard-requires Aadhaar, with DigiLocker as the reconciliation substrate for whatever identity document a candidate brings.[^11]
- **L6 — Governance:** the forged-order FIR is, at bottom, a grievance about process — the CPGRAMS layer has no channel that would have caught a fabricated judicial document before 129 blocks; verification obligations for executive action on judicial documents remain unlegislated.[^8]
- **L5 — Sectoral:** education is the layer's busiest sector this week — NAD marksheets (Karnataka), APAAR (NTA FAQ), and ABC IDs all route through the same DigiLocker substrate whose issuer-side gaps the Karnataka case exposed.[^9][^12]
