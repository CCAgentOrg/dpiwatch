# DPI Brief — November 05, 2026

**Top DPI headlines for November 5, 2026** — NPCI spins out a technology subsidiary for AI and blockchain, CERT-In publishes India's first AI security auditing guidelines with a six-hour incident clock, the Law Ministry drills ministries on paperless eFiling 3.0, the pensioners' digital life certificate drive enters its biggest-ever campaign, and Madhya Pradesh leads AgriStack with 1.2 crore farmer IDs.

## [L2 · Payments] NPCI forms NTSL, its tech arm for AI, blockchain — and non-payments DPI

NPCI announced on November 4 the formation of NPCI Tech Solutions Limited (NTSL), a wholly owned subsidiary set up with RBI approval to serve as the group's technology and innovation arm, led by chief technology officer Vishal Kanvaty as MD & CEO ([NPCI on X](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2117957238661021919), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/npci-launches-tech-arm-for-ai-blockchain-names-vishal-kanvaty-as-ceo-126110401642_1.html), [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/indias-payments-authority-npci-forms-unit-to-push-into-ai-blockchain-tech/articleshow/135538823.cms)). NTSL is NPCI's fourth wholly owned subsidiary after NIPL, NBBL and NBSL, and will build capabilities in AI, blockchain, digital financial infrastructure and global capability centre services "outside NPCI's core payments mandate." The quietly significant line: NTSL will also provide technology services for eKYC Setu, the Unified Ticketing Platform and other Digital Public Infrastructure programmes ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/npci-launches-tech-subsidiary-ntsl-to-focus-on-ai-blockchain-appoints-vishal-kanvaty-as-ceo-565717-2026-11-04)). A not-for-profit payments umbrella is therefore standing up a commercial entity whose customers include identity and transit DPI layers. Watch how the technology that runs UPI gets priced, licensed and sold once it lives in a subsidiary rather than the section-8 parent.

## [L7 · Trust] CERT-In publishes AI security auditing guidelines: four risk tiers, nine domains, six-hour reporting

On November 4, CERT-In released the Artificial Intelligence (AI) Security Auditing Guidelines — a 107-page framework for secure, ethical and auditable AI systems, developed by working group 03 of its expert groups on Indian standards for cybersecurity auditing ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/ai/artificial-intelligence/new-delhi-publishes-transparency-safety-guidelines-for-ai-11793801962574.html), [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/new-cert-in-guidelines-mandate-6-hour-reporting-for-all-ai-related-cybersecurity-incidents-article-14070205.html), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/cert-in-calls-for-ai-security-audits-six-hour-incident-reporting-by-firms-126110401936_1.html)). The framework classifies AI systems into four criticality tiers — low, moderate, high, critical — mandates audits across nine domains from data lifecycle to bias and explainability, requires AI-related security incidents meeting reportable criteria to be reported within six hours, and demands 180-day log retention with time synchronisation across AI components. The guidelines are non-binding for now, though MeitY discussions about turning standards into law are ongoing. Same day, IT Ministry sources confirmed CERT-In has gained access to Anthropic's Mythos model for cybersecurity use, to be assessed "in a measured manner" ([ETGovernment](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/technology/cert-in-gets-mythos-access-to-assess-its-use-in-measured-manner-it-ministry-sources/135523490)). The six-hour AI clock is tighter than the DPDP regime's 72-hour breach reporting — any DPI operator deploying AI (and NPCI just formed an AI arm) now audits against these domains and reports against this clock.

## [L6 · Governance] Government trains itself to file cases online: eFiling 3.0 workshop at Vigyan Bhawan

The Department of Justice, Ministry of Law and Justice, held a workshop on "eFiling 3.0: From Process to Practice" at Vigyan Bhawan on November 4 for officers of central ministries and departments, with Minister of State (Independent Charge) Arjun Ram Meghwal as chief guest and the eCommittee of the Supreme Court and NIC participating ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2332743)). The pitch, per the Secretary (Justice): eFiling is a key component of eCourts Phase III, enabling litigants to institute cases electronically from anywhere, and the Government — one of the largest litigants in the country — must align its litigation processes with the digital systems courts have adopted. The consumer stake is concrete: government cases are a huge share of court dockets, and paperless filing by the state itself is what converts eCourts investment into faster case processing rather than a parallel digital queue.

## [L1/L6 · Identity meets pensions] Digital Life Certificate Campaign 5.0: the November marathon for pensioners

DoPPW's fifth nationwide Digital Life Certificate campaign runs November 1-30, 2026 — its biggest yet, covering 2,000 cities and districts, so pensioners can submit the annual Jeevan Pramaan life certificate from home via Aadhaar-based face authentication on a smartphone camera, receiving a Pramaan ID on submission ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/save/jeevan-pramaan-how-pensioners-can-submit-life-certificate-from-home-with-face-authentication-step-by-step-guide-using-jeevan-pramaan-app/articleshow/135531130.cms), [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/latest-updates/digital-life-certificate-2026-how-pensioners-can-submit-jeevan-pramaan-using-face-authentication/article-202783)). Doorstep assistance is being routed through postmen and CSCs, and dedicated grievance officers handle submission failures. November is the make-or-break month: miss the window and pensions are withheld pending proof of life. Face authentication is the load-bearing Aadhaar use case here — which is why enrolment errors, poor lighting on worn devices, or a mismatched mobile number translate directly into a stopped pension, and why the grievance channel is as important as the app.

## [L5 · Sectoral] Madhya Pradesh leads AgriStack: 1.2 crore farmer IDs, 4 crore plots surveyed

Madhya Pradesh has emerged as the leading state in AgriStack implementation, generating more than 1.2 crore farmer IDs and completing crop surveys across over 4 crore agricultural plots ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/madhya-pradesh-leads-in-agristack-implementation)). The state also unveiled E-Vikas, a digital fertiliser distribution system it claims is a national first, extended its Bhavantar price-support scheme to soybean and mustard, and runs a dedicated farmer helpline (155253). With the Digital Crop Survey rolling across Kharif-2026 and states from Uttarakhand to J&K's Udhampur holding review meetings this week, the farmer registry is quietly becoming the substrate on which PM-KISAN, KCC and crop insurance verification will run — the consumer question remains who bears the cost of registration errors at the last mile.
