# DPI Deep Dive — Monday | November 23, 2026

**Focus Layer:** L1 — Identity & Authentication: UIDAI, Aadhaar, eKYC
**Coverage Period:** November 16 – November 23, 2026

## Executive summary

The identity layer's week turned on a single question: what happens when an authentication *default* becomes a gate? On November 16, a Supreme Court bench headed by Justice J.B. Pardiwala warned of contempt proceedings if the Union government and its authorities failed to file a detailed reply to an application showing that visually impaired citizens cannot independently complete Aadhaar face authentication — the modality on which many e-KYC flows now solely rely — with the petitioner annexing his own October 2026 e-KYC rejection by a private bank as evidence.[^1] The same day, the Ministry of Cooperation notified S.O. 6259(E), authorising the National Urban Cooperative Finance and Development Corporation (NUCFDC) to run voluntary Aadhaar authentication for employees, visitors and officials — the administrative companion to UIDAI's cooperative-banking framework that puts all 34 State Cooperative Banks, and through them 352 District Central Cooperative Banks, onto the authentication estate.[^4][^5] Midweek, UIDAI's Guwahati regional office and the Assam government launched "Mission Sankalp" to push mandatory biometric updates in schools, against a disclosed national backlog of roughly 17 crore Aadhaar numbers with pending childhood updates and a free-update window extended to September 30, 2027.[^7][^8][^9] The exam calendar supplied the deadline pressure: NTA advised JEE Main 2027 and other aspirants to refresh stale biometrics, is preparing to switch exam-centre verification from fingerprints to face recognition, and SSC told CPO candidates to keep their biometrics *unlocked* through the November 23–28 exam window.[^10][^11][^12] Beneath it all sits the modality shift of the year: face authentication — past 500 crore cumulative transactions and now shipped as a native SDK — is becoming Aadhaar's default, which is precisely why its accessibility failures have become a rights question argued before the country's highest court.[^14]

## Key developments

### 1. "The face cannot blink": Supreme Court warns of contempt over Aadhaar face-authentication exclusion

The week's most consequential development came on November 16, when a bench headed by Justice J.B. Pardiwala warned of initiating contempt action if the Union government and authorities did not give a "detailed and thorough" response to an application by advocate Amar Jain — himself a party to the underlying case — documenting that blind and low-vision users cannot independently complete Aadhaar face authentication.[^1] The mechanics of the exclusion are specific: the new Aadhaar app's face-authentication interface requires "eyes to be attentive and looking into the frame," which a person with blindness cannot satisfy. Because e-KYC flows increasingly accept *only* face authentication, the failure cascades — Jain annexed what he described as the latest rejection of his e-KYC for a credit card by a private sector bank in October 2026.[^1] His application also flags the systemic gaps around the technology: no grievance mechanism for accessibility complaints against apps and websites, and accessibility audits, nodal officers, human review of rejected KYC applications, and compliance monitoring "not visible" across regulated entities.[^1]

The application lands in the shadow of *Pragya Prasun v. Union of India*, the Supreme Court's April 2025 judgment that recognised digital access as part of the right to life under Article 21 and issued comprehensive directions to ease digital access for persons with disabilities.[^1][^3] Jain has asked the court to direct the RBI, MeitY, TRAI, DoT, SEBI, PFRDA and IRDAI to fully implement that judgment, and sought a clarification from MeitY on how it will handle accessibility complaints about unregulated digital platforms.[^1] A separate application challenges the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas's mandate — announced via a September 2026 press release — making biometric Aadhaar authentication compulsory for subsidised domestic LPG refills from October 1, 2026; per the government's own figures, 27.43 crore consumers (89.9%) had completed authentication before the deadline, which means the mandate's residual burden falls on precisely the last tenth, including those who cannot complete it.[^2] Jain's interim ask is pragmatic: until an accessible, unassisted mode exists, a declaration supported by a UDID card or disability certificate, sent by email, IVRS or letter, should suffice — and no disabled consumer should be denied LPG at the regulated price or the subsidy.[^1] The court has sought responses by December 16 and scheduled the next hearing in four weeks.[^2]

The analytical point is that India switched its identity default modality without shipping an accessible fallback. Face authentication is a genuine accessibility *win* for many users — it replaced fingerprint hardware dependency and powers 60% of pensioners' digital life certificates — but for a minority it is a hard lockout, and every service that collapses its verification flow onto face-only e-KYC inherits that lockout.[^13][^14] The RBI's clarification on eye-blinking addressed video KYC, not Aadhaar face authentication, and the gap between the two regimes is where citizens are falling through.[^3] *Pragya Prasun* made digital access a constitutional imperative; the contempt warning suggests the Court is now moving from declaring rights to enforcing replies. Watch December 16: the *content* of the government's answer — whether it commits to an accessible authentication mode or defends the mandate — will define the identity layer's accessibility trajectory for 2027.

### 2. NUCFDC notification: cooperative finance gets its own slice of the authentication estate

On November 16, the Ministry of Cooperation issued Notification S.O. 6259(E), authorising NUCFDC — the umbrella organisation of the urban cooperative banking sector — to undertake Aadhaar authentication under Rule 5 of the Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Rules, 2020, read with Section 4(4)(b)(ii) of the Aadhaar Act, following MeitY approval in consultation with UIDAI (UIDAI's letter dates to October 29, 2026).[^4] The permitted purposes are strictly administrative: one-time registration of employees and outsourced personnel under an attendance management system, identification of visitors during registration and visitor-card issuance, and establishing the identity of NUCFDC officials to prevent impersonation — using Yes/No and/or eKYC authentication facilities.[^4]

The notification arrives with the standard safeguard architecture on its face: consent is mandatory, authentication is voluntary, and the agency must inform holders of alternate identification — PAN card, voter ID, photo passbook, ration card, passport, driving licence, domicile certificate — and cannot deny any service for refusing or being unable to authenticate.[^4] That last clause deserves close reading against story 1: the Supreme Court is currently examining a case where alternative modes existed on paper but the service flow in practice did not honour them. Voluntary-by-notification is only as good as its enforcement data, and neither the notification nor the "good governance" rules framework publishes any.

The context matters too. This is the administrative layer of a deliberate two-track expansion of Aadhaar into cooperative banking. In September 2025, UIDAI rolled out a framework — developed with the Ministry of Cooperation, NABARD, NPCI and the banks themselves — to onboard all 34 State Cooperative Banks as master Authentication User Agencies and e-KYC User Agencies, with 352 District Central Cooperative Banks riding on their parent StCBs' IT infrastructure rather than building their own.[^5][^6] Circular 10 of 2025 codified the mechanics.[^6] A year on, the NUCFDC notification extends the same estate from customer onboarding (eKYC) to workforce and premises administration (attendance, visitors). The trajectory is familiar from every other DPI layer: infrastructure first, administrative uses second. What's missing is the usage transparency — UIDAI publishes transaction volumes for eKYC and authentication overall but nothing purpose-wise, so the public cannot see how much "good governance" authentication is actually happening, or whether the voluntary-alternative safeguards are being honoured at NUCFDC counters.

### 3. Mission Sankalp and the 17-crore backlog: childhood biometrics are the layer's maintenance problem

On November 17, UIDAI's Guwahati regional office, coordinating with the Assam government, launched "Mission Sankalp," a special campaign to complete Mandatory Biometric Updates (MBU) for school students, with enrolment camps in schools and additional enrolment kits deployed to handle the load.[^7] The campaign is a state-level instance of a national push: UIDAI is running regular biometric-update camps in schools nationwide, has made MBU free for all children aged 5–17 until September 30, 2027, and spent the week of November 17–22 publicly urging parents through regional offices and social channels not to miss the milestone windows — ages 5–7 and 15–17 — beyond which an un-updated Aadhaar number may face deactivation.[^8][^16]

The scale of the pending problem is disclosed in UIDAI's own collaboration with the Department of School Education and Literacy: approximately 17 crore Aadhaar numbers have the mandatory biometric update pending, which is why MBU status is being surfaced on the UDISE+ school-data application so education departments can chase it.[^9] The completed-side number provides the denominator check: the special drive begun in September 2025 has crossed 2 crore school-child MBUs across 1.56 lakh schools — roughly an eighth of the identified backlog in about fourteen months.[^10]

Childhood biometric decay is structurally unique in India's DPI. Every other identity asset, once issued, is durable; a child's fingerprints, iris and face are not, which means Aadhaar embeds two compulsory re-verification events into the life of every enrolled child — and attaches a deactivation threat if families miss the windows. The maintenance burden falls on parents with the least schedule flexibility, and the state's answer — school camps, UDISE+ integration, free windows — is a genuine piece of delivery innovation. But the deactivation penalty converts a data-quality problem into a compliance problem for minors, and the pending count shows how far the system still is from closure. The exam season (next story) is where unpaid maintenance gets invoiced.

### 4. Exam season: when authentication health becomes an admissions prerequisite

The week made explicit what has been quietly true for a while: national examination bodies are now downstream consumers of UIDAI's data hygiene, and they have started issuing their own advisories. NTA told JEE Main 2027, NEET UG, CUET and other aspirants to check and complete pending biometric updates if they enrolled before turning 5 or 15, since childhood fingerprints and iris records frequently fail to match at exam centres; Aadhaar-based authentication runs through NTA's process from registration via DigiLocker to identity verification at centres.[^10] More consequentially, NTA is accelerating preparations to make facial biometric verification the security check at all its examinations, replacing fingerprint verification outright — a formal notification is still pending, but the direction is set.[^11] And the Staff Selection Commission, in an advisory dated November 17, told CPO candidates (exams November 23–28) to keep their Aadhaar biometrics *unlocked* — an instruction that only exists because UIDAI's own security feature, biometric locking, collides with high-stakes exam scheduling.[^12]

Two things follow. First, the identity layer's maintenance debt now has a hard external deadline: a 17-year-old with stale biometrics does not face an abstract risk but a specific JEE or SSC centre. This de facto turns NTA and SSC into UIDAI's collections department — effective, but a strange division of labour that no rule visualised. Second, the shift of exam verification from fingerprints to face is the modality story again: face authentication is the escape hatch for stale fingerprints, which is efficient — and which routes every exam aspirant in the country through the very modality whose accessibility is contested before the Supreme Court. A candidate with facial paralysis, a visually impaired candidate, or any face-authentication edge case now has an exam-centre problem, not just a banking problem. The exam bodies' advisories cover stale biometrics; none of them, this week, covered inaccessible authentication.

### 5. Face authentication by the numbers: the default modality keeps compounding

The week's developments are best read against the volume curve. UIDAI's most recent monthly disclosure (November 2025) recorded 231 crore authentication transactions — then the highest of FY26 — with 28.29 crore face-authentication transactions, more than double November 2024's 12.04 crore, and 47.19 crore eKYC transactions, up over 24% year-on-year; nearly 60% of pensioners' digital life certificates that month were generated via face authentication.[^13] By September 2026, cumulative face-authentication transactions had crossed 500 crore, adopted by nearly 200 entities across government, banks, NBFCs, telecom and capital markets; UIDAI used Global Fintech Fest 2026 to ship a native Android/iOS SDK — embedding face authentication directly into apps, ending the separate FaceRD app hop — and a developer sandbox, while the Aadhaar app crossed 40 million downloads as of July 2026.[^14] The cumulative estate on UIDAI's homepage this week reads: 145.69 crore enrolments, 2,752.41 crore eKYC transactions, 2.49 crore registered devices.[^15]

The platformisation is the quiet structural shift. A native SDK plus sandbox means every fintech app can make Aadhaar face authentication an in-app step rather than an OS-level detour — friction falls, adoption compounds, and the modality's default status hardens by a thousand integrations rather than one mandate. That is efficient architecture and it is exactly why story 1 matters: each new integration is another surface that inherits face authentication's exclusion boundary. Volume growth and accessibility litigation are not separate stories about one technology; they are the same story told from the server side and the lockout side.

## Cross-layer connections

The week's identity-layer news is threaded through every other DPI layer. **L2 (Payments & Banking):** the NUCFDC notification and the StCB/DCCB onboarding framework extend Aadhaar authentication deeper into last-mile banking, while Jain's rejected credit-card e-KYC shows the layer's KYC rail failing disabled customers in practice.[^1][^4][^5] **L5 (Sectoral):** exam bodies' biometric advisories are education-layer dependencies on identity-layer maintenance; pensioners' digital life certificates remain the flagship face-auth use case.[^10][^13] **L7 (Security, Privacy & Trust):** the contempt warning operationalises *Pragya Prasun*'s Article 21 digital-access holding, and the "good governance" notification route is where purpose expansion happens with limited usage transparency — both are trust-layer questions wearing identity-layer clothes.[^1][^3][^4] **Welfare:** the LPG biometric mandate from October 1 is the highest-stakes fusion of authentication default with subsidy access — 89.9% compliance achieved means the remaining tenth is where exclusion concentrates.[^2]

## What to watch

Three dated triggers. **December 16, 2026** — Union government's response deadline in the Supreme Court face-authentication matter; the content of the reply will signal whether an accessible authentication mode is coming.[^2] **Rolling** — pace of school-camp MBU drives against the ~17-crore pending backlog and the September 30, 2027 free-window expiry; watch whether deactivation threats are actually enforced at scale.[^7][^8][^9] **FY27** — NTA's formal notification on face-only exam verification, and cooperative banks' eKYC go-live numbers under the 2025 framework, which will show whether the two-track expansion (infrastructure + administration) is delivering or stalling.[^5][^11]
