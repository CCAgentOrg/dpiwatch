# DPI Brief — October 23, 2026

**Coverage window:** 22 October 2026, 08:00–23 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

Four developments stood out: a reported MeitY investigation into eMudhra’s certificate-key incident, a new Aadhaar-enabled but explicitly voluntary Telangana property portal flow, a district-level digital fertilizer booking rollout, and NPCI’s latest payment-rail snapshot. The first three are news reports rather than direct official pages; that distinction is deliberate. The quieter layers are listed at the end rather than padded with older announcements.

## 1. eMudhra exits global TLS certificates as government probes key failure (L7 — trust)

Updated late on 22 October, *The Hindu BusinessLine* [reported](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/info-tech/emudhra-issue-government-looking-at-domestic-alternatives/article71610986.ece) two consequential steps after the compromise of private keys for eight intermediate certificates: eMudhra’s board decided to withdraw voluntarily from the global SSL/TLS certificate business, and MeitY formed a special investigating team. The report says the team is led by the Deputy Controller of the Controller of Certifying Authorities and includes officials from CERT-In, UIDAI and NPCI. It also reports that eMudhra said all eight certificates had been revoked on 19 October and none had issued customer certificates.

This is a reported government response, not a public investigation order or findings report located in this scan. The distinction matters: certificate trust underpins access to government sites and signed software, while the underlying incident was in the international web-certificate operation. The report says India’s regulated Digital Signature Certificate and eSign services remain unaffected; that is not the same as saying every website using eMudhra technology is unaffected. The next useful disclosure is a dated account of affected certificate chains, replacement and revocation status, and any disruption—not just the company’s exit from one business line.

## 2. Telangana authorises Aadhaar authentication for TDR portal, with an opt-out (L1 — identity)

*The Hindu* [reported](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/telangana/aadhaar-authentication-introduced-on-tdr-portal/article71612273.ece) that an order issued on 22 October authorises the Municipal Administration and Urban Development Department to use Aadhaar authentication on the online Transferable Development Rights portal. The covered actions include registration and login, filing TDR applications, linking them to property records, and authenticating DTCP and GHMC officials for administration.

The reported order makes authentication voluntary, requires the holder’s consent, bars denial of service to anyone who refuses or cannot authenticate, and requires acceptance of alternative government photo IDs, including passports, voter IDs, driving licences and PAN cards. Those are important safeguards, not implementation proof. The report does not link the order text; residents and builders will need the portal to make the non-Aadhaar route as visible and workable as the Aadhaar route, with a clear remedy when either method fails.

## 3. FFS fertilizer booking begins in Hingoli (L5 — sectoral; L1 — identity)

A 22 October *Sakal/eSakal* [report](https://www.esakal.com/marathwada/aurangabad/ffs-fertilizer-booking) says the central government’s Framework for Fertilizer Sale (FFS) system was implemented from that day in Hingoli district. It describes an app through which farmers can check nearby stock and pre-book subsidised chemical fertilizer. The reported flow accepts a Farmer ID or Aadhaar number, verifies land area, records crop choice, and lets the farmer reserve a quantity with a nearby dealer; collection is to be completed within three days using the booking number. The district agriculture superintendent urged local farmers and retailers to adopt it.

This is a district implementation report, not evidence of a nationwide launch or a newly verified AgriStack integration. A booking flow can improve visibility and reduce repeat trips, but it also makes identity, land and crop records part of access to an essential input. The public test is whether farmers without a smartphone, reliable connectivity or successful authentication can still buy their permitted quantity, and whether dealer stock, failed bookings and complaints are published.

## 4. NPCI’s 21 October snapshot shows UPI at ₹89,801 crore (L2 — payments)

NPCI’s official [daily statistics post](https://x.com/NPCI_NPCI/status/2113251501615522263), published on 22 October, reports 21 October activity: 778.43 million UPI transactions worth ₹89,801.43 crore; 2.71 million AePS transactions worth ₹642.84 crore; 10.88 million IMPS transactions worth ₹22,747.74 crore; and 10.92 million NETC transactions worth ₹215.27 crore. NPCI’s chart notes that its UPI figure excludes credit-card-on-UPI and credit-line-on-UPI accounts. These are throughput figures, not evidence of successful completion, low failure rates, timely reversals or low fraud losses.
