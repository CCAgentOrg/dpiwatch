# DPI Brief — November 23, 2026

A quiet Sunday kept the trust and open-finance layers busy while the commerce network reached for tribal India. India's cyber emergency team published its most consequential payments advisory of the month, the RBI's technology arm quietly yanked the account aggregator interoperability deadline forward by a month, bank-side security hardening started showing up as delayed fintech payouts, and a deputy governor told bank boards that algorithmic decisions are now their problem. Here is what matters.

## 1. CERT-In flags abuse of business payment workflows — and AI-driven attackers

The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team issued [critical advisory CIAD-2026-0062 on 20 November](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/cert-in-payment-workflow-advisory), warning that attackers are no longer just exploiting payment APIs — they are altering business records and approval chains inside otherwise legitimate systems, then letting normal payment runs push unauthorised transfers through. Because each step looks genuine in isolation, the fraud evades application-level controls. Crucially for DPI, the targets now extend [beyond banks to technology service providers handling government-linked payments and vendor payout platforms](https://www.moneylife.in/article/certin-warns-of-payment-fraud-and-aidriven-cyberattacks-in-india/82243.html) — exactly the intermediaries that move DBT and partner payouts. The advisory also catalogues [AI-driven "agentic" offensive frameworks](https://rmaindia.org/cert-in-flags-unauthorised-transactions-at-financial-sector-service-providers-payment-workflow-fraud-ai-driven-cyberattacks-and-lessons-for-banks), the third such warning in six weeks after [CIAD-2026-0046 on payment-disbursement APIs](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES02&VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0046) (9 October) and the CRAFTY FALCON advisory (4 November). Prescriptions: independently verify beneficiary records, restrict who can approve payments, and keep to the six-hour incident reporting clock.

## 2. Account aggregator interoperability deadline yanked forward to 1 December

ReBIT, the RBI's technology arm, [published version 1.1.0 of the account aggregator interoperability API specifications on 20 November](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/account-aggregator-interoperability-deadline) and moved the mandatory adoption deadline from 31 December to **1 December** — leaving AAs, banks and lenders roughly ten days to finish system changes and compliance reporting. The [RBI's 7 October policy statement](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/account-aggregator-interoperability-deadline) set the destination; Sahamati, the network's SRO, has spent November building the road — a [federated gateway design document (13 November)](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/account-aggregator-interoperability-deadline), customer-experience guidelines (17 November) and redirection guidelines v2.0 for consent journeys (19 November), with its new pricing model also landing 1 December. The fix targets a real gap: Sahamati CEO B.G. Mahesh has said 40–50% of institutions work with only two or three of the 17 active AAs. The stakes are scale — 352.19 million accounts linked and 595.11 million consents fulfilled cumulatively as of 30 September 2026 — plus a new frontier: SEBI-regulated depositories will add bank deposit data to consolidated account statements through AAs. One deadline now governs the entire open-finance handshake: next Tuesday.

## 3. Bank API hardening arrives, and fintech payouts feel it

[Inc42 reported on 21 November](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/yes-bank-api-security-fintech) that Yes Bank's "enhanced precautionary measures" — API changes against AI-driven attacks — are delaying settlements for some fintechs and payment service providers. Sources tied the timing to RBI cyber directions issued after the suspected SBI data exposure, and they follow the [RBI's 16 November advisory on API governance good practices](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/rbi-api-security-advisory/) for banks and NBFCs. Yes Bank has not commented, and none of the affected firms has named it publicly. But the ecosystem strain is visible: Coinbase India, CoinDCX and ZebPay have reported slower or paused INR withdrawals, [Razorpay delayed merchant settlements on 20 November](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/yes-bank-api-security-fintech) over a partner-bank technical issue, and City Union Bank shut an NEFT/RTGS gateway after an unauthorised transfer on 16 November. The consumer takeaway: security upgrades are necessary, but they are landing with little notice on the payout rails small businesses depend on.

## 4. RBI's Murmu: bank boards must own algorithmic decisions

Speaking in Mumbai on 19 November, [Deputy Governor S.C. Murmu told bank directors](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/rbi-murmu-bank-boards-ai) they "must know how much consequential decisions have been handed to algorithms" — and whether those decisions are "explainable, fair and accountable." With RBI's revised governance directions in force since 1 October freeing board time from checklists, his ask is to spend it on strategy and technology risk. Two lines cut especially close to DPI's vendor-heavy plumbing: outsourcing an activity does not outsource responsibility, and "risks now travel through contracts, not just through balance sheets." He also tied mis-selling to incentive design — [it "begins with a sales target and a variable pay scheme"](https://newsonair.gov.in/banks-must-focus-on-building-strategy-and-overcoming-technology-risks-says-rbi-deputy-governor/), not the branch counter — ahead of the April 2027 ECL provisioning shift. [Business Standard has the full remarks](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/banking/mis-selling-starts-with-sales-targets-not-branch-counters-rbi-dg-murmu-126111901077_1.html).

## 5. GeM signs MoU to bring tribal entrepreneurs into public procurement

The Government e-Marketplace signed an MoU with the Tribal Indian Chamber of Commerce and Industry (TICCI) on 20 November, [announced by the Ministry of Commerce & Industry on 21 November](https://utkarsh.com/current-affairs/national/agreements-n-mou/gem-signs-mou-with-ticci-to-empower-tribal-entrepreneurs), to boost tribal-owned businesses' participation in government procurement through [training, outreach and onboarding support](https://www.awazthevoice.in/business-news/gem-tribal-chamber-join-hands-to-boost-government-contracts-71280.html). The stated hurdles — low digital awareness, complex registration, unfamiliar tendering rules — are the standard onboarding friction of any DPI rail; GeM, ten years old this August, is betting a chamber-led bridge solves what self-serve registration did not.

## Also noted

- **UIDAI keeps pushing free children's biometric updates**: mandatory biometric updates for ages 5–17 remain [free until 30 September 2027](https://punjabnewsline.com/news/childrens-aadhaar-update-goes-free-uidai-urges-parents-not-to-miss-the-deadline), with school camps doing the legwork — the identity layer's quiet race against stale fingerprints (L1).
- **DPI accessibility on the agenda**: an [India Internet Governance Forum workshop on 25 November](https://x.com/ISOC_Live/status/2124149092880118181) asks whether persons with disabilities, older adults and language-minority users can actually use India's DPI for payments, identity and public services independently (L6/cross-cutting).

**Watch Tuesday:** 1 December is the day account aggregator interoperability, AA pricing and (eventually) the UPI MDR clock all converge — three dates, one week apart in the calendar of DPI's most consequential quiet change.
