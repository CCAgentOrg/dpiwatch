# DPI Brief — November 26, 2026

Markets, money and malls all moved at once today: SEBI rebuilt securities KYC around a central registry, India's consent-based data-sharing network finally received its first standard price list, ONDC declared war on the dark store, and the biggest UPI app started walking toward the stock market. Here is what matters across the DPI stack.

## 1. SEBI re-architects market KYC: one registry, fewer repeats

On 25 November SEBI [issued a circular](https://www.thehindu.com/business/sebi-eases-kyc-norms-simplifies-client-onboarding/article71743597.ece) overhauling the KYC framework for securities market investors, with the changes effective 24 December 2026. Supplementary client information that is common across intermediaries — income range, occupation, politically-exposed-person status, FATCA/CRS declarations, CKYC ID, DIGIPIN and document validity — will now be centralised at the KYC Registration Agency (KRA) level, so an investor opening a second demat or trading account no longer re-submits the same details. KRAs will tag individual KYC attributes as *validated* or *non-validated*, and intermediaries must seek additional information only for attributes that have not been validated. Mobile-number verification is simplified and documentation for name and address changes is eased; KRAs have 30 days to issue an implementation SOP ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/sebi-simplifies-kyc-norms-streamlines-investor-onboarding/article71743649.ece)). The quiet DPI story inside this: DIGIPIN — India Post's digital address grid — is being pulled into market KYC, the same pattern DigiLocker set when document re-use replaced re-submission. One registry, many consumers of it.

## 2. The Account Aggregator network gets a price list

[Sahamati](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/how-sahamatis-new-account-aggregator-fees-will-work-impact-borrower-assessments/136172187), the self-regulatory organisation for India's Account Aggregator (AA) network, today detailed a standardised pricing framework that turns three years of deliberation into a commercial model: financial information users (banks, NBFCs, fintech lenders) will pay ₹5.88–6.55 for each successful seven-month bank statement fetch and ₹8.64–9.60 for a thirteen-month statement, per account, excluding GST. The fee splits three ways — the bank holding the data earns ₹2.80 on the seven-month benchmark, the account aggregator collects a "DPI utility fee" of ₹2.68–3.35, and Sahamati takes 40 paise as an ecosystem management fee. Lenders receive a single bill from the aggregator; customers are never charged directly and consent remains mandatory before any fetch. Rollout happens through a common settlement agreement from 1 December — deferred from 1 November after industry pushback, per [Sahamati's 23 November circular](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/how-sahamatis-new-account-aggregator-fees-will-work-impact-borrower-assessments/136172187) — with rates reviewed after six months. The consumer-safety question: in small-ticket lending, where lenders screen two or three bank accounts per applicant and several applicants per disbursed loan, one ₹10,000–50,000 loan could quietly carry ₹23–34 of new data-fetch cost including GST. Fees are how free infrastructure grows up; watch that the cost lands on lenders' margins, not borrowers' effective interest rates.

## 3. ONDC takes on quick commerce with NearU — no dark stores required

ONDC will pilot **NearU** in parts of Delhi this December as a proof of concept, [reports Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/ondc-takes-on-quick-commerce-with-nearu-bets-on-local-stores-for-delivery-126112500939_1.html): a store-based alternative to the dark-store model of quick commerce. A consumer orders from a "NearU" catalogue; the order is broadcast to retail stores within a 4–5 km radius; any store stocking the product fulfils it — itself or through ONDC's logistics partners. Targets: four-hour delivery of mobile phones (sellers are already aligned for two hours), next-day for other electronics like air purifiers. Around 70–80 brick-and-mortar stores, including electronics chain Hotspot, are being onboarded, with talks on with apps such as Gadgets360. Phase two adds a bidding model — a customer's bid opens after 30 minutes and any willing retailer confirms it. The structural difference from Zepto-and-Blinkit style q-commerce: no dedicated dark stores, no incremental inventory, just existing neighbourhood shops gaining an online channel. ONDC has crossed 500 million transactions to date; electronics-first NearU is its most direct attack yet on the ten-minute incumbents. The proof will be in service quality — four hours is quick, but it is not ten minutes.

## 4. PhonePe heads for the IPO window carrying the UPI crown

Walmart-backed PhonePe [restarted its IPO process](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/walmart-backed-phonepe-restarts-ipo-process-eyes-up-15-billion-valuation-market-2026-11-25), eyeing an up-to-$15 billion valuation and a potential FY27 listing ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/ipo/phonepe-prepares-to-restart-ipo-process-amid-improving-market-conditions-126112500725_1.html)). The asset it brings to market: over 735 million registered users, 50 million+ merchants, and roughly 11.4 billion of India's 25.4 billion October UPI transactions — about 45% of the rail. FY26 revenue rose 11% to ₹7,920 crore while net loss widened 62% to ₹2,792 crore; its lending business is reportedly doubling year-on-year. Context for the month it lists into: October was UPI's first ₹30-lakh-crore month at ₹31.53 lakh crore across 25.47 billion transactions, and [Navi crossed the 5% market-share mark](https://inc42.com/buzz/navi-crosses-5-upi-market-share-as-phonepe-google-pay-slip-in-october), overtaking CRED in transaction value. The new MDR regime — 0.4% on eligible P2M transactions above ₹2,000 since 15 October — will now determine whether UPI dominance converts into listing-grade revenue, and who ends up paying for it.

## 5. Briefly noted

- **Trust (L7):** Comments on TRAI's draft AI guidelines for telecom close tomorrow, 27 November. IFF has [asked for a three-week extension](https://internetfreedom.in/four-weeks-is-not-enough-iff-writes-to-trai-on-telecom-ai-guidelines) to 18 December plus a counter-comment round — noting the draft obligates companies internally but gives users no notice of automated decisions, no way to contest them, and no counter-comment window at all.
- **Payments (L2):** Cashfree Payments launched a wallet on 24 November that lets personal AI agents pay bills on a user's behalf without exposing bank credentials ([report](https://telugurise.com/en/cashfree-agentic-ai-wallet-guide)) — an early consumer-facing step into agentic payments, arriving just as CERT-In warns the same agentic pattern is being weaponised by attackers.
- **Identity (L1):** UIDAI is re-pushing [free mandatory biometric updates for children aged 5–17 until 30 September 2027](https://kashmirvision.in/2026/11/25/uidai-urges-parents-to-complete-mandatory-aadhaar-biometric-updates-for-children) — missed age-5 and age-15 milestones are a leading cause of failed Aadhaar authentication later in exams and scholarships.

The through-line today is monetisation meeting maturity: the AA network got a price, market KYC got a single source of truth, ONDC is bidding for the last mile, and the largest UPI app is converting network effects into an equity story. India's DPI build-out phase is ending; the value-extraction phase is beginning. Who pays and who profits is now the live consumer question.
