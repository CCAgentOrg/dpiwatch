# DPI Brief — October 27, 2026

**Coverage window:** 26 October 2026, 08:00–27 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

Five developments surfaced across identity, payments, commerce, grievance administration and telecom regulation. Most are documented in government releases; the UPI merchant-charge item is a reported statement by SBI’s chairman, not a new NPCI rule. The quieter layers are recorded below rather than padded with older announcements.

## 1. Aadhaar enrolment reaches low-coverage areas in Wokha (L1 — identity)

Nagaland’s Department of Information and Public Relations [reported on 26 October that UIDAI’s Guwahati regional office and Wokha district administration are running a mobile Aadhaar Vahan drive](https://ipr.nagaland.gov.in/uidai-regional-office-guwahati-launches-special-aadhaar-vahan-drive-in-wokha-district). The vehicle is intended to take enrolment services to remote communities and other areas with lower coverage; the notice says the initiative is expected to extend to additional low-saturation districts. Local reporting [dates the field launch to 22 October](https://www.morungexpress.com/aadhaar-vahan-drive-in-wokha-to-cover-left-out-population), so this is a report in the current window about an operation already under way—not a claim of a same-day national policy change. The state notice also reminds families about mandatory biometric updates for children at ages five and 15.

The useful follow-through will be public reporting on locations served, enrolments and updates completed, and whether residents can get corrections resolved—not simply the number of districts visited.

## 2. SBI says it is working with NPCI on merchant-charge safeguards (L2 — payments)

In a [Business Standard report from the BFSI Insight Summit](https://www.business-standard.com/specials/bs-events/bfsi-insight-summit-looking-at-if-we-can-have-yono-bharat-says-c-s-setty-126102601674_1.html), SBI chairman C. S. Setty said the bank is working with NPCI, merchants and acquiring banks on a model to prevent merchant discount rate (MDR) costs being passed to customers where that can technically be monitored. He also called for stronger grievance redress and monitoring when merchants do pass charges on.

This is a relevant consumer-protection signal, but it is not an NPCI circular, an agreed scheme or proof that a particular surcharge is permitted. The important test is whether a customer can identify and contest an extra charge at checkout, and whether the mechanism produces an enforceable remedy without weakening the UPI payment itself.

## 3. GeM spotlights its AABHAR craft storefront (L4 — commerce)

In a 26 October [Press Information Bureau release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetail.aspx?PRID=2328424&reg=6&lang=1), GeM says it is promoting “The AABHAR Collection”, a digital storefront for curated gifts and hampers. It features 294 product listings from 10 state and central handloom and handicraft emporiums. The release also says seven more government emporiums and organisations are at an advanced stage of seller onboarding. This gives public buyers a visible route to browse regional crafts, with further listings still in the pipeline.

A catalogue count and an onboarding pipeline are not sales or livelihood measures. The next useful figures would be completed orders and value, repeat procurement, payment times, and how many small producer groups—not only established emporiums—can list and fulfil orders.

## 4. WCD asks States to clear portal grievances by 30 October (L6 — governance)

At a 26 October review meeting, 109 State and Union Territory officials took part in the Women and Child Development Ministry’s [Special Campaign 6 review](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2328675&reg=3&lang=1). The Ministry asked States to resolve pending grievances on POSHAN Tracker, PMMVY and CPGRAMS by 30 October, alongside daily field-level grievance camps and uploads to mission portals.

The deadline makes this a concrete administrative push, but the release does not state how many complaints are pending, how old they are, or what counts as resolved. Publishing those totals before and after the deadline—and reopening cases where the complainant’s issue remains unresolved—would make portal activity auditable rather than a reporting exercise.

## 5. TRAI proposes authorisation rules for internet-distributed live TV (L7 — trust)

TRAI’s [26 October recommendations for application-based linear television distribution](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-10/Recommendations_26102026.pdf) would create a new authorisation category for services that aggregate scheduled TV channels over the internet, including FAST services. The proposed structure includes a ₹10,000 processing fee, a one-time ₹1 lakh entry fee and an initial 20-year term. It also asks the Information and Broadcasting Ministry to maintain a master app repository, with device makers and operating-system providers making only authorised services available through their devices and app stores. The application provider would be the primary consumer-complaint contact.

These are recommendations, not rules in force. The app-store gate could affect what viewers can install, so the final rules should explain authorisation criteria, review and appeal rights, and how consumers can distinguish an unauthorised service from a technical failure. TRAI says its proposal excludes broadcaster-owned platforms carrying only their own channels and social-media live streams.
