# DPI Brief — October 30, 2026

**Top DPI headlines for October 30, 2026** — the DPDP Act reaches the Supreme Court, Aadhaar biometrics get harvested at fake health camps, Census 2027 trainers coach enumerators on the Aadhaar question, PM Kisan's 24th instalment rides the DBT rails today, and the National Testing Agency leans on DigiLocker for exam identity.

## [L7 · Trust] Twenty-one news publishers drag the DPDP Act to the Supreme Court

India's data protection regime is now formally contested in court. A group of 21 news publishers — including outlets owned by Mukesh Ambani and Gautam Adani — has [challenged the DPDP Act](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/ambani-adani-outlets-among-indian-publishers-contesting-modis-privacy-law-2026-10-26/) at the Supreme Court, arguing it would "trample upon investigative work" and let the government identify journalists' sources. In its filing, the Digital News Publishers Association notes that unlike the EU, Singapore, or the UK, India's law has [no written carve-out for journalism](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-privacy-law-threatens-newsrooms-media-groups-tell-top-court-2026-10-27/), while the Data Protection Board can levy fines and the government can block information "in the interests of the general public." IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw has [responded that the government is open to suggestions](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/dpdp-act-govt-open-to-changes-to-address-press-freedom-concerns-says-ashwini-vaishnaw-159645.html) and that the Act does not impinge on press freedom. The Supreme Court hears the matter next on November 17. For a DPI stack whose trust layer was meant to codify privacy as a fundamental right, the constitutional fight over who watches the watchers is just beginning.

## [L1 · Identity] Fake health camps harvested Aadhaar biometrics to fuel an e-SIM racket

Delhi Police's IFSO unit has [busted a SIM-fraud network](https://www.indiatoday.in/cities/delhi/story/delhi-police-esim-racket-fake-health-camps-aadhaar-biometric-fraud-ptag-3027865-2026-10-29) that ran fake health camps in villages — offering subsidised medicines — to collect villagers' Aadhaar numbers and biometric details. Two men arrested in Mathura allegedly used this data to activate more than 3,000 SIM and e-SIM connections over the past year, buying e-SIMs at ₹800–1,200 apiece and reselling them at ~₹2,000, with settlements in USDT cryptocurrency. Police linked the SIMs to 20+ complaints on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal involving suspected fraud of ~₹2.64 crore; the trail began with a "digital arrest" victim coerced into transferring ₹1.64 crore via six RTGS transactions. The case is a textbook illustration of the weakest link in SIM-binding and biometric KYC: the assisted-enrolment touchpoint, where a person's biometrics are handed to a human intermediary.

## [L1 + L6 · Identity/Governance] Census 2027 trainers coach enumerators on the Aadhaar question

With door-to-door enumeration for Census 2027's second phase beginning December 1 in Uttar Pradesh and three other poll-bound states, [training sessions are already wrestling](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/census-trainers-flag-queries-on-aadhaar-caste-age-and-birthplace-of-parents-as-key-challenges/article71640896.ece) with the thorniest questionnaire items. Question 37 seeks the respondent's Aadhaar number; one field trainer was heard telling enumerators that the data "may act as a proof of citizenship" — directly contradicting UIDAI's consistent position that Aadhaar establishes identity, not citizenship. A senior Census official said the question is optional and no such instruction exists, calling it the trainer's "misconception." The 40-question population schedule includes 13 first-time queries: caste (typed free-text, no OBC column), parents' place of birth to village level, number of bank accounts, and digital literacy. When the world's largest identity registry meets the world's largest census, the consent scaffolding around "optional" becomes the whole story.

## [L5 · Sectoral] PM Kisan's 24th instalment disburses today — DBT at 9.34-crore scale

Today the Centre releases the [24th instalment of PM-KISAN](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/pm-kisan-24th-installment-update-date-october-30-before-diwali-key-details-10941644/), transferring ₹18,680 crore to 9.34 crore farmers ahead of Diwali — one of the largest single-day Aadhaar-driven DBT exercises anywhere. But payment is not automatic: [e-KYC is mandatory](https://eng.ruralvoice.in/latest-news/pm-kisan-24th-instalment-to-be-released-on-october-30-farmers-to-get-rs-2000-ahead-of-diwali.html), via Aadhaar OTP on the PM-KISAN portal, face authentication on the mobile app, or a Common Service Centre visit, with Aadhaar-bank seeding and land-record verification also required. The same rails that deliver at 9-crore scale are also where exclusion happens — incomplete e-KYC, name mismatches between Aadhaar and registration, and unverified land records are the documented reasons payments stall.

## [L3 · Documents] Exam season makes DigiLocker the identity plumbing for JEE and GATE

The National Testing Agency has [opened JEE Main 2027 Session 1 registration](https://www.educationtimes.com/news/main-2027-registration-begins-nta-advises-candidates-to-complete-aadhaar-re-kyc-via-digilocker/articleshow/135293930.cms) and is advising candidates to complete Aadhaar re-KYC through DigiLocker before applying — a six-step refresh covering name, mobile number and photograph. Meanwhile, GATE 2027 applicants hitting [DigiLocker verification failures](https://www.shiksha.com/engineering/articles/digilocker-verification-failed-for-gate-blogId-240945) during registration have become common enough that troubleshooting guides circulate within days. India's document layer is now load-bearing for the country's largest competitive examinations, which makes its uptime and error-handling an education-policy issue, not just an IT one.
