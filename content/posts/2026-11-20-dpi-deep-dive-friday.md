# DPI Deep Dive — Friday | November 20, 2026

**Focus Layer:** L5 — Sectoral Infrastructure: ABHA, AgriStack, eCourts
**Coverage Period:** November 13 – November 20, 2026

## Executive summary

The sectoral layer's week put its three wings at three different stages of institutional maturity, and the juxtaposition is the story. Health infrastructure spent the week measuring itself honestly: a peer-reviewed time-motion study published November 18 offered the first prospective multi-state evidence on ABDM's most visible citizen touchpoint — the Scan and Share OPD registration service — complete with candid caveats about self-selection that launch-day press releases never carry.[^3] Agricultural infrastructure spent the week becoming compulsory: Chhattisgarh's paddy procurement season opened November 15 on AgriStack rails, making a Farmer ID linked to land parcels a precondition for selling at MSP, with biometric verification, digital crop surveys and 100% QR-code tracking of milled rice from mill to fair-price shop.[^4] And judicial infrastructure spent the week being disciplined by its own timelines: the Delhi High Court ruled November 18 that courts must pronounce orders within a reasonable time after hearings, directing one trial court to decide a pending application within fifteen days after it had fixed orders for late January — nearly three months after the last hearing.[^7] Around these, the week also delivered the sectoral layer's sharpest trust-and-safety story: a documented fraud chain in Gujarat running from compromised Anganwadi-worker logins on the Poshan Tracker app through downloadable ABHA cards to maternity-benefit vishing calls that drained Aadhaar-linked bank accounts.[^1] The connective thread: each sector's digital spine is now deep enough that its failure modes are becoming concrete, measurable, and attributable to specific design choices — which is exactly what a public infrastructure owes its users.

## Key developments

### 1. One worker's password, 5.02 crore identities: the Poshan Tracker fraud chain

The week's most consequential sectoral-infrastructure story was a consumer-harm story wearing the ABHA layer's uniform. Reporting out of Ahmedabad district documented at least ten cyber-fraud cases against pregnant and lactating women, with losses from ₹4,000 to ₹90,000, following a consistent chain: fraudsters obtain an Anganwadi worker's credentials for the Poshan Tracker app — by phone-based social engineering in the Viramgam case, with previous MP precedent suggesting hacked logins and never-rotated default passwords — and then download beneficiaries' ABHA cards, which carry the beneficiary's mobile number. Armed with names, pregnancy status and the local worker's identity, callers pose as ASHA workers or district health officials, promise ₹12,000 in maternity assistance (Gujarat's Namo Shree scheme; the central PMMVY pays ₹5,000), and in cases where the victim's account is empty, escalate to demanding fresh deposits — one Dholka victim borrowed ₹7,000 to "add balance" before the OTP was taken and her Aadhaar-linked account drained.[^1] The department's own counter-message is the system's design truth in one sentence: maternity assistance is credited directly to the beneficiary's account from the state level, so no genuine process ever requires an OTP.[^1]

The scale anchor is official. Per a Rajya Sabha reply answered July 29, 2026, Poshan Tracker held 8.96 crore beneficiaries as of June 2026 — and 5.02 crore of them are seeded with ABHA.[^2] The app's own documentation makes ABHA effectively mandatory in the pregnancy workflow: the RCH profile for a pregnant woman will not open without Aadhaar verification and an ABHA, created or retrieved by the worker via Aadhaar OTP on the beneficiary's behalf.[^2] The analytical point is where the consent architecture sits versus where the harm occurs. ABDM's consent framework governs *health record* sharing between verified participants — but this chain operates *below* the consent layer, at the last mile of credential issuance: one field worker's password yields a call-list of thousands of women per district (Ahmedabad alone counts 25,000+ registered pregnant women across 3,629 Anganwadi centres).[^2] The DPDP timeline sharpens the point: the rules' consent-manager regime went live this very week — November 13, 2026, one year after the rules were notified — while substantive fiduciary duties on security, breach reporting and retention apply only from May 13, 2027.[^2] A welfare database of 5 crore-plus health-linked identities is operating through that gap right now, and no public breach disclosure has come from MoWCD, NHA or MeitY. The sectoral layer's identity rails are only as strong as the least-supported worker holding a login.[^2]

### 2. Scan and Share gets its first multi-state evidence — and the honesty is the finding

On November 18, BMC Health Services Research published the first prospective multi-site time-motion study of ABDM's Scan and Share service — the QR-code flow, running since 2022, that lets patients share demographic data from their phones to skip manual OPD registration entry. The study observed 1,565 patients across eight public secondary and tertiary facilities in four states (Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana) during January 2026, predominantly at peak OPD hours: 631 Scan-and-Share users, 317 facilitated users, 617 manual registrants. The headline: Scan-and-Share users waited a median 1.0 minute in the registration queue versus 2.0 minutes for manual users (means 1.2 vs 7.2 minutes; *p* < 0.001) — and the state-level contrast is the real finding, with UP's manual mean queue at roughly 23.5 minutes collapsing to 1.5 minutes on the digital pathway.[^3]

Two honest design choices make this worth more than a press release. First, the authors explicitly decline causal language: registration pathways were self-selected, so the numbers are association, not effect — digital adopters are plausibly younger, better-connected patients who would have queued less anyway. Second, the token-issuing interval (counter to token) showed identical medians of 1.0 minute across all three groups — statistically significant differences of "negligible clinical magnitude." Read together, the study quietly tells system designers where the bottleneck went: the QR code eliminated the data-entry queue, not the counter itself, so congestion management (staffing, counter throughput) remains unautomated.[^3] The service's own designers — the study acknowledges NHA's support — commissioning and publishing evidence that undercuts maximalist claims is the evaluation culture ABDM has sometimes lacked elsewhere. And the context for why one minute matters is financing: the National Health Accounts 2023-24, released November 15, show government health expenditure nearly tripling to ₹4.14 lakh crore and out-of-pocket spending falling from 64.2% to 42.7% of total health expenditure — with primary and public-facility care, the exact setting where OPD queues live, now the dominant share of government spending.[^6] Registration friction is the front door of that entire shift; measuring it honestly is how the door gets fixed. The study's own recommendation — prioritise high-volume facilities where queues concentrate — is the right consumer-oriented reading.[^3]

### 3. AgriStack becomes compulsory: Chhattisgarh's paddy season opens on a digital ID

On November 15, Chhattisgarh opened Kharif marketing year 2026-27 paddy procurement at MSP with a full digital-surveillance stack wrapped around the entitlement. Per the state's framework, registration on the AgriStack portal is compulsory: a farmer must hold a Farmer ID and link it to the specific land parcel (khasra) Farm ID, with biometric authentication, digital crop survey records and AI-based image validation used to verify cultivated acreage and eligibility. Downstream, electronic weighing and 100% QR-code scanning of rice bags track grain from mills to the Food Corporation of India, the Civil Supplies Corporation and fair-price shops — closing the diversion channel the state has historically struggled to police.[^4][^5]

The structural significance is a state making a DPI identity a *precondition* for a statutory entitlement — the same saturation-move seen in Aadhaar-PDS linkages, now applied to MSP procurement in India's largest paddy-surplus state. The consumer trade-off is symmetrical and worth stating precisely: the same Farmer-ID-to-khasra linkage that blocks fraudulent or duplicate procurement can exclude the tenant farmer, the oral leaseholder and the woman whose inheritance is recorded under a male relative's name — the classic exclusion audit question the AgroSpectrum feature posed this month when it asked ten pointed questions before "plugging in" to AgriStack, noting the Farmer ID count's steep arc (2.06 crore in February 2025; past 9.2 crore by March 2026) and the ministry's consent-brokered data-access claims via the Unified Farmer Service Interface.[^8] Whether Chhattisgarh's compulsory stack builds documented exception-handling for unlinked or dispute-lying land will determine if this is transparency or a filter. The QR extension is the quieter innovation: it carries DPI's tamper-evident logic all the way into the PDS last mile, making every rice bag traceable — the grain economy getting the same transaction-integrity treatment payments got.[^4][^5]

### 4. AgriStack goes to the fair — and to the camps

The same week, the Agriculture Ministry's India International Trade Fair pavilion in Delhi showcased AgriStack to the public as "digital public infrastructure for agriculture," complete with AR/VR demos of the digital crop survey, alongside Natural Farming, Per Drop More Crop, PMFBY and e-NAM linkages for farmer producer organisations — including Pampore saffron and Lakadong turmeric FPOs.[^6] Meanwhile, district administrations in Kashmir continued AgriStack saturation drives, with farmer-ID registration camps in Kupwara and Baramulla's tehsils urging farmers to get IDs that "link farmers with land and agricultural records" for Kisan loans, land records and welfare schemes.[^9] The pairing captures the layer's current growth mode: pavilion marketing on top, tehsil-level compulsory saturation underneath.

The numbers underneath the marketing show a stack still in build-out: Farmer IDs grew from roughly 2 crore to over 9 crore in thirteen months, but the e-KYC completion drives running in parallel — one advisory this month reported 1.41 crore of 1.71 crore registered account-holders having completed e-KYC through CSCs — reveal the gap between *enrolled* and *usable*.[^8] A Farmer ID that fails e-KYC does not deliver a Kisan credit card or an MSP payment; it delivers a queue at a camp. The consumer analyst's metric for this layer over the next year is not the enrollment counter but the denial rate at the point of benefit — how many farmers present at a procurement centre, a lending camp or a scheme window and are turned away for identity reasons. DPI saturation campaigns everywhere share this blind spot, and AgriStack is now large enough that its exclusion events are becoming statistically visible even if they are not yet published.

### 5. Delhi High Court puts a clock on pending orders — case-flow management meets the digital file

The eCourts wing's most interesting development this week was not a platform launch but a judicial administration ruling with direct digital-infrastructure implications. On November 18, the Delhi High Court held that courts must pronounce orders within a reasonable time after hearing arguments, warning that criminal matters left undecided for long periods erode public confidence in the justice delivery system. It directed a trial court to decide a pending application — unheard-of-delay material that had been argued three times, with the last hearing on October 31 and the trial court having fixed January 28, 2027 for passing orders — within fifteen days. The State did not oppose.[^7]

The ruling sits at the intersection of two forces that define eCourts Phase III. The first is statutory: the BNSS era wrote binding timelines into criminal procedure, and courts are increasingly enforcing the legislature's clocks on themselves — the fifteen-day direction reads as the higher judiciary importing statutory deadline discipline into the orders-pending space. The second is infrastructural: digitised case files, electronic cause lists and dashboard-tracked case ages are what make "orders reserved and forgotten" visible, attributable and correctable at scale. The national digital backlog the project has assembled — over 1.25 crore cases e-filed as of the project's last major milestone disclosure — turns judicial delay from an anecdote into a time-series, and time-series invite exactly this kind of deadline enforcement.[^10] Phase III's stated ambition of digitising legacy records and live court data is quietly changing who can see judicial delay: not just the litigant refreshing a case-status page, but the High Court's registry running exception reports. A justice system that timestamps every event can be audited by the day — and this week shows the auditing is starting from within.

## Cross-layer watch

- **L1 — Identity:** both fraud and fungibility run through the identity substrate — the Poshan-ABHA chain ends at Aadhaar-linked bank accounts, and Chhattisgarh's procurement stack leans on biometric authentication anchored to Aadhaar-seeded Farmer IDs.[^1][^4]
- **L7 — Trust & Privacy:** the DPDP consent-manager regime going live November 13, 2026 — the same week a credential chain demonstrated identity disclosure *below* the consent layer — is the sectoral layer's trust gap made concrete; substantive fiduciary duties only begin May 13, 2027.[^2]
- **L4 — Commerce:** AgriStack's IITF showcase tied farmer registries to e-NAM FPO linkages, while Chhattisgarh's QR-tracked grain chain effectively makes MSP procurement a logistics problem — procurement digital rails converging on commerce rails.[^6][^4]
- **L6 — Governance:** Delhi HC's order-deadline direction is judicial self-audit; the health department's advisory-only response to a documented welfare-data fraud chain is the grievance layer's slower reflex in the health sector.[^7][^1]
- **L2 — Payments:** NHA 2023-24's ₹4.14 lakh crore government health spend flows largely through DBT and insurance rails — ABPM-JAY and maternity-benefit transfers — which is precisely where the OTP-fraud chain struck; payment integrity and identity hygiene are the same problem.[^6][^1]
