# DPI Brief — November 22, 2026

The past day kept two DPI theatres busy: the payments world waiting out the UPI MDR clock, and the Supreme Court holding the DPDP Act's first big constitutional test at arm's length for two more weeks. Between them, exam season is quietly stress-testing the Aadhaar–DigiLocker stack, and the commerce network is signing up tribal products and kiranas. Here is what matters.

## 1. UPI MDR for merchant-to-merchant payments likely a flat ₹5 — still no start date

The clearest signal yet on how India's first UPI merchant fee will be shaped: [Business Standard reported on 19 November](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-mdr-m2m-flat-fee/) that NPCI's UPI and Services Steering Committee has settled on a flat ₹5 per transaction for merchant-to-merchant (M2M) payments — wholesalers paying retailers, B2B marketplaces and B2B collections. That is far gentler than the fear traders had after [NPCI announced a 0.4% MDR on UPI transactions above ₹2,000](https://bankofmaharashtra.bank.in/blogs/what-is-upi-mdr) in September: with average M2M tickets at ₹20,000–₹40,000, a percentage levy would have meant ₹80–₹160 per payment. The flattening follows distributor pressure — the AICPDF told the finance minister that roughly 65% of consumer payments reaching retailers, and 40–45% of retailer payments to distributors against credit bills, already run on UPI.

The working framework, as [Elets BFSI summarises](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-mdr-m2m-flat-fee/): person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000 at 0.4% capped at ₹300; P2P transfers untouched; railway, telecom, insurance and fuel merchant payments above ₹2,000 at a flat ₹5; M2M now reportedly joining them at ₹5. But the date keeps slipping. The levy was due 15 October, was deferred past Diwali, and reports suggest it could now stay parked until after the five state assembly elections in early 2027, after NPCI pushed the rollout out of the festive season. Payment firms want the opposite — the Payments Council of India has pressed for early rollout, and the industry notes government UPI/RuPay incentives have gone unpaid for six quarters despite ₹2,000 crore budgeted in FY27. [NPCI's UPI product page](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi/product-overview) still carries no commencement notification.

## 2. Vyapaar Saksham: the acceptance build-out waiting on that same date

NPCI notified the [UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-2026) on 11 November — an acceptance-infrastructure push for small merchants targeting 5–10 lakh new soundbox installations a year and 30–50% annual transaction growth among existing small merchants. The catch: it is funded from MDR collections and runs three years from the date MDR takes effect, so the scheme's clock — like everything else in UPI economics right now — is stopped until the government names a date.

## 3. Supreme Court defers DPDP challenges to December 9, no interim stay

On 17 November the Supreme Court adjourned hearings on petitions challenging the DPDP Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 to [December 9](https://www.varindia.com/news/supreme-court-defers-dpdp-hearing-to-december-9), declining to stay the contested provisions in the meantime. Two threads dominate. First, the amendment to Section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act, which transparency activists including Anjali Bhardwaj argue will gut access to officials' personal details needed for scrutiny — from [electoral roll revisions to administrative irregularities](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/sc-adjourns-hearing-on-dpdp-act-rti-amendments-to-december-9-160384.html). Second, the absence of a journalistic exemption: the Digital News Publishers Association, representing 21 publishers including Network18, NDTV, The Indian Express and Times Internet, argues consent and erasure duties amount to [a pre-censorship regime for reporting](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/privacy-vs-press-freedom-where-should-the-line-be-drawn/article71704591.ece). Nothing was struck down and nothing was paused — the May 2027 enterprise compliance timeline stands, so December 9 is the date to watch.

## 4. Exam season puts Aadhaar and DigiLocker in the front line

With JEE Main 2027 registration in its final stretch, the National Testing Agency [advised candidates](https://www.jagranjosh.com/news/appearing-for-an-nta-exam-update-your-aadhaar-biometrics-free-until-age-17-for-stressfree-entry-189093) whose Aadhaar was issued before age 15 to refresh biometrics — free until age 17 — to avoid verification failures at exam centres. The NTA has also pushed [Re-KYC through DigiLocker](https://engineering.careers360.com/articles/how-do-re-kyc-digilocker-for-jee-main-2027-registration) for the application process, even as candidates report registration glitches, and [fees are due by November 22](https://telanganatoday.com/jee-main-2027-nta-urges-applicants-to-pay-fees-before-november-22). UIDAI, meanwhile, is running free [email updates via the Aadhaar app](https://uidai.gov.in) until December 31, 2026. Exam season has become the de facto national audit of identity-stack hygiene — every mismatch between a school record, an Aadhaar field and a DigiLocker document becomes a student's problem at a payment deadline.

## 5. ONDC: tribal commerce talks and kirana digitisation scale-up

The [TRIFED managing director met ONDC's COO](https://www.facebook.com/tribesindia/posts/expandingdigitalmarketaccess-trifed-explores-furthering-e-commerce-opportunities/1460470149594477) on November 18 in New Delhi to widen the online market for tribal products on the open network. That follows DPIIT-backed reporting that [ONDC's DigiDukaan has connected over 21,000 kirana retailers](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/digidukaan-connects-over-21000-retailers-with-more-than-450-brands-across-three-cities-piyush-goyal-chairs-chintan-shivir-on-trade-digitisation) with more than 450 brands across Hyderabad, Jaipur and Mumbai as of September — up from about 12,000 the previous quarter — with the network past 500 million cumulative transactions.
