# DPI Brief — October 11, 2026

**Coverage window:** 10 October 2026, 08:00–11 October 2026, 08:00 IST.

The strongest signals this cycle are in agriculture: a year-one dashboard snapshot for 100 targeted districts and a new international discussion on governing AI for food systems. DigiLocker’s deadline for feedback on a proposed partner model also fell on 10 October. None of these announcements is evidence that a new national platform went live; they are an official progress snapshot, a standards-and-governance discussion and a procurement consultation milestone.

## 1. PMDDKY publishes its first-year dashboard snapshot

A [PIB backgrounder on the Prime Minister Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (PMDDKY)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2321731), posted on 10 October, says the programme covers 100 Aspirational Agricultural Districts and brings together 36 Central schemes across 11 ministries and departments, alongside state schemes and private participation. District plans are tracked through 121 indicators—74 output and 47 outcome measures—with monthly rankings shown on the [PM-DDKY dashboard](https://pmddky.niti.gov.in/).

The government reports that all 100 districts had prepared District Action Plans and submitted output data through August 2026; it also says they submitted FY 2025–26 outcome data. The average score across six objectives rose from 18.85 in April to 46.89 in August 2026. These are programme-reported measures, not an independent evaluation. In particular, the headline score is a composite tracking measure: it cannot, by itself, show that digital monitoring caused improvements in farmer income, productivity or access to credit. The underlying figures also pre-date this week’s publication. The useful DPI angle is the dashboard’s role in making cross-scheme delivery more visible—and the need to publish indicator definitions and district-level evidence alongside rankings.

## 2. AI-for-agriculture initiative turns to governance and standards

At India Mobile Congress on 10 October, the Department of Telecommunications’ Telecommunication Engineering Centre and the Indian Council of Agricultural Research hosted the second meeting of the UN-coordinated [Global Initiative on AI for Agri-Food Systems](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2321784). PIB reports about 70 in-person delegates and 25 online participants. The meeting discussed a proposed governance and coordination structure, working-group leadership and terms of reference, and priority workstreams.

The agenda spans AI, connected sensors, weather stations, drones and satellite data for tasks such as crop-disease detection, resource use and traceability. These were described as potential applications, not as a newly deployed nationwide farmer service. The initiative says it wants “trustworthy and interoperable” farmer-centred AI. That ambition will need practical answers on data provenance, farmer consent, redress when an automated recommendation causes harm, and who is accountable across telecom, agriculture and technology providers. For DPI watchers, the important development is the attempt to put governance and standards around agricultural AI before describing it as a public-service layer.

## 3. DigiLocker partner feedback deadline passes

A [NeGD public notice](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/09/e03f3d4baff2d57d1ffeb96d31f83c6a.pdf) set 10 October as the deadline for comments on a draft Request for Empanelment of Specialized Ecosystem Enablement Partners for DigiLocker. The proposed partners would identify use cases, bring institutions to NeGD, integrate them as document requesters and encourage ongoing use, initially in finance, education and health. The draft says each requester and use case needs NeGD’s prior approval and that access should use DigiLocker’s consent-based APIs.

The safeguards in the draft matter as much as the growth target: partners are told not to retain, cache, profile or reuse document contents; requester integrations require a security audit by a CERT-In-empanelled auditor before production; and development, test and support infrastructure must be hosted in India. The notice invites comments, not applications, and says NeGD may revise the draft; the RFE’s application dates remain “TBD.” The deadline’s passage therefore does **not** mean vendors have been selected or that the partner scheme is operational. The next meaningful update is publication of the final RFE and any changes made after consultation.

## 4. IMC showcase puts several DPI components on display

MeitY’s [IMC 2026 pavilion announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2321282&lang=1&reg=1) said the congress ran through 10 October and featured Aadhaar, DigiLocker, UMANG, Entity Locker and API Setu, alongside a DPDP framework display. This is a showcase, not a launch or proof of new safeguards. Still, placing identity, documents, API exchange and data-protection messaging together makes the policy ambition visible: the platforms are presented as connected service infrastructure, while the practical test remains whether consent, security and accountability work in each real transaction.
