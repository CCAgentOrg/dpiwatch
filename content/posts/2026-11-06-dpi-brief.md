# DPI Brief — November 06, 2026

**Top DPI headlines for November 6, 2026** — UIDAI's offline-verification ecosystem crosses 300 entities as the Aadhaar App nears 7.5 crore downloads, UPI lands at Dubai Duty Free with a 10% incentive, MeitY's AI-training data handbook draws fire for calling individual consent impractical, and industry maps who pays when an AI agent causes harm.

## [L1 · Identity] UIDAI's offline verification ecosystem hits 300 entities; Aadhaar App nears 7.5 crore downloads

UIDAI has onboarded 300 Offline Verification Seeking Entities (OVSEs) since the system rolled out in January, with sectors from hospitality to government departments now accepting app-based Aadhaar credentials instead of routinely collecting photocopies ([DD News](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/11/uidai-onboards-300-entities-for-aadhaar-offline-verification-app-downloads-near-7-5-crore), [NewsOnAir](https://newsonair.gov.in/uidai-onboards-300-entities-for-aadhaar-offline-verification), [PTI via Rediff](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/uidai-onboards-300-entities-for-aadhaar-offline-verification/57604620261105)). The verifier-side expansion rides on consumer-side adoption: the Aadhaar App is approaching 7.5 crore downloads, over 86 lakh residents have updated their registered mobile numbers without visiting an enrolment centre, alongside 22.16 lakh address updates and 40.25 lakh email updates ([UIDAI on X](https://x.com/UIDAI/status/2118325205693247975)). The architecture is consent-based and offline — verification does not require the verifying organisation to connect to UIDAI's central database, and the resident controls which fields a hotel front desk or employer sees. April's first-100 milestone doubling to 300 in seven months is exactly the verifier-side adoption the credential architecture needs, because a signed QR code is only as useful as the officer willing to scan it. The watch item is acceptance breadth beyond government counters: banks, landlords and HR onboarding remain photocopy territory, and the OVSE count is the metric to track. April reported the first 100 entities; 200 more in six months is steady, not yet ubiquitous.

## [L2 · Payments] UPI lands at Dubai Duty Free — acceptance abroad now arrives with a discount

NPCI International (NIPL) announced on November 5 that UPI is now accepted at Dubai Duty Free outlets at Dubai International and Al Maktoum airports, with a 10% discount on eligible purchases capped at AED 100, and Network International as the acquiring partner ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/personal-finance/upi-payments-dubai-duty-free-what-indian-travellers-need-to-know-20027855.htm), [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/dubai-duty-free-to-offer-discounts-on-upi-payments/articleshow/135564295.cms), [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/shopping-in-dubai-just-got-cheaper-for-indian-travellers-if-they-pay-by-upi-heres-how-565880-2026-11-05)). Travellers must activate UPI International or UPI Global in their UPI app and link an eligible Indian bank account. Read alongside this week's NIPL moves for outbound tourists — Burj Khalifa bookings and Singapore attractions — the strategy is explicit: buy acceptance at high-traffic retail with incentives, then let habit form. The consumer math deserves scrutiny: a network-funded discount is a trial subsidy, and the real test is what happens when it expires, since cross-border UPI pricing (FX markup layered on the rail) is where the fine print lives. The number to watch remains non-P2P international volume, which NIPL has so far not published separately.

## [L5 × L7 · Sectoral meets Trust] MeitY's DEPA-for-AI handbook: when the state decides consent is impractical

The DEPA for AI Training handbook MeitY launched at an October 30 roundtable is getting its first sustained scrutiny this week, and its core concession is striking: at population scale — hospital records, bank transactions, education data — individual consent is "impractical," so the framework leans on sharing through confidential clean rooms, differential privacy and machine-readable contracts, with pilots planned in finance and healthcare ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/governance/govt-handbook-for-sharing-hospital-bank-data-to-train-ai-models-says-individual-consent-impractical/3089636/), [ETDatacenters](http://datacenters.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/policy-land-power/india-proposes-depa-framework-to-unlock-data-for-ai-training/135384759), [TechGig](https://techgig.com/news/governance-policy/meity-proposes-framework-to-unlock-data-for-ai-model-training/135415575)). MeitY pitches the route as unlocking $500 billion in AI-driven value for GDP by 2030. The consumer-rights problems are concrete: ThePrint's read of the draft notes it proposes training adaptive learning systems on children's learner performance data and building "skills passports" on the Academic Bank of Credits — without addressing how the DPDP Act's verifiable-parental-consent and no-behavioural-tracking rules for children would apply — and it does not state whether the framework relies on the DPDP research exemption that takes effect in May 2027. DEPA's original design promise was consent architecture for financial data; repurposing it for AI training while declaring consent impractical inverts the founding principle. If the state's own handbook treats consent as a bottleneck rather than a right, that precedent will be inherited by every sectoral data stack — health, agriculture, education. The draft should face public comment before "impractical" hardens into default.

## Elsewhere in the stack

- **[L7]** DSCI's agentic-AI liability guide argues that liability for harm caused by autonomous AI agents should be traced across the value chain by the decisions each participant makes and the controls it can enforce, rather than pinned solely on the deploying enterprise or the model provider ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/data-security-council-unveils-ai-liability-guide-for-indian-enterprises-126103001647_1.html), [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/accountability-tames-ai-autonomy-as-dsci-outlines-its-liability-framework/articleshow/135430535.cms)). It lands between CERT-In's November 4 AI security auditing guidelines and TRAI's telecom AI draft (comments due November 27) — industry is now drafting the liability layer that regulators will either adopt or override.
